# EPISODE 6 — "Twenty-Two Names"

SFX: A labour contractor's yard. A tarpaulin cracking in wind. A goat. A man eating, and going on eating while he speaks.

BANSI: I do not keep a list.

RUPA: You keep a list.

BANSI: I keep a list of the advance. That is money, so that I keep.

RUPA: Then read me the advance list for the twelfth.

BANSI: Madam, from which office are you?

RUPA: Barhagarh Sahayak Thana. First floor, above the bus stand. Read me the advance list for the twelfth.

BANSI: I will come to the office on Monday.

RUPA: The twelfth was three weeks ago. Monday is four days. In four days you will go to Patna and the register will have been kept somewhere and the somewhere will have had a leak in the roof.

SFX: Chewing stopping.

BANSI: You are very quick for a new one.

RUPA: I am not quick. I am here at eight in the morning, which is different.

SFX: Nothing. Long. Then a tin trunk. Then a school notebook, its cover peeling.

BANSI: Twenty-two advances. Two hundred each. Four thousand four hundred rupees. Names, and against each name a village. That is all it is.

RUPA: Read them.

BANSI: I will give you the book.

RUPA: Read them out loud, and I will write them, and then we will both sign at the bottom that twenty-two names were read on this date. Then you can have the book back.

BANSI: Why?

RUPA: Because a book can be lost and a signature cannot.

NARRATOR: There was no rule anywhere that required that. She had read the sentence in a guide book for the constable examination, in a chapter on the preservation of evidence, in a room with a forty-watt bulb, eleven months before anybody had ever called her madam.

SFX: A pen. Names being read, flat and fast, one after another. The tarpaulin.

SFX: The first-floor room. The fan with the bad bearing. Paper.

PUSHPA: You went to Bansi's yard alone.

RUPA: Yes.

PUSHPA: You went without telling anybody, in the shirt, at eight in the morning, and now his brother has telephoned Sahab twice, and Sahab was very polite on the telephone both times, that's all.

RUPA: Twenty-two names. Nineteen from Kachhwara, two from Sonbarsa, one from Chandpura.

PUSHPA: And?

RUPA: And the kilns take labour on a contract of forty days. Forty days from the twelfth is the twenty-second of next month. So they are all still there. Twenty-one people are standing in one place right now who saw where the twenty-second one went.

SFX: A chair pushed back.

PUSHPA: Sit down.

RUPA: The bus to Jhajha is at six-forty. There are four kilns on that road and the second one is the one Bansi has taken people to for six seasons because his wife's family is from there. I asked at the tea shop, not at the yard.

PUSHPA: You asked at the tea shop.

RUPA: Everybody in this district thinks the information is in the office. It is never in the office. It is in the tea shop and the bus stand and the ration line, and I have been standing in all three of those my whole life because I have never been in an office.

SFX: Nothing. Long. Then a drawer, opened. Then a purse.

PUSHPA: Six-forty is a hundred and ten rupees return. Take it from the paper-cost drawer and write it in as travel, and if he asks, it was my order and not your idea, because he does not like ideas that are not his, that's all.

SFX: A bus at dawn. A conductor's whistle. Then, distant, the sound of a brick kiln — the chimney draught, a low continuous roar, and under it the flat clap of moulds.

NARRATOR: It took her six hours. It did not take detection. It took a girl in a khaki shirt walking up to twenty-one people one at a time and asking each of them the same question, which is a thing that costs nothing and that nobody had done.

SFX: Kiln noise. A boy's voice, young, hoarse.

PHULCHAND: I got down at Jhajha.

RUPA: Why?

PHULCHAND: Because he said if I came back with them he would take the advance out of my mother's next season also. And I had broken the axle of the cart.

RUPA: An axle is eleven hundred rupees.

PHULCHAND: He said four thousand.

RUPA: He said four thousand and you believed him, and you have been sitting in a kiln for three weeks working off four thousand rupees for an axle that costs eleven hundred, and your mother has been to the yellow building four times.

SFX: The boy crying. Kiln noise going on over it, indifferent.

RUPA: Stop. Stop that now, there is a bus at four and we are getting it. Do you have shoes?

PHULCHAND: No.

RUPA: Then walk behind me and do not look at anyone.

SFX: The bus stand at Barhagarh. Evening. Horns. A crowd noise that changes when something is happening in it.

SARITA: Phulchand. Phulchand. Phulchand—

SFX: A woman on her knees on concrete. The crowd closing in and then, unusually, going quiet.

SARITA: (to the crowd, loud, cracked) Four times I went to the yellow building! Four times! This one — this one, in three weeks, this one went to the kiln herself—

RUPA: Sit her down, somebody. Somebody get water.

SARITA: Fourteen years and two months. That is what I said to her on her first day and she wrote it down and she said read it back and I said I cannot read, and she read it to me. Nobody has ever read anything back to me.

NARRATOR: Remember the box.

SFX: A verandah at Chandpura. A cycle bell, twice, at the gate. A tin box set down — and this time, opened before it is set down.

MUNNI: It is the same box.

HARIYA: It is the same box.

MUNNI: But the string is different. Look at the string. That is their string. That means they tied it.

MESSENGER: The boy's father says there was never any anger in it. He says with the government matter now settled, and with what happened at the bus stand, which the whole road is talking about—

MUNNI: Say the rest of it.

MESSENGER: He says the date can be looked at again after the harvest.

MUNNI: Take the box back.

MESSENGER: Beta—

MUNNI: Take it back and tell him my sister found a boy in three weeks that the station could not find in four months, and that I am sitting the examination myself next year, and that after the harvest I will be busy.

SFX: A cycle going. Faster than it came.

SFX: A cloth envelope. Notes counted onto a wooden table, in fives.

RUPA: Eighteen thousand. Count it.

HARIYA: I do not have to count it.

RUPA: Count it in front of me, because from now on everything in this house gets counted in front of somebody.

SFX: Notes. Slow. All the way to the end.

HARIYA: Eighteen.

RUPA: Four thousand five hundred to Lakhan on the fifth. He gets it at his gate, and he gets off the scooter for it, and he writes the receipt in front of you and you bring the receipt home. Two thousand for the house. Two hundred for Munni's form. The rest stays in the box and nobody borrows against anything ever again as long as I am earning. Say it back.

HARIYA: Nobody borrows against anything.

RUPA: The house paper stays where it is. In eleven months it comes back out.

SFX: An old man's breath, unsteady.

HARIYA: It was only that I thought—

RUPA: Not tonight, Baba.

SFX: Morning. The bus stand ticket window. A queue. Coins on a metal counter.

RUPA: One, Kachhwara.

TAPAN: Madam ji.

SFX: Boots coming together. A hand snapping up to a cap. Loud enough that the queue hears it.

RUPA: I— you have made a mistake.

TAPAN: No mistake, madam ji. Everybody at our end knows what happened with the Kachhwara boy. Everybody. Sir was told about it on Saturday and Sir did not say one word for a full minute, which madam ji, from Sir, is a great deal.

RUPA: Which end? Where are you posted?

TAPAN: Barhagarh station, madam ji. The yellow building. Five hundred metres from your stairs.
