# EPISODE 2 — "Returned Sweets"

SFX: A cycle bell, twice, at a gate. A tin box set down on a verandah floor. Hard.

MUNNI: That is my box.

SFX: The lid, lifted. Sweets, dry, shifting.

MUNNI: Those are the sweets we sent. On Thursday. That is the same box, the same paper, the string is still—

HARIYA: Beta, go inside.

MUNNI: The string is still tied the way I tied it.

SFX: A man clearing his throat. Not stepping up onto the verandah.

MESSENGER: The boy's father says there is no anger in it. He says these things happen. He says the horoscope was always difficult and now with the — with the government matter also uncertain, better now than after.

HARIYA: It is not uncertain. It was only a list, and lists are corrected, there is an appeal, there is a — there is a procedure, I have seen men in the block office who—

MESSENGER: He says there is no anger in it.

SFX: The cycle bell. Wheels on gravel, going.

NARRATOR: In Chandpura a marriage is not broken by a letter. It is broken by the sweets coming back with the string still tied, so that everyone on the road can see that nobody in that house even opened them.

SFX: A brass tumbler set down. Wind in a dry tree.

RUPA: How many people watched him come?

HARIYA: Nobody. It was early.

RUPA: How many.

HARIYA: The tea shop was open.

RUPA: Then everyone.

MUNNI: Didi, they can't do that. We sent the sweets. Sending sweets means it is fixed. Everybody knows sending sweets means it is fixed.

RUPA: Everybody knows I failed. So everybody knows the salary is not coming. So everybody knows what he was actually promised, and it was not me.

MUNNI: Say that again but slower, because it sounded like you are agreeing with them.

RUPA: I am counting, not agreeing. Eighteen thousand a month is what a constable gets in hand in the first year. Twelve months is two lakh sixteen. That is what they were marrying. It is gone, so they went.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

MUNNI: You should still eat.

NARRATOR: Remember the box. It will come back into this story with different string.

SFX: Evening. Crickets. A bicycle chain being oiled, badly.

RUPA: Baba. The ninety thousand.

HARIYA: The co-operative gives against the standing crop, everyone takes it, your uncle also took—

RUPA: I went to the co-operative today. On the four o'clock bus. I asked the clerk to show me the register for March.

HARIYA: You should not go to offices asking—

RUPA: There is no loan against this house at the co-operative. There is no loan against this house anywhere in the co-operative's book. So where did the ninety thousand come from?

SFX: The chain stops. A rag being folded and unfolded.

HARIYA: It was only that the co-operative wanted the papers to go to the block, and the block takes six weeks, and the man said the seats would be filled by then, he said eleven seats and forty men already sitting with money, he said if we came in April there would be nothing left, and I thought — I thought one year, one year of your salary and it is closed, and nobody would ever—

RUPA: Baba. Who has the house paper.

HARIYA: Lakhan.

SFX: A tumbler knocked over. Water running along a floor and off the edge of a verandah.

RUPA: Lakhan who makes the kattas. Lakhan who took Sukhdev's threshing floor.

HARIYA: He gives a proper receipt. He wrote it in front of me. He is not a bad man, he only—

RUPA: What does the receipt say.

HARIYA: Ninety thousand. Against the house and the front land.

RUPA: And?

HARIYA: Five per cent. Monthly.

RUPA: Four thousand five hundred rupees. Every month. Baba, that is more than you earn at the godown.

HARIYA: The salary was going to—

RUPA: The salary is not coming. When is the first one due?

SFX: Paper being taken out of a shirt pocket. Unfolded. Very slowly.

HARIYA: It says the fifth. Of every month.

RUPA: Today is the second.
