# EPISODE 12 — "The Pile on the Left"

SFX: Feet coming up the iron stairs too fast. Not one person's rhythm — a woman running in a sari, and the eleventh step taken wrong.

SARITA: Where is she. Where is she.

PUSHPA: Madam is at the — you cannot go in, there is a bench, there are thirty people who have been sitting since—

SARITA: Where is she!

SFX: A spring hinge slammed back against a wall.

RUPA: Sarita. Sit down. What has happened.

SARITA: They have taken Phulchand.

RUPA: Who has.

SARITA: The police. The real ones. The yellow building. Two of them came in the morning and they took him off the road outside the school with his slate still in his hand.

RUPA: On what.

SARITA: Theft. Four thousand rupees. Bansi's brother has given a complaint that my son broke a cart axle and ran away from the work and stole four thousand rupees, and that he has witnesses, and there is a number on the paper, and the paper is signed by a sir.

SFX: Nothing. Long. The bench outside — thirty people, absolutely silent.

RUPA: The axle is eleven hundred rupees.

SARITA: They have taken him, madam.

RUPA: The axle is eleven hundred rupees and I have the boy's statement and I have twenty-two names read out and countersigned by Bansi himself in his own yard, and the advance book, page by page. Where is the file. Pushpa. Where is the Kachhwara file.

PUSHPA: With Sahab. Everything for forwarding goes to Sahab, that has always been—

RUPA: Get him.

SFX: A jeep below. Not the two-short-one-long. A different horn.

SFX: The spring hinge. Unhurried steps. A plastic file set on a table.

NAGESH: Sit, sit, all of you. Sarita ji, has anybody given you water? Bishnu—

RUPA: The Kachhwara file. The forwarding.

NAGESH: Beti—

RUPA: Do not say beti in front of her. The forwarding.

SFX: The fan. The bad bearing.

NAGESH: There was no forwarding.

SFX: Nothing.

RUPA: What?

NAGESH: There was no forwarding of the Kachhwara matter, and there has never been a forwarding of anything, from this office, to anybody, in eight months. I told you on your fifth week that forwarding is my work. It is. My work with that file was to take it, in an envelope, to Bansi's brother's house in Sonbarsa, and to explain to him, at length and with great courtesy, what twenty-two names read out and countersigned would do to his brother, and what it would cost for that to stay in a drawer above a bus stand.

SARITA: (small) What is he saying.

NAGESH: Nineteen thousand, Sarita ji. That is what it cost him. And I will tell you what he bought with it, because you will find out anyway and it is better from me: he bought seven weeks, and in seven weeks he did the sensible thing, which is what any man does when he knows a paper exists that can hang him. He went first. He went to the yellow building and put in a complaint against your son so that when the twenty-two names finally surface, your boy is a thief with a case against him and the twenty-two names are a mother's revenge.

SFX: A chair going over.

RUPA: You sold it.

NAGESH: I converted it. Beti, understand the arithmetic. That file was worth nineteen thousand in an envelope and it was worth nothing at all in the yellow building, because in the yellow building it goes into the pile on the left, and you have been told what the pile on the left is by me and now also by the sir himself, who came up my staircase and said it in front of thirty-one people.

RUPA: I brought that boy back on a bus.

NAGESH: You did. And that is why the bench is thirty and not fourteen, and that is why Dularchand counted fifty-five thousand onto your table, and that is why this office is worth what it is now worth. Nothing here is wasted. Not even the good things.

SFX: A woman making a sound that is not a word.

SARITA: Madam. Madam, look at me.

RUPA: Sarita—

SARITA: You wrote it down. You read it back to me. Nobody had ever read anything back to me, and I told the whole of Kachhwara that, I told it at the well, I told it at the fair, I have been telling it for four months, madam — that there is a girl above the bus stand who reads it back.

RUPA: I know.

SARITA: I sent eleven people up these stairs. Eleven. Kalua's daughter, and the Ranchi widow, and Sukhdev's brother, and every one of them I told: do not go to the yellow building, the yellow building has a pile on the left, go up the iron stairs, they will read it back to you.

SFX: The bench. Thirty people. Feet shifting on iron.

SARITA: So when they took my son off the road with his slate in his hand, I did what you taught me. I took my file. I took my case number. I went to the court in the district and I stood in the line from six in the morning, and when it came to my turn I put the file on the counter and I said, this is my case, it is number one-one-four of this office, and it is stamped on every page.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

RUPA: What did the clerk say.

SARITA: Say it in front of them. Say it in front of the bench. They are all sitting out there with their numbers in their hands, same as I was.

RUPA: Sarita, please—

SARITA: SAY WHAT THE CLERK SAID.

SFX: The fan. Thirty people who have stopped pretending not to listen.

RUPA: What did he say.

SARITA: He turned the file over twice. He was not unkind. He said, mother, there is no Barhagarh Sahayak Thana. He said there is no such office on the court's list and there is no such office on the police list and there is no such office anywhere in this state, and there never has been. He said the number one-one-four is a number that somebody wrote on a page. And then he said the thing I have come here to say to you.

RUPA: Say it.

SARITA: He said: whose handwriting is this?

SFX: Nothing.

SARITA: Every page, madam. Every page of my son. The statement, the read-back, the twenty-two names, the thumbprint line, the witness column — it is one hand from the first page to the last, and the clerk held it up to me and asked me whose hand it was, and I did not know how to answer him, because I cannot read, so I have brought it back to you to tell me.
