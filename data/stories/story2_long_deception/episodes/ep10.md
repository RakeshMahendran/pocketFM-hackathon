# EPISODE 10 — "The Receivings"

SFX: A pen being capped. One dry sound in a quiet room.

NARRATOR: She did not sign that night. She signed on the fourth morning, at ten past nine, on a complaint about a stolen buffalo, and the buffalo was recovered eight days later, and that is the sentence she used on herself for the next four months every time she could not sleep.

SFX: The bench. Bigger — the shuffle now goes down past the fourth step and onto the road. A name called.

RUPA: Land, Kachhwara. Come forward. Not you, uncle, you are the well matter, you are after him.

SFX: A chair. A file. Pen on paper, fast, continuous.

KAJRI: Forty-one this week and it is only Thursday.

RUPA: Fifty-two.

KAJRI: Fifty-two? Ho—

RUPA: You have eleven on your table that you have not numbered. Number them or give them to me.

KAJRI: (laughing, then not) Yes.

NARRATOR: Two things happened in the seven weeks after she signed. The first was that she became, in fact, what the letter had said she was, which is a thing that happens more often in this country than anybody admits. The second was that she began, quietly, at night, on the last thirty pages of the fourth register, to copy something out.

SFX: Night. The fan off. A single bulb. Paper. Long, unbroken writing.

MUNNI: Didi, what is that book?

RUPA: Receivings.

MUNNI: What is a receiving?

RUPA: When you put a paper in a man's hand, he signs to say he took it. That signature is the receiving. Nobody keeps them. Everybody keeps the notice and nobody keeps the proof that it was served.

MUNNI: You keep them.

RUPA: I have kept every one since my first week. Eighty-one of them are Dularchand's.

SFX: A page turned.

MUNNI: What happens if you have eighty-one?

RUPA: Sleep, Munni.

SFX: The jeep below. Two short, one long.

SFX: Boots on iron stairs. A ring tapping the rail. The bench going quiet the way a bench does when a man it is afraid of arrives.

DULARCHAND: Ah. Madam is at the big table now. Sahab told me. Congratulations, congratulations. Tell me — the godown quarters. Nineteen years, no lease. Have you thought about it at all, or have you been busy?

RUPA: Sit, Dularchand ji.

DULARCHAND: I do not sit at counters.

RUPA: Then stand. The bench does not mind. Bishnu bhai — leave the door open, please. And the tea can wait.

BISHNU: Open, madam?

RUPA: Open. Everybody on the stairs should hear, otherwise they will only get half of it from the tea shop and the tea shop always makes it worse.

SFX: A door propped. Twenty-nine people on a staircase, listening.

DULARCHAND: What is this.

RUPA: This is a notice. Served on you. Take it, and sign the receiving.

DULARCHAND: Served by whom?

RUPA: By this office. The same office that served eighty-one notices for you, in your matters, on your instruction, between the seventh of the fourth month and last Tuesday. Sixty-three of them in land. Eleven on the canal side. Four on survey plot two-one-nine and the fields adjoining it. One typed but not served, on the seed godown compound, which you have mentioned to me in a jeep, in a room, and twice at a wedding.

DULARCHAND: Madam—

RUPA: Every one of those eighty-one has a receiving. Signed or thumbed by the man at the gate, dated in my hand, witnessed. Sukhdev Mahto's is the first. He signed it in front of nine people, one of whom spat on the ground.

DULARCHAND: So what. Notices are notices.

RUPA: There is no Sahayak Thana.

SFX: Nothing. Long. On the staircase, somebody says something and is hushed.

DULARCHAND: What did you say.

RUPA: Counter nine, district office. There is no temporary wing in this state. There is no assisting station. There is no cadre. The list has been on that wall since before this district was divided. You can go and stand in front of it tomorrow at eleven; the sir sits till twelve.

DULARCHAND: (very quietly) Then this office is—

RUPA: Then for eight months you have been taking possession of other men's fields by handing them paper from an office that does not exist, and eighty-one men have signed to say they received it, in my handwriting, with the date, and the date is the whole thing, Dularchand ji, because the dates put you on plot two-one-nine in the fourth month and your own suit in the district court says you were in possession from the second.

DULARCHAND: You cannot—

RUPA: I am not doing anything. I am telling you what one certified copy does, and I have eighty-one of them, and there are four farmers on the canal side who have four years of court ahead of them and nothing to put in it.

SFX: A chair. Nobody sits in it.

DULARCHAND: What do you want.

RUPA: Say it to the stairs, not to me.

DULARCHAND: What?

RUPA: The door is open, and there are twenty-nine people on those stairs, and eleven of them are canal side. Every one of the four canal-side matters is withdrawn. Say it out loud.

SFX: Nothing. Long. The fan. A cough on the stairs.

DULARCHAND: The canal-side matters are withdrawn.

RUPA: All four.

DULARCHAND: All four.

RUPA: Say Sukhdev Mahto's name.

DULARCHAND: Sukhdev Mahto. Two-one-nine. Withdrawn.

RUPA: And the sixth notice. The typed one.

DULARCHAND: There is no sixth notice.

RUPA: Take it out of the bag and put it on this table.

SFX: A leather bag. A zip. Paper, stiff, put down.

RUPA: Read the address on it.

DULARCHAND: Seed godown compound, Chandpura. Occupant, Hariya, night watchman, nineteen years.

RUPA: Tear it.

SFX: Paper tearing. Once. Then again, smaller. Then again.

RUPA: Now the money. Three crops off two-one-nine at eleven thousand a crop is thirty-three thousand, and there were two others on the canal side you took one season from each. Fifty-five thousand. Bishnu bhai will hold it and each man will come and count it in front of two witnesses, and I will write a receiving for that also, and you will sign that one too.

DULARCHAND: Fifty-five is not—

RUPA: Fifty-five, or one certified copy goes to the district court on Monday with your own dates on it.

SFX: Nothing. Long. Then a shirt pocket. Then notes — a great many notes — counted onto a table. It takes time. The staircase listens to all of it.

BISHNU: Fifty-five. I counted twice. Madam, I counted twice and it is fifty-five.

SFX: A pen. A receiving signed. The scrape of the paper being pulled across and turned around.

DULARCHAND: Madam.

RUPA: Sign at the bottom, not the side.

DULARCHAND: Who taught you to draft a notice like that?

SFX: The fan. Nothing else.

RUPA: Nobody. It is on page ninety-four of the constable examination guide, second-hand, forty rupees, in the chapter on service of process. It is the chapter I got wrong.
