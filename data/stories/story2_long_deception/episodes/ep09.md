# EPISODE 9 — "Counter Nine"

SFX: The district office. Eleven o'clock. Counter nine — a shutter going up, the specific grind of it, and a chair being sat in on the other side.

RUPA: Establishment section?

OFFICER: Sit. What service?

RUPA: Barhagarh Sahayak Thana. Confirmation of service at ninety days. My joining is the seventh of last-but-two.

SFX: A ledger. Pages, thin. Turning. Stopping. Turning again, backwards.

OFFICER: Which thana?

RUPA: Sahayak. Assisting. The temporary wing.

OFFICER: There is no temporary wing.

RUPA: There is a sanctioned strength of four.

OFFICER: Beti — madam. Listen to me carefully, because I am going to say a boring thing and boring things are the true ones. There are eighteen police stations in this district and four outposts and I have the list of all of them on this wall behind me, and it has been on this wall since before the partition of this district, and there is nothing on it called Sahayak. There is no temporary wing in the police in this state. There is no such thing as an assisting station. There is no cadre, there is no sanction, there is no head under which a person could be paid.

SFX: Nothing. Long. The bell in the corridor.

RUPA: I have a joining letter.

OFFICER: Show me.

SFX: Paper, unfolded. A pause of about four seconds.

OFFICER: Whose seal is this?

RUPA: It is the office seal.

OFFICER: It says Barhagrah.

SFX: The corridor. A hundred pairs of feet, going on with their day.

NARRATOR: She did not cry in the corridor. She walked the length of it twice, and then she went and stood outside the yellow building for eleven minutes without going in, and then she took the four o'clock bus and climbed the iron stairs, and on the fourth step, the cracked one that had been replaced with new cement, she stopped and put her hand on the rail like an old woman.

SFX: The first-floor room. The fan. The bench, outside, waiting. Twenty-nine separate small sounds.

RUPA: Sahab.

NAGESH: Sit, sit.

RUPA: There is no temporary wing.

NAGESH: No.

SFX: Nothing.

RUPA: There is no assisting station in this state.

NAGESH: No.

RUPA: There is no cadre and no sanction and no head under which I could be paid.

NAGESH: That is correct also. Have you eaten?

RUPA: Do not — do not say one more word about eating.

NAGESH: Then I will not.

SFX: A chair. Wood on concrete. Then quiet, in which the bench outside can be heard coughing, shifting, waiting.

RUPA: The stamp is not a mistake.

NAGESH: The stamp is a very good mistake. A wrong letter is worth more than a right one, beti. A perfect stamp gets you prosecuted for forgery of a government seal. A stamp with one letter wrong is a private office with a spelling problem, and the difference between those two things is nine years.

RUPA: The man in March.

NAGESH: I said you would want that later. You want it now.

RUPA: You said you were the man standing near the man from March.

NAGESH: I was. The man from March is a man in Patna who does postings, and he does not exist either, and there are nine of him, and one of them is in this room. Beti, there was no Sonbarsa girl who came second in the written. I made her up in a tea shop in eleven seconds because you were about to say no. There was one shirt. There was always only ever one shirt, and it had an R on it because it was stitched for you, and it was stitched for you because a girl who asks a sub-inspector to read the list again in front of two hundred people is the single most valuable thing I have ever found in this district.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

RUPA: Ratan.

NAGESH: Five hundred a day. He told you on your first day and you decided it was a joke, and you were the only person in this building who ever heard him say a true thing.

RUPA: The bench. Those twenty-nine people are sitting on those stairs right now.

NAGESH: They are.

RUPA: They think this is a police station.

NAGESH: They think this is somewhere that will call their name. That is not the same thing, and it is worth much more.

SFX: A drawer. The register lifted out — that heavy sound again — and set down on the table between them.

NAGESH: Now. You have had eleven minutes standing outside the yellow building, so I know you have already thought of it, so let us not both waste the evening. You go in. You tell them. What happens?

RUPA: You go to jail.

NAGESH: Eventually, possibly. What else happens?

SFX: The fan.

NAGESH: Say it.

RUPA: Sarita's file goes.

NAGESH: Sarita's file goes. Because it is not a file. It is paper in a room above a bus stand, and the day somebody official touches it, it becomes evidence of my crime and nothing else. Her boy came back, so she is lucky. The one from Kachhwara whose well is in this register — his well matter is four hundred days old and it exists in exactly one place on this earth, in your handwriting, and the day you walk into that yellow building his four hundred days become nothing and he starts again at counter four.

RUPA: Then they should start again. Properly.

NAGESH: How many of them, beti?

RUPA: How many what.

NAGESH: How many files in that register. You are the one who counts. Count.

SFX: Pages. Many of them. Fast, then slower, then stopping.

RUPA: Four hundred and nine.

NAGESH: Four hundred and nine households. And of those, how many told you, in your own hearing, that they went to the yellow building first?

RUPA: I do not have that number.

NAGESH: You do. You always do.

RUPA: A hundred and forty-something.

NAGESH: A hundred and forty-something. So the choice you are holding is not "truth or lie." It is: four hundred and nine people go back to the pile on the left, and one man in a bush shirt goes to jail. That is a genuine choice and I am not going to insult you by pretending it is easy. Take a week.

RUPA: I do not want a week. I want my ninety thousand.

NAGESH: Ah.

RUPA: I want my ninety thousand and I want to walk down those stairs.

NAGESH: I do not have your ninety thousand and I never touched your ninety thousand, and when you find out where it went you will not believe me, and I am telling you now only so that you remember, afterwards, that I said it first.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

NAGESH: Second offer. Better than the first.

RUPA: There is no offer.

NAGESH: The register is yours. Complaints, notices, service, the bench, the calling of names — all of it, and Kajri under you, and Pushpa's window stays hers. Twenty-eight thousand, and one rupee in ten of everything the register earns.

RUPA: Why would you give me that?

NAGESH: Because the bench doubled for you in five weeks.

RUPA: That is not the reason.

NAGESH: No. Beti, open it. Any page. Any of the eight months.

SFX: Pages. Turning. Turning. Turning.

NAGESH: What is at the bottom of every one?

RUPA: A stamp.

NAGESH: And?

SFX: Pages. Faster. Then stopping.

RUPA: Nothing. There is nothing else. Four hundred and nine files in eight months and not one of them is signed by anybody.

SFX: A pen. Uncapped. Set down on the open page and pushed, slowly, across the table.
