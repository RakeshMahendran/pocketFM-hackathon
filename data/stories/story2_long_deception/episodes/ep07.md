# EPISODE 7 — "Ninety Days"

SFX: The first-floor room, mid-morning. The bench outside — but the bench has grown; the shuffling goes past the tin roof and down the iron stairs, and each person on the stairs is a separate footfall.

BISHNU: Twenty-nine. I counted twenty-nine on my staircase and four of them are sitting on the fourth step which is the step that was already cracked, and if that step goes, Sahab, that is not eleven thousand, that is—

NAGESH: Bishnu bhai, sit, sit. What does a new step cost?

BISHNU: Nine hundred. With cement, eleven hundred.

NAGESH: Take it from Pushpa, and take four hundred more for yourself for having counted properly. Nobody in this town counts.

SFX: Feet going away, satisfied. A door.

NAGESH: Twenty-nine, beti. Do you know what the bench was on the day you joined?

RUPA: Fourteen.

NAGESH: Fourteen. It has doubled in five weeks and it has doubled because of one woman screaming at a bus stand. This is what I want you to understand about this district: nothing here travels except by mouth, and one mouth at a bus stand is worth more than a hoarding.

RUPA: Sahab, the Bansi matter.

NAGESH: What about it.

RUPA: The advance book. Twenty-two names, read out and countersigned. The boy's statement, which I recorded and which he signed. The axle at eleven hundred that he was charged four thousand for. That is a case. That is bonded labour and a child, and it should be with the—

NAGESH: With?

RUPA: With the yellow building. Or the court. Somebody who can arrest him.

SFX: The fan. The bad bearing. A long pause in which a chair does not move.

NAGESH: And who sends it there?

RUPA: I assumed—

NAGESH: I send it. That is my work. Forwarding is my work, and it is the only part of this that is my work, because forwarding is not writing, it is knowing which man's table it lands on and what he owes to whom. You write. I forward. Beti, if I gave you the forwarding you would take it to the yellow building on Monday morning and hand it to the sir at the desk and he would put it in the pile on the left, and do you know what the pile on the left is?

RUPA: No.

NAGESH: It is the pile on the left. That is all it is. There is no other name for it.

SFX: A plastic file being opened. Papers, stiff.

NAGESH: Sit. Now the other thing. From today, targets.

RUPA: Targets.

NAGESH: Forty files in ten days. Complaints only, not Pushpa's window. Forty, properly numbered, properly stamped, receiving taken.

RUPA: I did fourteen in one day.

NAGESH: You did fourteen in one day and then you spent six hours on a bus to Jhajha and did none for two days. Beti, I am not scolding you. The boy was very good. The boy was the best thing that has happened to this office. But the boy took two days and the bench does not care about the boy, the bench cares about being called forward.

RUPA: And if it is forty-one?

NAGESH: Then it is forty-one and I will say nothing and you will be annoyed with me for saying nothing. And if it is thirty-nine?

RUPA: Then?

NAGESH: Then nothing happens on the eleventh day. Nothing at all. And on the day of your confirmation, somebody in a room will look at a page and the page will say thirty-nine, and I will not be in that room.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

RUPA: Who is in that room?

NAGESH: Sit, sit. Have you eaten? Your sister — she has put in a form for the examination, I heard.

RUPA: Who told you that?

NAGESH: A messenger with a cycle told a tea shop. Beti, I told you. Mouths.

SFX: The bench. Twenty-nine people. A name being called. A chair. A pen.

NARRATOR: In nine days she wrote forty-four. Land, land, land, a stolen buffalo, three dowry matters, a well, two boundary walls, a man who had been beaten at a wedding and could not say by whom, and eleven cases in which the complainant said, at some point, the sentence: I went to the yellow building first.

SFX: Evening. The room emptying. A register closed — a heavy, final sound.

KAJRI: Forty-four.

RUPA: Forty-four.

KAJRI: In nine days. (laughing first, as always) Ho, ho. You know what you have done, no? You have made forty the number. Next month forty is the ordinary thing and fifty is the good thing, and I am the one who has to do fifty.

RUPA: Then do fifty.

KAJRI: See, that. That is what I mean about you. Anybody else would have said sorry.

SFX: A drawer. A purse. Coins.

KAJRI: Listen. Small thing. That thing I said on your first day.

RUPA: What thing.

KAJRI: If a matter comes on the bench from Sonbarsa — Sonbarsa only, nothing else — send it to me. Do not write it. Just call the name and point at my table.

RUPA: Why?

KAJRI: Because they are my people and they talk more freely, that is all.

RUPA: Pushpa says "that's all" the same way you say "that is all." You have both learned it from him.

KAJRI: (laughing, but late) You are too clever for this office, Rupa.

SFX: Sandals. Fast, on iron stairs.

SFX: A calendar page. The specific crackle of thin paper on a nail.

PUSHPA: Leave that.

RUPA: I am counting.

PUSHPA: Counting what, there is nothing on it, I only keep it for the Patna bus timings, that's all.

RUPA: My joining was the seventh. Confirmation at ninety days, he said. Ninety days from the seventh is — this month is thirty, next is thirty-one—

PUSHPA: The fifth.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

RUPA: You knew it without counting.

PUSHPA: I knew it because confirmation is always the fifth. Kajri's was the fifth. Mine was the fifth. Rent is the fifth, Bishnu is the fifth, everything in this building is the fifth, that's all.

RUPA: Was Kajri confirmed?

PUSHPA: Leave the calendar.

SFX: A pen tapping a table. Stopping.

RUPA: The fifth.

NARRATOR: Four thousand five hundred rupees had gone to Lakhan on the ninth of the first month instead of the fifth. Four days late. It had cost one hundred and eighty rupees, and Hariya had brought the receipt home and put it in the box and said nothing more about it, and neither of them had turned the receipt over.

SFX: A verandah. Night. Paper — old, folded four times, being unfolded under a bulb.

MUNNI: Didi, what are you looking for?

RUPA: The back of Lakhan's receipt.

MUNNI: There is writing on the back?

RUPA: There is one line on the back.

MUNNI: Read it.

RUPA: "In the event any instalment is delayed beyond four days, the lender may call the entire principal, with the property, on the fifth day of any subsequent month."

MUNNI: The first one was late.

RUPA: The first one was four days late.

MUNNI: So he can call it. Any month. On the fifth.

RUPA: On the fifth. The same day they hand me my confirmation.
