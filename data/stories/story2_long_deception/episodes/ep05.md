# EPISODE 5 — "Canal Side"

SFX: Morning. The iron staircase. A file slapped down on a table before anybody has said good morning.

RUPA: Sahab. The stamp is wrong.

NAGESH: Sit, sit.

RUPA: G-R-A-H. It should be G-A-R-H. It is on fourteen files from yesterday and it is on every file in that register going back—

NAGESH: Going back to the fourth one, yes. Beti, do you know Ashfaq, the stamp maker, opposite the sweet shop, the one with the shutter that only goes half up?

RUPA: No.

NAGESH: He has made every stamp in this town for thirty years. Every school, every society, the sugar co-operative, two banks. He is seventy-one and his eyes are finished. Last year he gave the veterinary hospital a stamp that said Barhagarh Veterinary Hospitl. They used it for nine months. Nine months of dog bite certificates.

RUPA: And nobody—

NAGESH: Nobody. Because a stamp is not read, a stamp is seen. That is the whole of what a stamp is. I have ordered a new one. Two hundred and eighty rupees, and he will take six weeks because his shutter only goes half up. Now. Are you going to tell me the arithmetic of yesterday or shall I tell you?

RUPA: Fourteen.

NAGESH: Fourteen. Kajri did nine in a week. Pushpa did eleven in a week and Pushpa has been doing this since before the roof was put on.

SFX: A chair leg scraping. Someone leaving a room quickly and not quietly.

NAGESH: That was Pushpa. She heard. Good.

NARRATOR: He had used her the way you use a stone to sharpen a blade, in front of the person who was the stone. He would do it again, in the other direction, and each of them would go on believing they were the blade.

SFX: A jeep, heavy, pulling up below. The horn — two short, one long, a man's signature.

NAGESH: Beti. Today you go out.

RUPA: Out where?

NAGESH: Notices. Service of notice, which is the most ordinary thing there is, you put the paper in the hand and you get the receiving signed. Dularchand ji has four.

SFX: Boots on the iron stairs. Slow. A ring tapping the rail.

DULARCHAND: Ah, the new one. Sahab was telling me. Chandpura, no? Chandpura. Tell me — your father's house, the front land there, is it the piece that touches the road or the piece behind?

RUPA: The piece that touches the road.

DULARCHAND: Good land, road land. And the quarters at the seed godown, he lives in the quarters or in the house?

RUPA: Why?

DULARCHAND: I am making conversation. Sahab, she does not make conversation.

NAGESH: She does the register. Conversation you and I can do.

SFX: A jeep on a dirt track. Windows down. Wind. Dry sugarcane against the door.

DULARCHAND: See, the whole difficulty is time. If I go to the court in the district it is four years. Four years, madam, and in four years the man has taken three crops off land that is mine on paper. The paper is with me, in this bag, you can see it. But the paper does not walk to his house by itself. That is all we are doing. We are walking the paper to his house.

RUPA: If the paper is good, why do I have to carry it?

DULARCHAND: Because when I carry it, I am a man with an interest. When you carry it, you are the government.

SFX: The jeep stopping. A dog. Children. A hand pump.

NARRATOR: The village was Kachhwara. Nine kilometres from Chandpura, on the canal side, and she had come here twice a year all her life for the fair.

SFX: A gate. A woman's voice calling somebody inside. Then quiet. Too quiet.

RUPA: Sukhdev Mahto?

SUKHDEV: Yes.

RUPA: Notice from the Barhagarh Sahayak Thana, in the matter of survey plot two-one-nine, canal side. You are required to appear on Thursday at eleven o'clock and to bring your papers. Sign the receiving here.

SUKHDEV: Whose office?

RUPA: Sahayak Thana. First floor, above the bus stand.

SUKHDEV: There is no such thing.

RUPA: Sign the receiving.

SFX: Nothing. Long. Then a pen. Then the paper taken.

SUKHDEV: You are Hariya's girl.

SFX: The hand pump stopping. Somewhere, a bucket set down on the ground so it can be heard.

RUPA: Sign, and I will go.

SUKHDEV: I gave your father the bus fare in February. Twice. Forty rupees each way, both times, because he said the girl is going for the police exam and the girl will be a constable and then Chandpura will finally have somebody in uniform who picks up the phone.

RUPA: Sir. The receiving.

SUKHDEV: You have the uniform. Look at you. You have the uniform and the first thing they made you do with it is carry Dularchand's paper to my gate.

SFX: A man spitting. Not near her. Deliberately not near her, which is worse.

VILLAGE WOMAN: (from a doorway, loud, to the street) That is Hariya's daughter. That one. In the shirt.

SECOND VOICE: Which Hariya?

VILLAGE WOMAN: The night watchman. The one who mortgaged everything so she could be police. And she is police, look — she is police for Dularchand.

SFX: Laughter down a village street. A door closing. Another door closing.

NARRATOR: She was ashamed. She had been ashamed on the ground in front of two hundred people, and that had been a public thing, a thing that happened to two hundred people at once. This was nine kilometres from her mother's kitchen and it was going to be repeated at the fair.

SFX: The jeep door. It does not close properly the first time. It closes the second.

DULARCHAND: Three more. Chak road, then the two at Sonbarsa.

RUPA: I am not doing the two at Sonbarsa.

DULARCHAND: You are not?

RUPA: No.

SFX: The engine, idling. The engine, switched off. That silence.

DULARCHAND: Madam. Do you know how many pieces of canal-side land there are in this block?

RUPA: No.

DULARCHAND: Sixty-one. And of the sixty-one, do you know how many have proper paper — clean, mutated, no brother, no sister, no old lady in Ranchi who signed nothing?

RUPA: How many.

DULARCHAND: Four. Everything else has a hole in it. Everything. Your father's front land, the piece that touches the road — that came from his mother's side, correct? And her brother's people, are they in Ranchi or Jamshedpur, I forget which.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

DULARCHAND: I am only saying that everybody in this district is standing on a hole and most of them will die before anybody looks. It is a nice arrangement. It works because nobody looks.

RUPA: Take me back.

DULARCHAND: Of course. Chak road is on the way, so we will do Chak road, and Sonbarsa we can leave for now, since you are new.

SFX: The engine starting. The jeep turning in a narrow lane, three movements.

DULARCHAND: And the quarters at the seed godown. I looked at the map in the block office this morning, only out of interest, because you had said the road piece and I could not picture it.

RUPA: What about the quarters.

DULARCHAND: The godown compound is canal side. All of it. So the quarters are also canal side, and your father has been in them nineteen years without a lease, which happens, which is very common, madam, do not make a face. I am not saying anything against your father. I am only telling you that the notice for that compound is already typed. It has been typed since Tuesday. It is the sixth one in my bag, and I have not given it to Sahab yet, because there is no hurry, and because I like to see how a new person works before I decide what to give them.
