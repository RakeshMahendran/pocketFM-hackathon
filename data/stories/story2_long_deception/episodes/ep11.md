# EPISODE 11 — "Which Batch"

SFX: Below, in the hotel. A door being knocked on with a stick, not a hand. Then another. Then another.

BISHNU: Sir, the register is at the desk, the register is always at the desk, I have never in nine years—

KISHORE: Every room. Names, address, identity number, date of arrival, date of departure. Show me the entries for the eleventh.

BISHNU: The eleventh, the eleventh — sir, on the eleventh there was a marriage party from Jhajha and they took four rooms and one of them wrote his name in English which I cannot—

KISHORE: Next.

SFX: The iron staircase. Two men. Boots on eleven steps, unhurried.

TAPAN: Sir, that is the first floor. That is a separate — I have told you, sir, that is the—

KISHORE: It is part of the building. The order says the building.

SFX: The bench going silent. All of it, at once. A tin roof ticking in the heat.

NARRATOR: There were thirty-one people on that staircase and every one of them stood up.

SFX: A spring hinge. The fan.

PUSHPA: Sir, please — the scheme window is this side, if it is housing list you want, the list is with the block, we only fill the form, we have never—

KISHORE: Sit down, madam.

SFX: A chair.

KISHORE: Whose office.

RUPA: Mine.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

KISHORE: Name?

RUPA: Rupa.

KISHORE: Rupa what?

RUPA: Rupa. It is on the shirt.

NARRATOR: He looked at her for two seconds, which is a long time, and then he looked past her at the wall, and she understood, standing there with her heart going like a bus over a cattle grid, that he did not know her. Two hundred people on a ground and a pencil line through a roll number, and he did not know her face.

KISHORE: This is what — private complaints? Petition writing?

RUPA: Assisting work, sir. Complaints, notices, service of process. Women's matters mostly.

KISHORE: Registered under?

RUPA: The office was set up under the proprietor, sir. He is not here. Everything on paper is with him.

KISHORE: Everything on paper is always with the man who is not here.

SFX: A register lifted, opened, set down. That heavy sound.

KISHORE: This is yours?

RUPA: The writing is mine, sir.

SFX: Pages. Turning. Slowly. Stopping. Turning back two. Stopping again.

KISHORE: Who taught you the format?

RUPA: Nobody, sir.

KISHORE: Every entry is numbered on the top right. Every page is stamped, not only the last. Complainant's statement, then read back, then thumb, then a witness, and the witness is a different person each time so it is not one man's cousin signing four hundred pages. And there is a column here I have not seen in any station in this district. What is this column?

RUPA: Receiving, sir.

KISHORE: Meaning?

RUPA: When a notice is put in a man's hand, he signs to say he took it, and I write the date and who stood there. Otherwise afterwards he says nobody ever came to my gate, and there is nothing to put against it.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

KISHORE: Tapan.

TAPAN: Sir.

KISHORE: How many receivings do we hold at our end?

TAPAN: Sir, we — we hold the notice copy, sir.

KISHORE: We hold the notice copy. Four hundred matters and we hold the copy of our own paper and nothing to prove it ever left the building. Every land case we lose in the district court, we lose on that, and I have said it in writing twice.

SFX: Pages, one more time. Then the register closed.

KISHORE: Kachhwara. The labour contractor's boy.

RUPA: Sir.

KISHORE: We were told a woman from a private office went to the kilns and brought a boy back on the four o'clock bus.

RUPA: The boy walked to the bus himself, sir. He had no shoes.

KISHORE: That matter came to my desk four times and it went into the pile on the left four times and I am the one who put it there. I want that on record in this room, because there are — Tapan, how many?

TAPAN: Thirty-one on the stairs, sir.

KISHORE: Because there are thirty-one people on the stairs. Four times. That is my failure and it is not the office's failure, it is mine, and I have thought about it since Saturday.

SFX: Nothing. The fan. Thirty-one people not moving.

NARRATOR: She had imagined this man saying something to her four hundred times, at night, in a bus, in a corridor. In every version of it she had imagined, she had said something back. She stood there and said nothing at all.

KISHORE: Bansi.

RUPA: Sir?

KISHORE: The contractor. Twenty-two names, read out and countersigned, and the advance book photographed page by page. That is what I was told. Was I told correctly?

RUPA: Yes, sir.

KISHORE: Where is it now?

RUPA: With the proprietor, sir. For forwarding.

KISHORE: For forwarding.

SFX: A long breath out through the nose. A man deciding not to say a thing.

KISHORE: When it comes back from forwarding, bring it to me directly. Not to the desk. To me. Ask for me by name at the gate and if the man at the gate makes you stand, tell him I said, and if he still makes you stand, come at seven in the morning because I am there at seven and he is not.

RUPA: Yes, sir.

KISHORE: Tapan. Note the number of this office.

TAPAN: There is no telephone, sir.

KISHORE: Then note the staircase.

SFX: Boots. Turning at the door. The bench standing up again — thirty-one people, all at once.

KISHORE: One more thing, and I will say it here rather than downstairs.

RUPA: Sir.

KISHORE: (raised, to the room and the stairs) There are eighteen stations in this district and I have inspected eleven of them. This is what file work is supposed to look like. Not one of the eleven does it like this. Nobody is to come to me next month and say the paperwork of this district cannot be improved, because it is being done above a bus stand by a girl with a forty-watt bulb.

SFX: Somebody on the stairs starting to clap and stopping, uncertain, alone.

KISHORE: And madam.

RUPA: Sir.

KISHORE: The recruitment. Which batch are you from?
