# EPISODE 13 — "Page Four"

SFX: A scooter at a gate at six in the morning. Not switched off. A ledger against a petrol tank.

LAKHAN: Fifth.

HARIYA: It is here. It is ready, it has been ready since the twenty-eighth, I have not been late again, not once since the first—

LAKHAN: Not the instalment. The principal.

SFX: Nothing. Long. A hen.

HARIYA: What?

LAKHAN: Ninety thousand. On the fifth of next month. It is the clause on the back and you put your thumb under it, and I read it to you at the time, in front of your brother-in-law, because I read everything to everybody.

HARIYA: But I have paid every month—

LAKHAN: Since the first one, which was four days late. That is what the clause is for. It does not go away because a man improves.

HARIYA: Why now? Nine months you have said nothing—

LAKHAN: Because on Tuesday I was asked a question about your house by a man who does not usually ask me questions, and when that man asks a question the paper stops being worth its interest and starts being worth the house. I am not going to tell you his name and you are not going to ask me again.

SFX: The scooter, revving once. Not going yet.

LAKHAN: Hariya. The fifth. Ninety thousand, in the hand, at this gate. And I am saying this to you as a written man: bring your daughter with you, because you are going to want somebody there who can read.

SFX: The scooter going.

SFX: The first floor. Different sound — no bench. No shuffling. The tin roof and the fan and nothing under them.

RUPA: Where is the bench?

PUSHPA: Downstairs. On the road. Bishnu will not let them on the staircase since Tuesday, he says thirty people is a court case waiting to fall on his head, and he is not wrong, that's all.

RUPA: And this?

PUSHPA: Audit. Sahab's word. Everything into cartons, everything listed, the four registers, my forms, the scheme window, the receipts, all of it, and a lorry on the sixth for Patna.

RUPA: The sixth.

PUSHPA: The sixth.

RUPA: The confirmations are on the fifth.

PUSHPA: (a pause, and in it, the fan) Yes.

SFX: Cardboard. Tape being pulled off a roll, torn with teeth.

RUPA: Pushpa.

PUSHPA: I am working.

RUPA: You have been in this room longer than anybody. Longer than Kajri, longer than the roof. What is in Patna?

PUSHPA: A room. He has had it three years and none of us has been to it and none of us knows the street, that's all.

RUPA: What happens to your window in Patna?

SFX: Tape stopping.

PUSHPA: There is no window in Patna. He told me on Monday. He said Patna is only the registers and himself, and that the scheme work is a village business and does not travel, and he said it while he was eating, and he did not stop eating.

RUPA: Eleven years.

PUSHPA: Nine.

RUPA: Nine years and he told you while he was eating.

PUSHPA: Do not do that. Do not do the thing you do where you say a number back to a person and wait.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

PUSHPA: What do you want, Rupa.

RUPA: The receipt book.

PUSHPA: There is no receipt book. There is a paper-cost register and you have seen it a hundred—

RUPA: Not the paper cost. Postings. Every rupee that has come into this office for a job that does not exist. Kajri's fifty-five thousand. Mine. Ratan's, if Ratan ever paid anything. Whatever the ones before us paid. You count everything twice without stopping what you are saying, and a person who counts like that writes it down. Where is the book?

PUSHPA: If I give it to you, what do I have?

RUPA: A window.

PUSHPA: There is no window—

RUPA: Not here. Kachhwara. Two rooms next to the well, the widow pension and the disability certificates and the housing list, in your name, on a board with your name spelled correctly. Fifty-five thousand of Dularchand's money is still with Bishnu because two of the canal-side men have not come to count theirs and one of them is dead and the son is in Surat. That is your rent for four years.

PUSHPA: And in exchange I hand you a book that has my handwriting in it.

RUPA: Yes.

PUSHPA: You understand what you are asking.

RUPA: I have four hundred and nine files in my handwriting. I am not asking you for anything I have not already done to myself.

SFX: Nothing. Long. Then a chair. Then a woman crossing a room and stopping.

PUSHPA: Under the scheme window there is a floorboard. Do not lift it with your hands, the nail comes up.

SFX: A board levered. A tin box — the sound of tin, unmistakable, and a lid that does not want to come.

PUSHPA: Three years. Before the roof, before Kajri, before me. It goes back to when this was two rooms and a table.

SFX: A book. Soft cover. Damp pages, separating.

RUPA: Read it to me.

PUSHPA: You can read.

RUPA: Read it to me, Pushpa, because I want to hear it in the voice that wrote it.

SFX: The fan. Pages.

PUSHPA: Page one. Sub-inspector's post, twelve lakh, refused, returned. Page two — that is Kajri. Fifty-five thousand, sixth of the eighth month, thumb. Page three, forest guard, forty thousand, taken, no post existed. Page three back, home guard, twenty-two thousand, taken.

RUPA: Go on.

PUSHPA: Page four.

SFX: A page turning. It sticks. It is peeled.

PUSHPA: Rupa.

RUPA: Read it.

PUSHPA: Received from Hariya, night watchman, seed godown, Chandpura. Ninety thousand. Against post of constable, candidate Rupa. Thumb impression, left. Witness, N.

RUPA: That is my father's thumb.

PUSHPA: It is.

RUPA: What is the date.

PUSHPA: Rupa—

RUPA: What is the date on page four.

SFX: Nothing. Long. The tin roof ticking.

PUSHPA: The ninth of the eighth month. Last year.

SFX: Nothing.

RUPA: Say it again.

PUSHPA: The ninth of the eighth. Last year.

RUPA: He told me March. He told me he borrowed from Lakhan in March and gave it in March. Lakhan's paper is dated March, I have held it, I have read the back of it under a bulb.

PUSHPA: The paper is March because that is when he paid it. This is when he promised it. Sahab lets a man owe him for seven months. He prefers it. A man who owes you for seven months brings you his neighbours.

SFX: A book being taken. Pages, back and forth, twice.

RUPA: The recruitment notice was published on the second of October. I bought the paper myself, at the bus stand, and Munni cut it out and put it above the door and it is still above the door.

PUSHPA: Yes.

RUPA: My father paid for that post seven weeks before anybody in this state knew the posts existed.

PUSHPA: Yes.

RUPA: And the roll. The sir read it out to me on the ground, in front of two hundred people, and he said the pencil line was initialled and dated in the margin, and he read me the date.

PUSHPA: (very quietly) I know what the date is.

RUPA: The eleventh of the eighth.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

RUPA: My name was struck off that roll two days after my father put his thumb in this book.
