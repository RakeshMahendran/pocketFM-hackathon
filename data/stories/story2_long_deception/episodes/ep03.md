# EPISODE 3 — "The Man With the Shirt"

SFX: Three days later. A scooter idling at a gate and not being switched off. A ledger opened against a petrol tank.

LAKHAN: Fifth. Four thousand five hundred. I have written the date already, so you see I am not adding anything after.

HARIYA: By Saturday. Saturday for certain. The godown pays on Saturday and there is also the—

LAKHAN: Saturday is the ninth. Ninth is four days. Four days at daily rate is one hundred and eighty. So on Saturday it is four thousand six hundred and eighty, and I will write that also in front of you, so nobody says Lakhan does arithmetic in the dark.

HARIYA: You are a fair man.

LAKHAN: I am a written man. Fair is what people say about somebody after he has forgiven something.

SFX: The scooter revving once, going. Dust. A hen.

MUNNI: Didi, he wrote it in the book while he was still sitting on the scooter. He did not even get off.

RUPA: He got off for Sukhdev. Twice. Then he stopped getting off.

MUNNI: What does that mean?

RUPA: It means he has decided how this ends.

NARRATOR: Four days. In Chandpura a family is not ruined at once. It is ruined in instalments, and the instalments are announced from a scooter that is never switched off.

SFX: A tea shop. A kettle. A radio playing a film song two rooms away. Glass on a wooden bench.

RUPA: One tea.

TEA SELLER: Special?

RUPA: Ordinary.

SFX: A pause exactly one second too long.

TEA SELLER: Ordinary.

NARRATOR: Six weeks earlier she had been a girl who was going to be a constable. Now she was a girl who had to say ordinary out loud in front of four men on a bench, and one of them repeated it, quietly, to the man next to him, and both of them laughed into their glasses.

SFX: A jeep door. Good hinges. Expensive, or maintained.

NAGESH: Is this the Chandpura tea or the Barhagarh tea? Because people tell me the Chandpura one is better and I have been drinking the wrong one for eleven years.

TEA SELLER: Sit, sahab, sit—

NAGESH: No, no. Sit, sit, all of you. Nobody stands. Standing up is for offices. Beti — you are Hariya's daughter?

RUPA: Who is asking?

NAGESH: A man who has been sent to find you, which is a much worse job than being found. Your father came to my office on Tuesday.

RUPA: My father does not have offices to go to.

NAGESH: Everyone has one office. Usually it is the last one that took money from them.

SFX: Nothing. Long. The kettle.

RUPA: You are the man from March.

NAGESH: I am the man who was standing near the man from March. There is a difference and it will matter to you later, so remember it. Your tea is cold. Give her a hot one, and give me one also, ordinary, because that is what she is drinking.

SFX: Glass on wood. Twice.

NAGESH: Beti, how many marks in the written?

RUPA: Sixty-eight out of eighty.

NAGESH: Out of eighty. And the running?

RUPA: Three minutes twelve.

NAGESH: And on the ground, when the sir was reading the list, you asked him to read it again. In front of two hundred people. Is that correct?

RUPA: How do you—

NAGESH: Beti, two hundred people. By evening it was four hundred. By Sunday it was a girl who shouted at a sub-inspector, which is not what happened, but that is what a story becomes when it walks. Do you know what I heard in that story? Not the shouting. The asking to see it again. In eleven years nobody has ever asked to see it again.

SFX: A plastic file being opened. Papers, stiff ones.

NAGESH: The state has eleven seats and two hundred candidates, so the state is not the only one who needs people. There is a temporary wing. Assistance work. Complaints, registers, notices, women's cases especially, because a woman will not come to a counter and say what happened to her if there is only a man sitting behind it. Sanctioned strength for the assisting station is four. We are three.

RUPA: What is the pay.

NAGESH: Straight to the pay. Good. Eighteen thousand, in hand, cash, on the last working day. Confirmation at ninety days.

RUPA: Constable pay is twenty-one seven hundred. It was printed on the notice.

NAGESH: It was. And of the twenty-one seven hundred, do you know what actually reaches the hand after the fund and the mess and the two people who have to be kept happy in the office? Ask any of them. Ask the ones who got in. Ask them on the ninth of the month, not the first.

SFX: Cloth. A shirt taken out of a paper packet and shaken open once.

NAGESH: Try it against the shoulder. Do not put it on here. Here there are four men on a bench and I have seen what they do with a story.

RUPA: There is a name on it.

NAGESH: Stitched. R. Yesterday. Before I knew whether you would say yes, which tells you how the arithmetic in my head went.

RUPA: And if I say no?

NAGESH: Then I keep the shirt and I go to the girl in Sonbarsa who came second in the same written and whose father also went to somebody in March. Beti, I am not threatening you. I do not do that, it is a waste of everybody's evening. I am telling you there is one shirt and two of you, that is all.

SFX: Paper unfolding.

RUPA: Give me the joining letter.

NAGESH: Sit, sit. Read it properly. People who do not read the paper are the ones who complain afterwards.

SFX: Paper. The radio, two rooms away, finishing the song.

RUPA: Report on Monday. Zero nine hundred hours. Barhagarh Sahayak Thana.

NAGESH: Nine o'clock, and nine means nine.

RUPA: Where is it? There is no Sahayak Thana in Barhagarh. I have been past the station a hundred times, it is the yellow building near the—

NAGESH: The address is on it. Under the seal.

SFX: Paper turned over. The radio stops.

RUPA: Hotel Mayur. First floor. Above the bus stand.
