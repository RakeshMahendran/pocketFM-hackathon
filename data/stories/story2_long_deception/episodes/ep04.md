# EPISODE 4 — "Zero Nine Hundred Hours"

SFX: A bus stand at twenty to nine. Horns layered on horns. A conductor slapping the side of a bus twice. Above it, close, a metal staircase — each step a different note.

BISHNU: Not that side. That side is the hotel. Rooms are the hotel. You want the first floor, you go outside and up the iron stairs, that is the arrangement, that has always been the arrangement.

RUPA: There is no board.

BISHNU: There is a board. Top of the stairs. It has been there eight months and I have not raised the rent once in eight months, eleven thousand on the fifth, ask anybody in this line what eleven thousand gets you above a bus stand.

SFX: Iron steps. Eleven of them. A door with a spring hinge.

NARRATOR: Two rooms. A verandah with a tin roof over it, and on the verandah a wooden bench with fourteen people sitting on it at nine in the morning. That was the thing she noticed first, and it was the thing that decided her, and she would remember later that nobody had to say a single word to convince her. Fourteen people were already waiting. People do not wait for nothing.

SFX: A ceiling fan with a bad bearing. A tricolour ribbon tacked to a wall, flapping under it.

PUSHPA: You are early. Nine means nine but nine also means the tea has not come, so sit — no, not there, that is the scheme window, that is my window — that side. Behind the register. That is your side, that's all.

RUPA: You are?

PUSHPA: Pushpa. Housing list, widow pension, ration correction, disability certificate follow-up. You are complaints. Complaints is the register and the register is the whole job, that's all.

SFX: A drawer. A register, thick, dropped onto a table. The sound is heavy.

RUPA: This is full.

PUSHPA: That is the fourth one.

SFX: Cheap plastic sandals. Somebody laughing before they speak.

KAJRI: Oh ho ho. Look at the stitching on that shirt. Look at it. Mine came without a name, they gave me a strip of tape and a marker, I swear on my mother. Sahab is fond of you already and you have not sat down.

RUPA: Rupa.

KAJRI: Kajri. Eleven months. So — anything you do not understand, you ask me and not her, because she will tell you and then she will tell him that you asked. That is not a complaint, that is a system, and I am giving it to you free on your first day. Later you can do me one small thing.

RUPA: What thing?

KAJRI: Later, later. Sit, the bench is looking.

SFX: The bench. Fourteen people not talking, which is its own sound. A child. Somebody's cough.

RUPA: Who is that at the end? In the sunglasses.

KAJRI: That is Ratan sir. Deputy superintendent.

RUPA: He is younger than me.

KAJRI: He does not talk. Do not make him talk, he does not like it.

RATAN: Five hundred.

RUPA: What?

RATAN: For the day. If you are asking what I am, that is what I am. Five hundred.

KAJRI: (fast, over him) He is joking, he jokes, he has a very dry way — Ratan sir, go stand at the door, the bench likes it when you stand at the door.

SFX: A chair pushed back. Boots. Steps outside onto the verandah.

NARRATOR: She wrote that down in her head and put it somewhere she did not look at again for five months. Remember it. The listener may keep it; she did not.

SFX: A file being opened. A pen tested on a corner of paper. The scratch of a nib that works.

PUSHPA: First one. Call the first one.

RUPA: How do I call them?

PUSHPA: By what they are here for. Not by name. Names come after, in the register, that's all.

RUPA: Land. Land dispute, come forward.

SFX: The bench moving. Somebody sitting down opposite. A sari. A bundle in cloth being set on the table.

SARITA: Not land.

RUPA: Then wait for—

SARITA: My son is Phulchand. He is fourteen. He is fourteen years and two months. He went with the labour contractor on the twelfth and the contractor came back on the nineteenth and Phulchand did not come back with him, and I have gone to the yellow building near the court four times.

RUPA: The station.

SARITA: Four times. The first time they said write an application. The second time they said the application is with the sir. The third time they said the sir is on election duty. The fourth time a constable told me that boys of fourteen run away and come back when they are hungry, and that I should not stand in the compound crying because there were people from the press there for another matter.

SFX: The fan. The bad bearing.

RUPA: What did the contractor say?

SARITA: That Phulchand got down at Jhajha to buy something.

RUPA: Which contractor?

SARITA: Everyone calls him Bansi. He takes twenty-two people every season and he brings back twenty-one and every year it is a different one that got down somewhere to buy something.

RUPA: Twenty-two and twenty-one. Say that again.

SARITA: Every year, madam. Phulchand is fourteen years and two months.

SFX: Pen on paper. Fast. Continuous. Long.

NARRATOR: Nobody had ever asked Sarita which contractor. Four times at the yellow building and nobody had written down the number twenty-two.

RUPA: Read it back to me. Can you read?

SARITA: No.

RUPA: Then I will read it and you tell me if any word is wrong. Complainant, mother of Phulchand, aged fourteen years two months, resident of Kachhwara. On the twelfth of this month the labour contractor known as Bansi took twenty-two persons from Kachhwara and adjoining villages to the kilns. On the nineteenth he returned with twenty-one. The complainant's son was not among them. The contractor states the boy alighted at Jhajha. The complainant states this is the fourth such instance in four seasons and requests that the contractor be asked for the names of all twenty-two.

SARITA: That is what I said. That is all of what I said.

RUPA: Then sign here. Thumb, if you like. Here, on the line, and again on the second page.

SFX: An ink pad. A thumb pressed, twice.

PUSHPA: Number it. Top right. Then stamp — bottom of every page, not just the last, otherwise they come back saying we changed the middle, that's all.

SFX: A rubber stamp lifted out of a tin. Pressed into the pad. Thump. Thump. Thump.

NARRATOR: Fourteen people on the bench. Fourteen files by four o'clock. Two hundred and forty-one rupees taken in as "paper cost," entered, receipted, and locked in the drawer by Pushpa, who counted it twice without stopping what she was saying.

SFX: The bench emptying. Slippers on iron stairs. The last one going. The fan.

KAJRI: First day and fourteen. I did nine in my first week. Nine, total. Sahab is going to be unbearable now.

RUPA: Kajri. Come here.

KAJRI: Ho, so formal—

RUPA: Look at this.

SFX: Paper held up. Held still.

RUPA: The stamp.

KAJRI: What about it.

RUPA: Read it.

KAJRI: Barhagarh Sahayak Thana, Barhagarh, District — arre, so? It is a stamp.

RUPA: Read the district. Letter by letter.

KAJRI: B. A. R. H. A. G. R. A. H.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

RUPA: I pressed that stamp on fourteen files today. Twice on every page.
