# Episode 4 — The Shape of a Country

SFX: A heavy seal slams into hot wax. Papers snap open.

PIPPA: Ewan, do not touch that seal.

MACRAE: Mister Kerr, come forward and authenticate your country's constitution.

EWAN: It is not my country. And I have never seen that document.

SFX: Low conversation dies around a long table.

BELL: Investors are waiting, Kerr. Let us be practical.

EWAN: Practical would be checking who wrote it.

MACRAE: The gentleman who copied three hundred and twelve bond entries overnight now objects to reading eight pages aloud. Your mother must be proud of your new leisure.

EWAN: Leave my mother out of this.

MACRAE: Then give these gentlemen the efficient clerk they paid for.

MILES: Is this the actual constitution? With ministers and everything?

MACRAE: Every civilized arrangement, Miles. A legislature, courts, revenues and a sovereign seal.

EWAN: There are no names beside the offices.

ROSS: Names can be inserted. The type was set to allow it.

EWAN: Constitutions are not printed with empty spaces for governments.

ROSS: Fine paper often has empty spaces. That is where authority goes.

PIPPA: Ewan is being asked to swear to offices he has never seen.

BELL: Miss Shaw, you keep subscription figures. Kindly keep them.

SFX: A chair scrapes. Macrae places three thick documents before Ewan.

MACRAE: Constitution. Royal seal. Charter of the Royal Bank of Ravenshore. Authenticate the signatures and we may proceed.

EWAN: I cannot authenticate a government.

MACRAE: Nobody asked you to. Confirm the hand, paper and seal. Such modest work for a copying clerk.

EWAN: And if I refuse?

MACRAE: Your father’s creditors still write to your mother’s house, do they not? It would be discourteous if Bell’s office stopped forwarding your wages.

MILES: We can all hear you, Macrae.

MACRAE: I intended that, Miles.

NARRATOR: Ewan was ashamed. Not because he had made the documents, but because everyone in the room now knew the exact price of his obedience.

SFX: Pages turned slowly.

EWAN: The constitution is written in Ross’s commercial round hand.

ROSS: My best hand.

EWAN: The paper bears the same mill mark as the bond issue.

BELL: Louder, Kerr.

EWAN: The paper matches the bonds.

MILES: And the sovereign signature?

EWAN: It resembles Macrae’s signature on the office correspondence.

MACRAE: Resembles?

EWAN: I have never witnessed him sign as sovereign.

MACRAE: You are witnessing me now.

SFX: Pen scratches across parchment.

MACRAE: There. Are you satisfied?

EWAN: I have witnessed Macrae sign this page today.

BELL: Excellent. The constitution is authenticated by the government clerk.

EWAN: That is not what I said.

MILES: It is what we heard.

SFX: Several investors murmur approval.

MACRAE: The seal, Mister Kerr.

EWAN: The wax impression matches the die used on the recruiting proclamations.

ROSS: Because it is the same die.

EWAN: Who commissioned it?

ROSS: I received payment through Bell’s office.

BELL: A printer discussing clients is an ugly sound.

ROSS: Then pay me before inviting me into crowded rooms.

MACRAE: The seal has been identified. Now the bank charter.

EWAN: This charter authorizes a treasury, note issue, land office and customs house.

MILES: Precisely what a country needs.

EWAN: It gives no street address for any of them.

MACRAE: A capital under construction has many addresses ahead of it.

EWAN: It has no directors’ names. No paid capital. No correspondent bank.

BELL: It has my endorsement.

EWAN: Your endorsement is not a treasury.

BELL: In this room, it is worth considerably more.

SFX: Bell taps the auction catalogue.

BELL: Eight hundred certificates. Five percent interest. Revenues secured against customs, land and mineral rights. The first subscription closes today.

EWAN: The certificates carry my initials. I did not write them.

MACRAE: Your initials certify the register, not your penmanship.

EWAN: There is no register for this issue.

BELL: You recreated it.

EWAN: I recreated the damaged entries you locked me in with. These certificates were already at the auction house.

SFX: A stunned murmur. Then one investor laughs.

MILES: He means Bell works quickly.

BELL: Thank you, Miles.

PIPPA: He means his name was forged.

BELL: Miss Shaw, one more interruption and your desk will be available to someone quieter.

MACRAE: Ewan, look at the families of these gentlemen. Look at their coats, their watches, their confidence. Are you alleging that every man here is a fool?

EWAN: I am saying none of them has seen Ravenshore.

MACRAE: Nor has every shareholder inspected a mine. Paper allows civilization to act across distance.

EWAN: Paper also allows a lie to travel faster than a witness.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

BELL: Auction room. Now.

SFX: An auction bell rings. A crowded room erupts with bids.

AUCTIONEER: Lot twelve! Forty Ravenshore certificates at one hundred pounds each!

VOICE IN ROOM: Par!

SECOND VOICE: One hundred and two!

MILES: One hundred and five!

AUCTIONEER: Sold at one hundred and five!

SFX: Gavel strike. Applause.

NARRATOR: Every objection Ewan had spoken became, in Bell’s catalogue, evidence of careful scrutiny. Every document he had limited became, in Macrae’s speech, a document he had confirmed.

AUCTIONEER: Lot thirteen! Eighty certificates!

VOICE IN ROOM: One hundred and three!

SECOND VOICE: One hundred and six!

AUCTIONEER: Sold!

SFX: Gavel. Louder applause.

BELL: Stand beside the display, Kerr.

EWAN: No.

BELL: The certificates bear your initials. Buyers may wish to see the clerk responsible.

EWAN: I am not responsible for them.

MACRAE: Then this is your chance to appear honest while saying nothing.

SFX: Ewan’s reluctant footsteps cross the floor.

MILES: There he is! The man who checked the issue!

VOICE IN ROOM: Well done, clerk!

ANOTHER VOICE: Try not to spoil the next one!

SFX: Public laughter.

NARRATOR: In Bell’s salon, they had laughed because Ewan was incompetent. At the auction, they applauded because his frightened face looked like accountability.

AUCTIONEER: Final lot sold! Eight hundred certificates fully subscribed!

SFX: Gavel pounds three times. Sustained applause.

BELL: Gentlemen, the first Ravenshore issue has succeeded in full.

MACRAE: And the expedition may now be provisioned and released.

EWAN: The settlers were scheduled to sail a week ago.

MACRAE: Schedules are aspirations. Money is permission.

BELL: The subscription proceeds will cover the vessel, recruiting commissions and government expenses.

MILES: Then the country exists now.

MACRAE: It existed before you believed in it, Miles. Your money has merely shown better manners.

SFX: Coins pour into a counting tray. Ledgers close.

NARRATOR: By evening, Bell’s office had the money it needed to finance the settler expedition. The buyers had receipts. The certificates themselves had not yet been delivered.

SFX: In a quieter office, an account book opens. Pippa runs a finger down columns.

PIPPA: Ewan. Come here.

EWAN: If Bell sees me at your desk—

PIPPA: The auction brought in eighty-four thousand pounds including premium.

EWAN: He said it would remain in the subscription account until delivery.

PIPPA: It remained there for fourteen minutes.

EWAN: Where is it now?

SFX: Pippa turns one ledger toward him.

PIPPA: Bell moved the investors’ money into an account controlled by Macrae before the bonds were even delivered.
