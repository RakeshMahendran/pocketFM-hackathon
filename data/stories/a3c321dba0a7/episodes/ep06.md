# Episode 6 — The Only Clerk

SFX: Damp pages slap onto a counting desk.

EWAN: “We have found only jungle, mud, and unfamiliar coastal guides. The promised city is missing.”

PIPPA: There is a cargo list behind it.

EWAN: Lock the front door. Bell must see this before another passage is sold.

BELL: The front door will remain open.

SFX: Bell takes the letter. Paper tightens in his hand.

BELL: Private correspondence from a disappointed passenger is not an official report.

EWAN: Thomas was hired as a surveyor.

BELL: By the expedition committee, not by my bank.

PIPPA: Your ledger paid his fee.

BELL: My ledger pays many regrettable things.

EWAN: Read the second page.

BELL: I have clients arriving.

EWAN: Read it.

BELL: Mind your manner, Kerr.

EWAN: Seventy settlers reached a coast with no city. My manner is the least expensive problem in this room.

SFX: Bell unfolds the second page.

BELL: “Cargo observed at landing.” Very dramatic.

PIPPA: Thomas has listed every case by the ship’s mark.

EWAN: Twelve cases of proclamations. Eight chests of Ravenshore bank notes. Four crates of dress uniforms. Two boxes of medals. One printing press without spare type.

BELL: Symbols of administration.

EWAN: Zero government flour. Zero preserved meat. Zero medical chests.

BELL: Passengers were instructed to carry personal provisions.

EWAN: Zero axes issued by the expedition. Zero saws. Six spades for seventy settlers.

PIPPA: One of those arrived without a handle.

BELL: The list could be false.

EWAN: Thomas recorded the shipping marks. We can compare them with your freight book.

BELL: My freight book is not available.

PIPPA: It is in the green cabinet.

BELL: You were not asked.

PIPPA: You asked me yesterday where I had filed it.

SFX: Cabinet key turns.

BELL: That cabinet is private.

PIPPA: So was the ledger with the duplicate serial.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

BELL: What duplicate serial?

PIPPA: R-4187.

EWAN: Miles’s certificate carries a number already sold to another investor.

BELL: This is not the subject before us.

EWAN: No. The subject is everything your books refuse to say in the same room.

SFX: Heavy freight ledger lands on the desk.

PIPPA: Departure consignment. Vessel released third of October.

EWAN: Read the cargo totals.

PIPPA: Printed government material, twenty-one cases. Ceremonial clothing, four crates. Office equipment, three crates. Agricultural tools—

EWAN: Yes?

PIPPA: Six spades.

BELL: The settlers purchased their own tools.

EWAN: Agnes paid seventeen pounds for land and passage after Mara promised supplied farms.

BELL: You were present at that meeting?

EWAN: Mara made me stand beside her.

BELL: Then perhaps you should have corrected her.

EWAN: I did.

PIPPA: In front of the settlers?

EWAN: In front of everyone.

BELL: And they paid anyway. That rather weakens your case.

EWAN: It proves how much they trusted the papers we placed behind Mara.

BELL: We will discuss this after the investor meeting.

EWAN: We will discuss it during the investor meeting.

BELL: No.

EWAN: Then I take Thomas’s list to the newspaper offices.

BELL: Your forged initials are on eight hundred certificates. A public scandal may not distinguish the hand that wrote them from the clerk who benefited from wages.

EWAN: I know.

BELL: Do you?

EWAN: Yes. That is why I want witnesses.

SFX: Double doors open. Investor voices enter.

MILES: Uncle, the room is full. Macrae says the next issue is larger.

MACRAE: Twelve hundred certificates, provided our clerks have recovered their discipline.

EWAN: Before anyone buys them, Bell will reconcile the expedition account.

MACRAE: Will he.

MILES: Is something wrong?

BELL: A surveyor has sent an intemperate letter.

EWAN: Thomas reports that the capital is missing.

SFX: Investors murmur.

MACRAE: A capital cannot be missing. It may be incomplete, relocated or temporarily obscured by growth.

EWAN: He found jungle and mud at the coordinates on our map.

MACRAE: Then he found undeveloped value.

EWAN: He found no harbour, government house, bank, fort, street or storehouse.

MILES: What did the expedition carry?

BELL: Adequate supplies.

EWAN: Bell’s freight ledger says otherwise.

MACRAE: Mister Kerr, you are a copying clerk. You are not equipped to interpret state logistics.

EWAN: I can subtract.

SFX: Ewan spreads papers across the investor table.

EWAN: Seventy settlers. Government food: zero. Medical chests: zero. Axes: zero. Saws: zero. Spades: six. Printed proclamations, bank notes and uniforms: thirty-three cases and crates.

MILES: Thirty-three?

PIPPA: I checked the additions twice.

MACRAE: Governments require visible institutions.

EWAN: Hungry settlers cannot eat visible institutions.

ROSS: For the record, I recommended oilcloth around the proclamations.

EWAN: They arrived dry. The flour the families carried did not.

ROSS: Then my workmanship is not at issue.

VOICE IN ROOM: Bell, did subscription money pay for provisions or not?

SECOND VOICE: My certificates were sold to finance that expedition.

BELL: The subscription proceeds paid the vessel.

EWAN: How much?

BELL: Kerr—

EWAN: How much did the vessel receive?

PIPPA: Four thousand eight hundred pounds.

EWAN: Recruiting commissions?

PIPPA: Nine thousand two hundred.

EWAN: Printing, seals, uniforms and bank notes?

PIPPA: Eleven thousand six hundred and forty.

EWAN: Food, tools and medicines delivered by the office?

PIPPA: Nothing entered.

SFX: The room erupts.

MILES: Uncle, you said the expedition was provisioned!

BELL: I said the money enabled it to proceed.

MILES: That is not the same sentence.

BELL: It was sufficient at the time.

VOICE IN ROOM: Who approved these accounts?

MACRAE: The treasury acted under sovereign authority.

EWAN: There was no treasury account. The investors’ money went into an account controlled by Macrae.

SFX: Murmurs sharpen into accusations.

BELL: Miss Shaw disclosed that entry to you?

PIPPA: The entry disclosed itself. It was written in your ledger.

BELL: You may collect your wages and leave.

EWAN: If she leaves, nobody here can trace the subscriptions.

BELL: You believe you can?

EWAN: Give me the auction book, freight ledger and Macrae account.

MACRAE: Absolutely not.

MILES: Give them to him.

MACRAE: Miles, enthusiasm does not confer authority.

MILES: My money does. So does theirs.

VOICE IN ROOM: Let the clerk reconcile it!

SECOND VOICE: Yes. Read every figure aloud.

SFX: Investors strike the table in agreement.

BELL: Quiet! All of you.

SFX: The pounding stops.

BELL: Kerr will examine the three ledgers.

EWAN: With Pippa.

BELL: Miss Shaw has been dismissed.

VOICE IN ROOM: Then dismiss her after she finds our money.

MILES: Say it plainly, Uncle.

BELL: I beg your pardon?

MILES: Last month you called Ewan an incompetent provincial clerk. Now you need him. Say it where everyone can hear.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

BELL: Ewan Kerr is the only clerk in this office who can reconcile these books.

SFX: Several investors voice agreement.

NARRATOR: Bell’s admission was public. The same men who had laughed at Ewan now pushed their ledgers toward him. For the first time, his precision had become more valuable than Macrae’s applause.

SFX: Pages turn rapidly. Chalk scratches figures onto a board.

EWAN: Auction receipts, eighty-four thousand. Vessel and port charges, four thousand eight hundred. Recruiting commissions, nine thousand two hundred. Printing and ceremonial expenses, eleven thousand six hundred and forty.

PIPPA: Transfer to Macrae’s controlled account, fifty-eight thousand three hundred and sixty.

MILES: That is the balance.

EWAN: Every pound of it.

BELL: Record the reconciliation as provisional.

MACRAE: No. Record it as treasury work.

EWAN: It is not treasury work.

MACRAE: Bell has just declared you uniquely qualified. We should honour his confidence.

EWAN: Do not use my name again.

MACRAE: My dear Ewan, a government rewards useful service.

SFX: Macrae takes a printer’s proof. Pen scratches across it.

MACRAE: Ross, print the next bond issue with this line exactly as written.

ROSS: “Treasurer of Ravenshore — Ewan Kerr.”
