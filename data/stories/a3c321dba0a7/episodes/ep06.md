# Episode 6 — The Promised Capital

SFX: Gangplank chains clatter against a crowded quay.
PURser: Name for the southbound coastal packet?
EWAN: Elias Bell.
PURser: Occupation?
EWAN: Accounts clerk.
PURser: Papers?
EWAN: Paid passage and one trunk.
PURser: That is money, Mr Bell. I asked for papers.
EWAN: The ship that just sailed carried one hundred and seventy-three people on false papers. Mine should trouble you less.
PURser: People who trouble me pay double.
SFX: Coins counted into a palm.
PURser: Welcome aboard, Mr Bell.
SFX: Gangplank lifted. Packet whistle shrieks.
NARRATOR: Ewan took the coastal packet south under a false name, transferred to a mail brig in London, and spent six weeks crossing with Ada's plate wrapped beneath his spare shirt.
SFX: Surf crashes against a small boat. Oars knock in wooden locks.
BOATMAN: Sable Coast ahead. No dock at your landing.
EWAN: The map marks a stone harbour.
BOATMAN: Then ask the map to tie the boat.
EWAN: There should be a capital beyond the river mouth.
BOATMAN: There is jungle beyond the river mouth.
SFX: Boat grinds onto shingle.
INES: Stay in the boat.
EWAN: Are you the interpreter from Crownwater Colony?
INES: Who asks?
EWAN: Elias Bell.
INES: No. Elias Bell keeps his eyes on his own boots. You counted the shoreline twice and the crates three times. Clerk's habit.
EWAN: Ewan Pell.
INES: The name on the Briarhaven certificates.
EWAN: You have seen them?
INES: Seven months ago, two men came with a land grant and muskets. Last month, another came with a customs appointment. All three expected me to salute.
EWAN: Did any Briarhaven official receive them?
INES: You are speaking to the person who sent them back alive.
EWAN: A settler ship is coming. Thirty-eight children are aboard.
INES: Then turn around and warn it.
EWAN: I tried before it sailed.
INES: Try from the sea.
EWAN: My packet is gone. I need to see the capital and carry proof to the authorities.
INES: You need water, boots and permission to cross land that your map gives away without asking.
EWAN: Whose permission?
INES: Not Gregor Vale's.
EWAN: Can you take me there?
INES: No.
EWAN: I can pay.
INES: Men with paper countries always offer payment after they arrive on somebody else's shore.
EWAN: Then name what you want.
INES: Every document you carry. I read them first.
EWAN: The copper plate stays with me.
INES: Then the jungle stays between you and the capital.
EWAN: If you take it, I lose the only proof Ada left.
INES: Ada is a person?
EWAN: A printer. She found that the constitution, bank and royal seal came from one Edinburgh room. Then she vanished.
INES: And you came here instead of finding her.
EWAN: I came because one hundred and seventy-three settlers are following this route.
INES: That answer is nearly good enough.
SFX: Cloth unwraps. Copper rings beneath a fingernail.
INES: Eleven laurel leaves. The grants I saw had the same border.
EWAN: Will you guide me now?
INES: I will guide you until you lie. After that, the insects may have you.
SFX: Machete cuts thick growth. Boots sink into mud.
NARRATOR: On the first day, the road printed on Ewan's map took them into a mangrove channel and ended underwater.
EWAN: The guidebook calls this King's Avenue.
INES: Does it mention the crocodiles?
EWAN: It says carriages travel four abreast.
INES: Four crocodiles could.
EWAN: The capital is marked three miles inland.
INES: We have walked five.
EWAN: There should be milestones.
INES: There should be honesty. Walk.
SFX: Insects pulse. A boot tears free from deep mud.
EWAN: Wait. I hear stone shifting.
INES: That is not stone.
SFX: Rotten timber collapses.
EWAN: A roof beam.
INES: An abandoned trading shelter.
EWAN: The map labels this Government House.
INES: It had one room. Traders stored salt here before the fever years.
EWAN: Parliament House should stand east of it.
INES: East is swamp.
EWAN: The barracks?
INES: Termite mounds.
EWAN: Hospital?
INES: Nothing.
EWAN: Cathedral?
INES: Trees.
SFX: Ewan tears a page from the guidebook.
INES: Do not throw it down. The jungle did not print the lie.
EWAN: I prepared certificates naming streets that were never built.
INES: Then remember the names. Survivors may ask who sold them.
EWAN: I did not sell the land.
INES: Your signature did.
EWAN: Gregor forced me.
INES: That may matter in Edinburgh. It will not make a roof here.
SFX: Distant thunder rolls.
EWAN: How long before the settler ship arrives?
INES: If winds favour them, ten days. If not, twenty. If fever boards first, longer.
EWAN: We need shelter for one hundred and seventy-three.
INES: There is shelter for twelve at the old post, if the roof holds.
EWAN: Food?
INES: Not for a ship. Local villages will not empty their stores because strangers bought imaginary farms.
EWAN: Fresh water?
INES: Two wells. One turns brackish after heavy rain.
EWAN: Then we warn Crownwater Colony and bring supplies.
INES: The nearest official post is four days by boat.
EWAN: Take me.
INES: The storm arrives tonight. The river may close.
EWAN: There must be a signal station.
INES: On your map?
EWAN: Yes.
INES: Ask it for a lantern.
SFX: First heavy raindrops strike broad leaves.
NARRATOR: On the second day, Ines led Ewan past the last marked street and into ground the Briarhaven map left blank.
SFX: Rainwater pours from trees. Machete stops.
INES: Do not move.
EWAN: Snake?
INES: Cut stone.
SFX: Mud scraped with a blade.
EWAN: A boundary marker.
INES: Read the face.
EWAN: The letters are worn. B... R... I...
INES: Briarhaven.
EWAN: That cannot be here.
INES: Copper plate in Edinburgh. Carved stone here. Your paper country prepared more than one kind of evidence.
EWAN: The lower line is buried.
SFX: Hands dig rapidly through wet earth.
INES: There is a date.
EWAN: Eighteen thirty-one.
INES: When does Gregor claim he first received this territory?
EWAN: Three years later.
SFX: Rain strikes exposed stone.
INES: This Briarhaven boundary marker is dated 1831.
INES: Three years before Gregor claims he first received the territory.
