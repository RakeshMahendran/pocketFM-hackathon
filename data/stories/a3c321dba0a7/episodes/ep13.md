# Episode 13 — The Witness on the Certificate

SFX: A hearing-room door slams open. Papers scatter across a crowded gallery.

VEY: Mr Bell, you requested this hearing. Speak plainly or leave.

BELL: I will speak. Ewan, put the sealed grant before him.

MACRAE: No. That paper belongs to my government.

EWAN: Then let your government answer for it in public.

SFX: A sealed document strikes the hearing table.

NARRATOR: On the twenty-third of April 1835, the men who had sold Ravenshore came before the same kind of investors who had once applauded it.

SFX: Benches creak. Low voices fill the chamber.

VEY: This inquiry concerns the remaining Ravenshore bonds held by local investors. It does not concern every grievance since 1822.

EWAN: It must concern the paper that created those bonds.

VEY: You are under a creditor’s writ, Mr Kerr. You are not directing this hearing.

EWAN: Eight hundred certificates carry initials copied from mine. If I may be charged by the paper, I may question the men who made it.

VOICE IN GALLERY: Let the clerk speak!

MILES: Uncle, tell them the bonds were proper when we bought them.

BELL: Sit down, Miles.

MACRAE: Sir Alistair, this provincial clerk is trying to turn a banking disagreement into a trial of a sovereign state.

TAWIS: No sovereign state recognized you.

MACRAE: Your opinion has already been received.

TAWIS: It is a coastal record, not an opinion.

VEY: Enough. Mr Kerr, you may ask Mr Bell about the instruments presented to investors. Nothing more.

EWAN: Mr Bell, when you accepted the first issue, had you seen Ravenshore?

BELL: No.

EWAN: Had you sent a surveyor?

BELL: No.

EWAN: Had you written to any recognized coastal authority?

BELL: No.

EWAN: What did you inspect?

BELL: The constitution. The bank charter. The proclamations. The engraved notes. The seal.

EWAN: All made in Britain?

ROSS: Printed in my Edinburgh workshop. Every sheet.

MACRAE: Printed under lawful commission.

EWAN: Mr Ross, did you verify the government before printing its seal?

ROSS: I was paid to cut a seal, not recognize a country.

SFX: Uneasy murmuring rolls through the gallery.

AGNES: We were shown that seal when we paid seventeen pounds.

THOMAS: We were promised a capital, a harbour and government stores. We found none of them.

MACRAE: Survivors remember hardship more clearly than opportunity.

AGNES: Twenty-three people died. I remember their names clearly enough.

VOICE IN GALLERY: Shame!

ANOTHER VOICE: My bond has that seal!

VEY: Silence, or I clear the room.

EWAN: Mr Bell, why did investors accept the bonds?

BELL: Because my bank accepted them.

EWAN: Why did your bank accept them?

BELL: Because the documents appeared complete.

EWAN: Appeared official?

BELL: Yes.

EWAN: Because anyone had verified the territory?

BELL: No.

MACRAE: Bell, consider your answer. Your bank’s standing rests on more than one unfortunate issue.

BELL: And your office still uses my account number, does it not?

MACRAE: A historical convenience.

BELL: Then history may settle it.

SFX: Bell rises. The gallery quiets.

BELL: The bonds were accepted because the paper, seals, bank, uniforms and proclamations were made to imitate a government. No one in my bank verified that the territory was governed by Macrae.

SFX: The gallery erupts.

MILES: Uncle, you told us the seal proved everything!

BELL: I told you what I wished to be true.

INVESTOR: I paid six hundred pounds!

ANOTHER INVESTOR: He called it sovereign security!

MACRAE: Mr Bell is protecting himself by describing ordinary statecraft as deception.

VEY: Mr Bell, do you accept responsibility for introducing these instruments?

BELL: I introduced them. I released the subscription money before delivery. I allowed Macrae to control the proceeds. I did not verify the land.

MACRAE: You received your commission.

BELL: Yes. That is why I am still standing here.

EWAN: And the remaining bonds?

BELL: One hundred and twenty-six are held through my local subscription book.

MILES: Including mine.

BELL: Including yours.

VEY: Will the bank defend them?

BELL: No. My bank will cancel all one hundred and twenty-six and return the recoverable subscription balance under the settlement account.

SFX: A man in the gallery begins applauding. Others join him.

MILES: You are cancelling my fortune.

PIPPA: It was ink on paper, Miles. It was never a fortune.

MACRAE: No banker can cancel sovereign debt merely because a clerk embarrassed him.

BELL: Ewan Kerr did not embarrass me. He uncovered a false treasury that I should have examined before I offered it to my investors.

SFX: The applause grows louder.

BELL: I blamed him for a duplicate number. I called him useless. I permitted his initials to stand on certificates he did not authenticate.

EWAN: Mr Bell—

BELL: No. You wanted it said plainly.

SFX: Bell turns toward the packed benches.

BELL: Ewan Kerr was the person who found the duplicate bond, reconciled the false accounts and exposed the empty Ravenshore treasury. My bank will record that statement beside every cancellation.

VOICE IN GALLERY: Hear him!

AGNES: They laughed when you questioned the farms.

THOMAS: Let them hear your name now.

EWAN: Acknowledgment does not release the creditor’s writ.

VEY: Nor does it settle whether Macrae possessed authority to issue the later grants. That question requires the original instruments.

EWAN: Then admit the April 1834 grant.

MACRAE: I object. Its seal has not been authenticated.

ROSS: I can authenticate it.

MACRAE: You have not examined it.

ROSS: I printed it.

SFX: Ross opens a leather portfolio and removes a stiff certificate.

ROSS: The grant Bell produced was one of sixty sheets. This is the printer’s retained proof from the final Ravenshore issue.

VEY: Why was it not surrendered earlier?

ROSS: Because nobody asked what happened after the settlers came home.

EWAN: Read the date.

ROSS: April 1834.

EWAN: Read the authority.

ROSS: By order of Gregor Macrae, Sovereign Cazique of Ravenshore.

MACRAE: A title does not become false because a printer reads it with contempt.

ROSS: I am reading it exactly as you approved it.

SFX: Paper turns slowly.

PIPPA: There is a witness clause on the reverse.

VEY: Bring it here.

MACRAE: Sir Alistair, that proof was never issued. It has no standing.

VEY: Then you should have no objection to its examination.

SFX: The certificate slides across the hearing table.

VEY: The clause requires the named treasury witness to affirm the seal, register and authority before any grant in the issue can be enforced.

EWAN: Who is named?

VEY: Macrae’s own final certificate identifies Ewan Kerr as the witness who can invalidate the entire Ravenshore issue.
