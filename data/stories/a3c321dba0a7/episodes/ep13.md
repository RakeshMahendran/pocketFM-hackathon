# Episode 13 — The Country Made of Paper

SFX: [en] A dispatch slapped onto a crowded hearing table. Benches scrape. Voices rise.

HEARING CHAIR: [en] This document names Ewan Pell as the source of every loss before us.

GREGOR: [en] Then begin with his liability.

EWAN: [en] Begin with my evidence.

HEARING CHAIR: [en] The government dispatch is not a finding of this hearing. Mr Pell, do you ask for immunity before you speak?

EWAN: [en] No.

SFX: [en] The room falls quiet.

EWAN: [en] Enter the dispatch as the first exhibit. Let every creditor see what was placed over my name. Then enter the plate, the ledger and the boundary record beside it.

ROBERT: [en] Mr Pell, I advise you to reconsider.

EWAN: [en] You advised me to surrender the evidence quietly.

GREGOR: [en] A junior clerk carrying stolen papers is not a government witness. He is an unsuccessful thief.

VOICE AMONG CREDITORS: [en] We paid eighty pounds a bond. Let the thief speak.

ANOTHER VOICE: [en] My brother sailed on those papers.

HEARING CHAIR: [en] Mr Pell will be heard.

SFX: [en] A wooden case opens. Metal touches the table.

EWAN: [en] This is the duplicate printing plate taken from the Edinburgh workshop that produced Briarhaven documents.

GREGOR: [en] It is one printer’s tool. Nothing more.

EWAN: [en] Ada, please examine the lower edge.

ADA: [en] There are three faults. A split line beneath the crown, a missing point on the western star and a cut through the letter R.

EWAN: [en] Constitution.

SFX: [en] Paper laid down.

ADA: [en] All three faults.

EWAN: [en] Bank charter.

SFX: [en] Another sheet.

ADA: [en] All three.

EWAN: [en] Military commission.

SFX: [en] Paper slides across wood.

ADA: [en] All three.

EWAN: [en] Royal seal impression.

ADA: [en] The same faults. The parliament, the bank, the army and the royal office were manufactured from one damaged plate in one Edinburgh print room.

SFX: [en] The creditors erupt.

HEARING CHAIR: [en] Order!

GREGOR: [en] Shared workmanship proves efficiency. A young nation cannot afford four printers merely to please a clerk.

EWAN: [en] A nation needs four institutions before it needs four printers.

GREGOR: [en] And who appointed you to decide what constitutes a nation?

INES: [en] The empty ground did.

SFX: [en] A stone fragment is placed heavily on the table.

INES: [en] I found this beyond the abandoned trading shelter at the place your map calls the capital. There was no harbour. No parliament. No barracks. This was the only boundary marker.

HEARING CHAIR: [en] Its date?

INES: [en] Eighteen thirty-one.

GREGOR: [en] Local labourers often make errors in European dates.

EWAN: [en] Then the local labourers also kept an Edinburgh account.

SFX: [en] Ledger pages turn rapidly, then stop.

EWAN: [en] Entry dated March eighteen thirty-one. “Two carved boundary stones, marked Briarhaven, carriage to the Sable Coast.” Paid from Gregor’s private account.

GREGOR: [en] Preparatory exploration.

EWAN: [en] You claimed you first received the territory three years later. The marker predates your supposed acquisition because you ordered it before you claimed to possess the land.

INES: [en] It was not a border. It was a prop sent overseas to support a country being printed here.

VOICE AMONG CREDITORS: [en] He planted the country like a signboard!

HEARING CHAIR: [en] The entry and marker are admitted.

GREGOR: [en] The authority behind the concession remains untouched.

EWAN: [en] Then we come to the money that sold that authority.

SFX: [en] A second ledger opens. Coins clink as someone nervously handles a purse.

CYRUS: [en] I have already explained that commissions are ordinary banking practice.

EWAN: [en] One commission from your bank. One from Gregor’s private account. What service did the second payment purchase?

CYRUS: [en] Advice.

EWAN: [en] Four thousand pounds for advice?

CYRUS: [en] For arranging the subscription.

EWAN: [en] Silas, read the consideration line.

SILAS: [en] I would prefer the chair to read it.

HEARING CHAIR: [en] You produced this ledger. You will read it.

SILAS: [en] “Access to bank rooms, subscriber names, institutional seal and independent public validation.”

SFX: [en] Angry voices fill the chamber.

CYRUS: [en] The seal confirmed that documents had been submitted. It did not certify territory.

EWAN: [en] At the loan presentation, you called the two-hundred-thousand-pound issue sound.

CYRUS: [en] On the instruments before me.

EWAN: [en] Instruments from one print room. You supplied the rooms, the subscriber list, the bank seal and the words “independently validated.” Gregor paid you privately for the appearance of independence.

CYRUS: [en] That is your interpretation.

SILAS: [en] It is also the payment description.

VOICE AMONG CREDITORS: [en] Say it plainly, banker!

HEARING CHAIR: [en] Cyrus, did your private commission purchase the validation spoken at the presentation?

SFX: [en] Nothing. Long.

CYRUS: [en] It purchased my participation, my premises and my public endorsement.

HEARING CHAIR: [en] Let the record show that answer.

GREGOR: [en] A banker’s arrangements do not void a sovereign grant.

EWAN: [en] No. The grant voids itself.

GREGOR: [en] Be careful, clerk. Your own seal appears on those grants.

EWAN: [en] I engraved one seal. I certified papers I knew had no issuing register. I was ashamed, and I am saying it before the families who paid because they heard my voice. But my guilt does not create your government.

HEARING CHAIR: [en] Do you have evidence concerning the claimed sovereignty itself?

EWAN: [en] The original certificate submitted by Gregor this morning.

GREGOR: [en] At last. The territorial grant bearing the proper seal and witness.

SFX: [en] A large certificate unfolds.

EWAN: [en] Call the registry clerk.

REGISTRY CLERK: [en] I have the burial register requested by the hearing.

GREGOR: [en] A burial register has no place in a creditors’ matter.

EWAN: [en] It does when a dead man witnesses a country into existence.

HEARING CHAIR: [en] Read the certificate date.

REGISTRY CLERK: [en] April eighteen thirty-four.

HEARING CHAIR: [en] Read the witness entry in the burial register.

REGISTRY CLERK: [en] The named witness died in eighteen twenty-three.

EWAN: [en] The final land certificate submitted as proof of Briarhaven sovereignty is dated April eighteen thirty-four and carries Gregor’s seal.

REGISTRY CLERK: [en] But its witness signature belongs to a man who died in eighteen twenty-three.
