# Episode 7 — The Empty Relief Fund

SFX: Office doors slam against the wall. Wet boots strike marble. Papers scatter.
AGNES: I want the relief money opened now. Seventy settlers sailed. I have seventy names.
BELL: Madam, this is a private subscription office.
AGNES: It was public when you wanted our deposits.

SFX: Investors murmur. A chair scrapes back.

MILES: Uncle, is that one of the settlers?
AGNES: Agnes Murray. Seventeen pounds for thirty acres and three berths. Your clerk wrote it down himself.

SFX: A thick ledger lands on Bell’s desk.

AGNES: Read the names, Ewan.
EWAN: Agnes—
AGNES: Read them. You stood in Calderwick and watched us pay.

SFX: Pages turn slowly.

EWAN: Seventy deposits. Seventy passages.
AGNES: Then begin with the dead.

SFX: The room falls quiet.

AGNES: Three children buried above the tide line. A cooper dead of fever. Two infants without milk. Twelve people too weak to stand when I left. Thomas stayed because the others could not walk to the coast.
MARA: You left the settlement?
AGNES: There was no settlement. There was mud, jungle and paper money nobody would take.

SFX: A man in the investor crowd gives a shocked laugh.

INVESTOR: Paper money? Bell, you sold us a national bank.
BELL: The lady has endured a difficult voyage. We will not turn grief into accountancy.
AGNES: Accountancy put us on that ship.

SFX: Agnes tears pages from a small notebook and lays them down one by one.

AGNES: The families sold houses, workshops, carts and winter stock. Here are the amounts. Here are the children. Here are the graves.
MARA: Agnes, I did not know the ship lacked supplies.
AGNES: You knew I had children.
MARA: I believed there would be stores.
AGNES: Belief did not feed them.

SFX: Noon clock mechanism begins winding in the distance.

MACRAE: Mr Kerr, our relief treasury will answer this unfortunate spectacle.
EWAN: What relief treasury?
MACRAE: The Government Relief Fund of Ravenshore. You are its treasurer now.
EWAN: I have never seen that ledger.
MACRAE: Then this is an excellent morning to become familiar with your office.
AGNES: I do not want another title. I want food, medicine and passage home.
MACRAE: And you shall have figures prepared by noon.
EWAN: Figures are not money.
MACRAE: Mr Kerr, respectable men understand that the distinction is often temporary.

SFX: Investors whisper more loudly.

MILES: He cannot refuse, can he? His name is printed beneath Treasurer.
BELL: My nephew has identified the practical difficulty.

SFX: Bell slides a brass key across the desk.

BELL: Counting room, lower cabinet. Produce the relief balance before the clock strikes twelve. It would be regrettable if these investors concluded that their new treasurer could not locate his own fund.

SFX: A door opens. Fast footsteps descend stone stairs.

NARRATOR: Ewan had fifty-three minutes to find money in an office built to make paper resemble money.

SFX: Cabinet doors bang open. Ledgers thump onto a counting table.

PIPPA: Relief subscriptions, emigrant assistance, colonial stores. Three labels. All empty folders.
EWAN: Search cash transfers after the October auction.
PIPPA: Macrae’s account received the proceeds. Bell took commission. Ross took printing charges. The shipmaster took passage fees.
EWAN: There must be a reserve.
PIPPA: There is a column called reserve.
EWAN: Amount?
PIPPA: Blank.

SFX: Ewan rapidly turns pages.

EWAN: Here. “Emergency provision authorised against future land revenue.”
PIPPA: Authorised is not deposited.
EWAN: The investors were told two per cent went to settler relief.
PIPPA: Two per cent appears on every certificate.
EWAN: That is sixteen certificates’ worth from the first issue.
PIPPA: Yes.
EWAN: Where did it go?
PIPPA: Nowhere. It was printed as a benefit and never entered as cash.

SFX: The distant clock strikes the first warning quarter.

EWAN: Bell’s private daybook.
PIPPA: Locked.
EWAN: He gave me the cabinet key.
PIPPA: Not his desk key.
EWAN: Then bring the duplicate register.
PIPPA: Why?
EWAN: Every false account here borrows a number from a real one. If the relief fund exists only on paper, its number will belong to something else.

SFX: Two ledgers open side by side. Fingers track lines.

PIPPA: Fund R-F-nine.
EWAN: Freight account F-nine.
PIPPA: Same opening date.
EWAN: Same balance carried forward.
PIPPA: Four thousand eight hundred pounds.
EWAN: And the freight account closed when the expedition sailed.
PIPPA: They copied its balance into the relief ledger.
EWAN: Without transferring a penny.

SFX: A fist pounds on the counting-room door.

BELL: Twenty minutes, Mr Kerr.
EWAN: Open the door.

SFX: Lock turns. The investor chamber swells with impatient voices.

BELL: I trust you have found a reassuring answer.
EWAN: I found the figure used to advertise the relief fund.
MACRAE: Four thousand eight hundred pounds.
EWAN: That was the expedition freight balance. It was copied into a second ledger without a transfer.

SFX: Public murmurs sharpen.

INVESTOR: So the relief account was false?
BELL: Mr Kerr is describing an internal posting delay.
AGNES: Can you buy one sack of meal from it?
EWAN: No.
AGNES: One bottle of medicine?
EWAN: No.
AGNES: One berth home?
EWAN: No.

SFX: Agnes strikes the ledger with her palm.

AGNES: Then say it where they can hear you.
EWAN: The families were promised four thousand eight hundred pounds of emergency provision. No cash was deposited.

SFX: Several investors rise at once.

MILES: Uncle, you told us the settlers were protected.
BELL: Sit down, Miles.
MILES: Are they alive?
AGNES: Some were when I left.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

MACRAE: Mr Kerr, draft relief warrants against the next subscription issue.
EWAN: More bonds?
MACRAE: Assistance requires confidence. Confidence requires orderly sales.
AGNES: You would sell another family passage to rescue mine?
MACRAE: Mrs Murray, a sovereign administration cannot be governed by one distressed citizen.
AGNES: You have no administration. You have Ewan and a cupboard.

SFX: A few investors laugh. Macrae’s cane taps once.

MACRAE: Mr Kerr, your mother’s house is supported by wages from this office, is it not.
EWAN: Leave her outside this.
MACRAE: Of course. Have the warrants ready by noon tomorrow.

SFX: A messenger pushes through the crowd and places a sealed packet on the desk.

MESSENGER: Cross-Channel dispatch for the Ravenshore treasury. Immediate.
BELL: Give it to Mr Kerr. Apparently, he is the treasury.

SFX: Wax cracks. Paper unfolds.

PIPPA: There is no money in the relief ledger.
EWAN: But this sealed notice says a second ship of settlers is due to depart from France tomorrow morning.
