# Episode 5 — Two Hundred Thousand Pounds

SFX: Bare shelves echo. A loose shop bell rattles.
EWAN: Where is Ada Wren?
MASTER PRINTER: There is no printer by that name here.
EWAN: This shop was full yesterday.
MASTER PRINTER: Then yesterday is the address you require.
SFX: Coins slapped onto a counter.
EWAN: Tell me where they took her.
MASTER PRINTER: Keep your three shillings. You may shortly need them for your own journey.
EWAN: Did Gregor empty this place?
MASTER PRINTER: A gentleman settled my liabilities. I have been advised not to burden him with gratitude.
SFX: Shop door opens sharply.
MESSENGER: Ewan Pell?
EWAN: Who is asking?
MESSENGER: Briarhaven Finance Office. London. You are required before the loan presentation at noon tomorrow.
EWAN: Required by whom?
MESSENGER: Mr Cyrus Perring. The Northhaven settler manifest closes at four.
EWAN: What has a loan presentation to do with a settler ship?
MESSENGER: I carry invitations, not answers.
SFX: Invitation card tears in Ewan's hand.
NARRATOR: Ewan went to London with Ada's copper plate under his shirt and the duplicate banknote in his pocket.
SFX: Auction bell rings. A crowded banking hall murmurs.
COMPERE: Gentlemen, subscribers and representatives of the press. The Briarhaven national loan is fixed at two hundred thousand pounds.
SFX: Applause swells.
CYRUS: Mr Pell. You are late.
EWAN: I did not agree to prepare a loan.
CYRUS: Agreement is a sentimental word for employment. Stand beside the certificate table.
EWAN: What assets secure the bonds?
CYRUS: Land, customs, mineral revenues and the future prosperity of a nation.
EWAN: Which customs house collected the revenue?
CYRUS: Investors do not purchase yesterday, Mr Pell. They purchase tomorrow.
EWAN: I need the treasury accounts.
CYRUS: You need a clean pen.
SFX: Walking stick taps twice.
GREGOR: Our clerk has arrived. Do proceed.
EWAN: I require one answer before I put my name on any bond. Where is Ada Wren?
GREGOR: Miss Wren's domestic arrangements are not a banking matter.
EWAN: Her father's shop was emptied overnight.
GREGOR: Then he has discovered efficiency. You should congratulate him.
EWAN: I will not prepare these certificates.
GREGOR: Your mother's shop is on leased ground, is it not.
EWAN: Do not speak of her here.
GREGOR: Quite right. Family debts are private. Public defaults are so much uglier.
CYRUS: We have journalists waiting.
GREGOR: Mr Pell will perform his duty. He has a gift for recovering from hesitation.
COMPERE: Mr Perring, may the market understand that your bank has examined the security?
CYRUS: It may understand precisely this: after review of the submitted instruments, I validate the issue of a Briarhaven loan in the principal amount of two hundred thousand pounds.
SFX: Louder applause. Pens scratch across reporters' notebooks.
VOICE IN CROWD: Perring validates it!
SECOND VOICE: Put me down for five hundred!
EWAN: You have not named a single asset.
CYRUS: I named the submitted instruments.
EWAN: A constitution printed in Edinburgh. A bank charter from the same room. A seal made from the same plate.
GREGOR: Careful. An incompetent clerk questioning a banker before subscribers may sound like envy.
CYRUS: Mr Pell, do you possess evidence suitable for this room?
SFX: Ewan touches cloth-wrapped metal beneath his coat.
EWAN: Not yet.
CYRUS: Then this room possesses more evidence than you do.
COMPERE: Subscription books are open!
SFX: Crowd surges toward desks.
SILAS: Pell, move. You are blocking money.
EWAN: Silas, what price are you quoting?
SILAS: Issue price is eighty. First resale is already eighty-six.
EWAN: The certificates do not exist.
SILAS: That is why they are light enough to rise.
EWAN: People are paying above issue price for unprinted bonds?
SILAS: Six pounds profit before luncheon. Nobody asks a harbour to interrupt that.
EWAN: You knew there was no harbour.
SILAS: I know there is no harbour account in the papers sent to London. Those are different statements, especially in court.
EWAN: How many certificates has Cyrus promised?
SILAS: Two thousand at one hundred pounds principal. Four hundred are subscribed. Another three hundred reserved.
EWAN: And I am expected to make them real.
SILAS: You are expected to make them rectangular.
SFX: Ledger closes.
GREGOR: Mr Pell, the certificate room.
EWAN: No.
GREGOR: I admire a man who chooses principle after his mother's roof has entered the discussion.
EWAN: If I print them, I want access to every supporting paper.
CYRUS: An unusual condition from a junior clerk.
EWAN: Your validation is public. My work will be public. Give me the maps, harbour accounts, official appointments and capital expenditure schedules.
CYRUS: Give him the submission box.
GREGOR: Cyrus—
CYRUS: If our instruments are sound, examination improves them.
GREGOR: Naturally. Mr Pell may inspect everything.
SFX: Iron box unlocks.
NARRATOR: For six hours, Ewan numbered bond certificates while subscribers paid deposits in the next room.
SFX: Pen scratches. Numbering stamp thumps repeatedly.
EWAN: Certificate 311. No harbour account.
SILAS: Stamp it.
EWAN: Certificate 312. No construction payroll.
SILAS: Stamp it.
EWAN: No customs officers, no magistrates, no warehouse leases.
SILAS: The market closes at five. Misery can continue afterward.
EWAN: Here is the capital map.
SFX: Large paper spreads.
SILAS: Very green.
EWAN: Parliament House, Royal Bank, military barracks, hospital, cathedral, three paved squares.
SILAS: Thomas writes attractively.
EWAN: There are no surveyor's coordinates. No builder. No dates. This is an illustration.
SILAS: It was coloured by hand. Investors appreciate labour.
EWAN: The first settlers are on the Northhaven manifest.
SILAS: One hundred and seventy-three passengers. Thirty-eight children. Departure at four tomorrow afternoon.
EWAN: Why did you not tell me?
SILAS: You did not ask for shipping. You asked for bonds.
EWAN: They are sailing toward a capital supported by no expenditure at all.
SILAS: They purchased land, appointments and banknotes. Hope was included without charge.
EWAN: Help me stop the ship.
SILAS: At eighty-six, I am earning six pounds per bond. At four o'clock tomorrow, I expect eighty-nine. That is my answer.
EWAN: When these families return, they will name everyone in this room.
SILAS: If they return, I shall reconsider.
SFX: Chair scrapes back.
EWAN: I am taking copies of the missing accounts.
SILAS: Copies of nothing are still nothing.
EWAN: Then I will send nothing with official signatures attached.
NARRATOR: By dawn, Ewan had assembled the empty harbour account, the unsigned building schedule and the list of government posts with no officials appointed to them.
SFX: Rain on carriage roof. Hooves hammer a road.
EWAN: Faster!
DRIVER: The horse has one speed left, sir, and you are spending it!
EWAN: Northhaven harbour. The settler quay.
SFX: Distant ship bell rings twice.
EWAN: That bell—how far?
DRIVER: Half a mile.
EWAN: Take the lane by the warehouses.
DRIVER: Gates will be shut.
EWAN: Break the gate.
DRIVER: With whose carriage?
EWAN: Mine if we reach the quay. Gregor's if we do not.
SFX: Hooves accelerate. Wooden gate splinters.
EWAN: Harbour master! Hold the Briarhaven vessel!
HARBOUR VOICE: She has cleared the wall!
SFX: Ewan runs over wet stone. Papers slap in his hand.
EWAN: Signal her! I have proof from the loan box!
HARBOUR VOICE: What proof?
EWAN: No harbour expenditure. No buildings. No appointed officials. The promised capital is not there!
SFX: Ship's departure cannon fires across the water.
NARRATOR: The first ship carrying settlers sailed before Ewan could send proof that the promised capital had no harbour, buildings or officials.
