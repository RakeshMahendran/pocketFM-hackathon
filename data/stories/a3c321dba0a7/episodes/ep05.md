# Episode 5 — Where the City Should Be

SFX: A ship’s hull grinds against a sandbar. Ropes strain. Dense insects scream from shore.

THOMAS: Hold the boat. Where is the harbour?

SHIP’S MASTER: This is the position marked on your map.

AGNES: Then where is the city?

SFX: A wave slaps mud beneath the gangplank.

THOMAS: Nobody steps ashore until I have taken bearings.

VOICE ON DECK: We have been at sea for nine weeks!

SECOND VOICE: My boy has a fever!

AGNES: Thomas, the children need dry ground.

THOMAS: Dry ground is the one thing I cannot see.

SFX: Brass compass lid clicks open. Paper map crackles.

THOMAS: Headland east. River mouth south-east. Latitude agrees within minutes.

AGNES: Speak plainly.

THOMAS: We are where the Ravenshore map says the capital should be.

AGNES: I see trees.

THOMAS: So do I.

NARRATOR: On the third of October, seventy settlers had sailed after Macrae’s office paid the vessel from the successful bond sale. On the ninth of December, the first ship reached the advertised coast.

SFX: A gangplank drops into sucking mud.

SHIP’S MASTER: I cannot keep seventy passengers aboard while you debate a map. Take your stores ashore.

THOMAS: Where are the docks?

SHIP’S MASTER: Not my contract.

THOMAS: The contract names a government harbour.

SHIP’S MASTER: My contract names coordinates. We have reached them.

AGNES: You heard him in Calderwick, Thomas. Ewan said he had never seen a completed survey.

THOMAS: I heard him.

AGNES: And I paid anyway.

THOMAS: So did I.

SFX: Boots descend. One person slips heavily into mud.

CHILD: Mam!

AGNES: I have you. Do not pull your boot. Give me your hand.

VOICE ON SHORE: There are men by the trees!

SFX: Unfamiliar voices call from a distance. Paddles knock against a narrow canoe.

THOMAS: Keep the muskets covered. We came as settlers, not soldiers.

AGNES: Can they take us to the capital?

THOMAS: First we discover whether they have ever heard of it.

SFX: Thomas walks through shallow water.

THOMAS: Ravenshore? Macrae? Saint Alaric City?

COASTAL GUIDE: No city.

THOMAS: Government house? Fort? Settlement?

COASTAL GUIDE: No.

AGNES: Ask about the bank.

THOMAS: They do not know the city, Agnes.

AGNES: Ask them.

THOMAS: Bank? Ravenshore bank?

COASTAL GUIDE: No bank.

SFX: The ship’s cargo winch starts turning.

THOMAS: Stop unloading! We need to inspect every case before the ship leaves.

SHIP’S MASTER: You inspect it on shore.

THOMAS: If our stores are incomplete, you carry a report back.

SHIP’S MASTER: I carry cargo, not accusations.

SFX: A crate lands in mud and splits. Bundles of printed proclamations spill out.

AGNES: Are those blankets?

THOMAS: Paper.

AGNES: Seventy people crossed an ocean, and they sent paper.

NARRATOR: Two hundred and sixty miles of printed borders had fit inside Ross’s maps. Not one paved yard appeared beneath the settlers’ feet. Remember that.

SFX: London street traffic. A shop bell rings. Certificates slide across a counter.

BELL: Three farm grants, two town lots and five passages. Copy them before four.

EWAN: I want confirmation that the first ship sailed with provisions.

BELL: It sailed. That is confirmation of sufficient confidence.

EWAN: Confidence is not flour.

MACRAE: Flour spoils. Confidence attracts trade.

EWAN: Where did the ship land?

MACRAE: At the government harbour.

EWAN: Give me its coordinates.

MACRAE: You authenticated the map.

EWAN: I identified Ross’s engraving plate. I did not authenticate a harbour.

BELL: A distinction nobody purchasing passage has requested.

EWAN: Then I am requesting it.

BELL: Request denied.

SFX: Bell pushes a stack of forms toward Ewan.

BELL: The auction succeeded. Interest is due in six months. We now require land sales to show commercial activity.

EWAN: Has anyone written from the settlement?

MACRAE: Ocean correspondence is slow.

EWAN: Then stop selling passage until it arrives.

MACRAE: You have developed an expensive affection for the word stop.

EWAN: Agnes paid for three berths because Mara said there was a city.

BELL: I do not know Agnes.

EWAN: You have her seventeen pounds.

BELL: Then I know her account.

SFX: Customers enter, shaking rain from coats.

MARA: Perfect timing. These gentlemen want plantations near the capital.

EWAN: There may be no capital.

MARA: Mister Kerr means the central lots are scarce.

CUSTOMER: Are the streets completed?

EWAN: I have seen no report that a single street exists.

MARA: Because the latest survey is travelling from the coast.

EWAN: There is no latest survey.

MACRAE: Ewan.

EWAN: Ask him for the name of one street.

CUSTOMER: Well?

MACRAE: Sovereign Avenue runs from the harbour to Government Square.

EWAN: That name appears nowhere in the plans.

MACRAE: Because I have named it now. Countries are allowed to grow, Mister Kerr.

MARA: Plantation grants begin at twenty-five pounds. Passage is separate.

CUSTOMER: And labour?

MACRAE: Abundant.

EWAN: From whom?

SFX: Silence spreads through the office.

MACRAE: Mister Kerr has worked too many nights. Bell, give him air.

BELL: Kerr, outside.

EWAN: No. If the ship has arrived, we need a report. If it has not arrived, we need to stop taking money.

BELL: Outside, or your mother receives nothing this month.

NARRATOR: Ewan was frightened. Bell knew it. The customers knew it. Macrae smiled because a frightened clerk looked less trustworthy than a decorated sovereign.

MARA: Shall we discuss the riverside lots?

SFX: Door shuts on Ewan. Street noise swallows the office.

NARRATOR: For seven weeks after the landing, Ewan copied passage warrants as though the capital were functioning. Bell sold town lots by street number. Mara promised wages by trade. Macrae signed proclamations from a government house that existed only in Ross’s ink.

SFX: Jungle rain hammers canvas. A metal cup rattles beside coughing.

AGNES: The flour is wet again.

THOMAS: Move it onto the proclamation cases.

AGNES: We are sleeping on bank notes and using a constitution to light the cook fire.

THOMAS: Keep one copy dry.

AGNES: Why?

THOMAS: Because London will ask what we were promised.

AGNES: London sent us here.

THOMAS: Then London can hear what it purchased.

SFX: Pen scratches on damp paper.

THOMAS: “To the Ravenshore office. First report from the settlement party.”

AGNES: Do not call it a settlement.

THOMAS: What shall I call it?

AGNES: Seventy people in mud.

THOMAS: The ship’s boat leaves at dawn. I will send the cargo list, bearings and every name.

AGNES: Write about the graves.

THOMAS: Not until I have the numbers.

AGNES: You always want numbers.

THOMAS: Numbers cannot be persuaded to applaud.

SFX: Pen resumes. Rain continues.

NARRATOR: Thomas’s letter crossed the ocean more slowly than the bonds had crossed London. It reached Bell’s office on the thirty-first of January.

SFX: A mailbag drops onto Pippa’s desk. Twine cuts. Envelopes shuffle.

PIPPA: Ewan.

EWAN: Is it from my mother?

PIPPA: No. Blackwater Coast. The paper is damp and the postage was unpaid.

EWAN: Who wrote it?

PIPPA: Thomas. It is his first letter.

SFX: Ewan tears open the envelope. Several pages fall out.

EWAN: “We have found only jungle, mud, and unfamiliar coastal guides. The promised city is missing.”
