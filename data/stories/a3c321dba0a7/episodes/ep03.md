# Episode 3 — The Clerk on the Platform

SFX: A meeting-hall door bangs open. Boots stamp on bare boards. A handbell rings over a crowded room.

MARA: You are late, Ewan. Get on the platform.

EWAN: I need five minutes alone with you.

MARA: You need to stand beside me and answer questions.

EWAN: About a country I have never seen?

MARA: Names first, doubts later. Up you go.

SFX: Mara pulls him up two wooden steps. The handbell rings again.

NARRATOR: Seven days after Bell locked the counting room, Ewan was sent north with a case of maps and a warning to be useful.

MARA: Families of Calderwick, thank you. Tonight we take final deposits for land and passage to Ravenshore.

SFX: Chairs scrape. Excited murmurs fill the hall.

MARA: You have heard promises from travellers and agents. Now you may question a government clerk from the London treasury itself.

EWAN: I am a copying clerk.

MARA: Mr Ewan Kerr prepares the official bonds, registers and land grants.

VOICE IN HALL: Is he a treasury man or not?

MARA: He works inside the Ravenshore office. Ask him anything.

SFX: Several voices call at once.

AGNES: Wages first.

MARA: Mrs Murray has the floor.

AGNES: My eldest is a wheelwright. The notice says skilled men earn four shillings a day. Is that guaranteed?

EWAN: The printed schedule states four shillings.

AGNES: I asked whether it is guaranteed.

MARA: The land company requires roads, carts and mills. A wheelwright will not lack work.

AGNES: Let the clerk answer.

SFX: The room quiets.

EWAN: I have seen no signed wage contract.

VOICE IN HALL: Then what good are the notices?

MARA: Names first, doubts later. Mr Kerr means wages depend upon trade, as wages do here.

AGNES: Here, I know who pays them.

EWAN: That is a fair question.

MARA: And Ravenshore has a fair answer. New settlements pay the men who build them.

THOMAS: What has already been built?

MARA: Your name?

THOMAS: Thomas Hale. Surveyor.

MARA: Mr Hale, the capital has government offices, a bank, a cathedral square and planned streets running from the harbour.

THOMAS: Has the clerk seen those streets?

EWAN: No.

SFX: Murmurs sharpen.

THOMAS: The harbour?

EWAN: No.

THOMAS: The bank?

EWAN: I have seen its charter.

THOMAS: Paper.

EWAN: Yes.

VOICE IN HALL: He has never been there!

ANOTHER VOICE: They brought us a clerk who has never seen the place!

SFX: A few people laugh. Someone knocks a cane against the floor.

MARA: A London clerk does not inspect every acre governed by his office.

THOMAS: How many acres have been surveyed?

EWAN: The land schedule lists one hundred and fifty thousand available for first settlement.

THOMAS: That was not my question.

EWAN: I have not seen a completed survey.

MARA: Which is why Ravenshore is recruiting surveyors. Mr Hale has just explained his own opportunity.

SFX: Laughter breaks the tension.

MARA: Farms begin at thirty acres. Skilled families may claim sixty. Deposits are five pounds, with passage charged separately by berth.

AGNES: My family shop sold for eighty-six pounds. That is all we possess.

MARA: In Scotland, eighty-six pounds purchases another year of rent and worry. In Ravenshore, it purchases land.

AGNES: I have two children.

MARA: Then purchase two futures.

EWAN: Mrs Murray, do not pay tonight.

SFX: The room reacts with a low gasp.

MARA: Mr Kerr is concerned that you choose the correct acreage.

EWAN: I am concerned she has sold her shop.

AGNES: You heard her. Thirty acres begins at five pounds.

EWAN: A deposit is not land.

MARA: It secures land.

EWAN: Only if the grant can be honoured.

MARA: The grants carry Macrae's seal, Bell's bank certification and your office registration.

AGNES: Is that true?

EWAN: Those marks are on the documents.

AGNES: And you enter them in the register?

EWAN: I have entered land grants, yes.

AGNES: Then my family's claim will be written where nobody can deny it.

EWAN: A written claim is not the same as possession.

MARA: He is a clerk. To him, nothing exists until it has three copies and a ribbon.

SFX: The room laughs again, this time warmly.

AGNES: I will pay five pounds for the land and twelve for three berths.

SFX: Coins spill onto a table.

EWAN: Wait.

MARA: Deposit received from the Murray family. Seventeen pounds.

SFX: Applause. More chairs scrape as people rise.

VOICE IN HALL: Put me down for thirty acres.

ANOTHER VOICE: Two berths here.

VOICE NEAR TABLE: I have ten pounds now and the rest at the dock.

SFX: Coins, pens and overlapping voices build into a rush.

EWAN: Mara, stop taking money.

MARA: I have spent four months bringing these families to this table.

EWAN: Bell's bonds carry duplicate numbers.

MARA: Not here.

EWAN: My initials were forged on the latest shipment.

MARA: Not here.

EWAN: They deserve to know what the London office is doing.

MARA: And do you know what awaits them here? Rent, debt and sons working another man's field. Do not mistake uncertainty for cruelty.

EWAN: You promised a capital city.

MARA: Macrae promised it. Bell financed it. You put it into respectable handwriting.

EWAN: I copied what I was given.

MARA: So did I.

SFX: A ledger page turns. Mara writes quickly.

AGNES: Mr Kerr, come witness my entry.

EWAN: I cannot.

AGNES: You told us you keep the register.

EWAN: I said I copied it.

AGNES: Then copy my name correctly. Murray. Three berths. Thirty acres.

SFX: Pen is pressed into Ewan's hand.

MARA: Names first, doubts later.

SFX: Ewan writes one line. The pen scratches loudly beneath the crowd.

EWAN: Agnes Murray. Deposit, seventeen pounds.

AGNES: Thank you, Mr Kerr. My children will know who witnessed it.

NARRATOR: That gratitude cost Ewan more than the public laughter. Agnes trusted him because his handwriting looked like authority.

SFX: Meeting hall empties. Coins are counted into stacks. Rain of loose papers settling.

EWAN: Give me the address list.

MARA: No.

EWAN: I will visit every family tonight.

MARA: You will return to London on the mail coach. Bell paid for your seat.

EWAN: Then I will miss it.

MARA: Your case is already loaded. The driver has orders not to wait.

EWAN: How many names did you take?

SFX: Mara closes the deposit ledger.

MARA: Seventy settlers have paid deposits.

EWAN: When are they meant to sail?

MARA: Their ship is scheduled to sail tomorrow morning—before you can warn them.
