# Episode 8 — The Complaint Book

SFX: A government corridor echoes with running feet. A heavy registry book is dragged across a counter.
EWAN: Keep the book open. Seventy names must be entered before Bell closes this office.
CLERK: Complaints concerning foreign settlements require sworn evidence.
AGNES: I crossed an ocean to give it.

SFX: A door unlocks. Measured footsteps approach.

VEY: Nobody crosses my corridor at a run unless Parliament is burning.
EWAN: Sir, a second settler ship leaves France this morning.
VEY: Then speak without adjectives.
EWAN: The first expedition lacked office-supplied food, tools and medicine. Mrs Murray has returned from the advertised site. No capital was there. Seventy settlers were exposed, and another ship is being dispatched.
VEY: By whom?
EWAN: Lucien D’Arcy, acting through the Ravenshore treasury.
AGNES: Write down the people already lost.
VEY: This department does not certify deaths from a private colony.
AGNES: Then certify that I said their names in this building.

SFX: Agnes opens her notebook.

AGNES: Three children. One cooper. Two infants. Begin there.
CLERK: Sir?
VEY: Open the public complaint book.

SFX: A giant cover creaks. Pen scratches.

CLERK: Complainant?
AGNES: Agnes Murray. Settler. Mother. Former shopkeeper.
CLERK: Substance of complaint?
AGNES: We were sold a city. We found jungle and mud. We were sold stores. We found none. We were given Ravenshore notes. Nobody on the coast accepted them.
EWAN: Add the cargo reconciliation. Seventy passengers; no medical chest entered, no axes, no saws, no office food allocation.
VEY: Source?
EWAN: Bell’s freight ledger and Thomas Hale’s cargo list.
VEY: Your office title?

SFX: Ewan hesitates. Paper rustles in his hand.

EWAN: Treasurer of Ravenshore.
AGNES: They printed that yesterday. He did not hold the money when we sailed.
VEY: The qualification will be entered.

SFX: Pen scratches steadily.

CLERK: Complaint numbered forty-one. Supporting names appended: seventy.
AGNES: Read the entry aloud.
CLERK: “The losses and reported conditions of the first Ravenshore expedition are formally recorded pending inquiry.”

SFX: Agnes releases a shaking breath.

AGNES: Now they cannot say nobody told London.
VEY: Bell will sign an undertaking before you leave.

SFX: Another door opens. Bell enters, followed by Macrae and Miles.

BELL: Sir Alistair, an official summons before breakfast is rather theatrical.
VEY: So is a country without a recognized desk in any ministry.
MACRAE: Ravenshore’s standing does not depend upon London hospitality.
VEY: Its ships use European ports. Its bonds use London money. Today, that is sufficient hospitality.

SFX: A single sheet is placed on the desk.

VEY: Sign that no further settler expedition will depart until the first complaint is examined.
BELL: I do not command French shipping.
VEY: Your bank validates the treasury certificates.
BELL: Temporarily.
VEY: Your signature can also be temporary. Put it there.

SFX: Pen scratches.

BELL: There. No further expedition is authorised to leave.
VEY: Mr Kerr, take the duplicate.

SFX: Paper folds sharply.

EWAN: I am going to the French subscription sale.
MACRAE: You will return to the office.
EWAN: The sailing notice names Lucien. If his investors withdraw, his passengers cannot be financed.
MACRAE: Mr D’Arcy is conducting lawful treasury business.
AGNES: With families aboard?
MACRAE: Mrs Murray, your complaint has received the dignity of ink. Do not demand the indignity of influence.

SFX: Ewan turns and runs. Corridor doors slam behind him.

NARRATOR: The complaint was official by nine. At ten, Lucien’s sale opened three streets from Bell’s bank.

SFX: Auction bell rings. French and English voices bid over one another. Certificates flutter.

LUCIEN: Twelve acres with river frontage. Who offers twenty pounds?
EWAN: Stop the sale!

SFX: Bidding collapses into startled murmurs.

LUCIEN: Ah. Ravenshore sends its treasurer personally.
EWAN: No further expedition is authorised. Bell signed this undertaking less than an hour ago.
LUCIEN: I sell land, monsieur. Ships are a separate romance.
EWAN: Your notice says passengers depart this morning.
LUCIEN: Prospective passengers. Prospective mornings. Language must breathe.

SFX: Ewan lays the official undertaking on the auction table.

EWAN: Investors, the first expedition reached no capital. The office supplied no food, tools or medicine. The losses have been entered in a public complaint book.
INVESTOR: Is that Bell’s signature?
EWAN: Yes.
LUCIEN: And yours appears on the bonds, Treasurer.
EWAN: My initials were copied without permission.

SFX: Main doors open. Macrae’s cane crosses the floor, slow and distinct.

MACRAE: That is a serious defence from a man who has emptied his own treasury.
EWAN: What?
MACRAE: Mr D’Arcy, forgive this interruption. Our treasurer is distressed because an audit has discovered a shortage.
LUCIEN: How large?
MACRAE: Four thousand eight hundred pounds.

SFX: Investors react loudly.

MILES: That is the relief figure.
MACRAE: Precisely. Mr Kerr had sole responsibility for reconciling it yesterday.
EWAN: The account never held cash.
MACRAE: A convenient conclusion from the clerk controlling its ledger.

SFX: Bell arrives, breathing hard. A ledger lands on the auction table.

BELL: Ewan, I gave you an opportunity when others called you provincial and incompetent.
EWAN: You know that balance was copied from freight.
BELL: I know this ledger records a withdrawal under your authority.
EWAN: I made no withdrawal.
MACRAE: Then your seal has developed independence.

SFX: Pages turn. A metal seal knocks against wood.

LUCIEN: Investors, you are witnessing a treasury matter. The sale may continue at eleven.
INVESTOR: Not with a thief at the table.
SECOND INVESTOR: Search him.
EWAN: Nobody touches me.

SFX: Chairs scrape around Ewan.

BELL: There will be no vulgarity. Mr Kerr will answer one figure. Four thousand eight hundred pounds left the relief account. Where is it?
EWAN: It was never there.
MACRAE: His answer is now entered before witnesses.
MILES: Ewan, did you take it?
EWAN: No.
MACRAE: Then perhaps he intended to repay it after selling his mother’s property.
EWAN: Do not speak about her.
MACRAE: I merely read what your own security schedule provides.

SFX: Pippa pushes through the crowd and snatches the ledger.

PIPPA: This is not Ewan’s handwriting.
BELL: The seal is more authoritative than handwriting.
PIPPA: Let me see the withdrawal page.
MACRAE: Certainly. Accuracy has always been your office’s small consolation.

SFX: Pippa bends the page. Waxed paper crackles.

PIPPA: Ewan, look at the seal impression.
EWAN: The outer ring is mine. The centre has been recut.
BELL: An allegation made after discovery carries limited weight.
PIPPA: And this ink is still blue at the fold.
MACRAE: Enough.

SFX: Macrae pulls the ledger away. One loose schedule slips free and lands at Ewan’s feet.

EWAN: The theft ledger bears my forged seal.
PIPPA: And it lists your mother’s house as collateral for the missing funds.
