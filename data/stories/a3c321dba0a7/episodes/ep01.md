# Episode 1 — The Number Twice Sold

SFX: Champagne glasses clink. A sheet of bond paper snaps open.

MILES: This border is crooked. I want the certificate replaced before anyone sees it.

EWAN: Give it to me, please. I can correct the impression.

BELL: Correct it? You printed it.

SFX: Conversations fall quiet, one by one.

EWAN: I copied the register entry. The printing was done elsewhere.

BELL: Let us be exact. My guests did not purchase excuses from elsewhere. They purchased a Ravenshore bond from this office.

MILES: Two hundred pounds, Uncle. For two hundred pounds, the palm trees ought to stand straight.

SFX: Scattered laughter.

EWAN: The engraving is ornamental. The bond remains valid.

BELL: You hear that, gentlemen? Our provincial clerk has declared it valid. Perhaps the Bank of England may now rest.

INVESTOR: Does the clerk also govern Ravenshore?

MILES: No. He governs the ink.

SFX: Louder laughter.

EWAN: If you please, the date, denomination and signature are correct.

BELL: And yet the border has shifted, the lower seal is blurred, and the word treasury has lost a letter.

EWAN: That plate did not leave my desk in that condition.

BELL: Careful. A man may survive poor work. He rarely survives blaming his betters for it.

SFX: A ceremonial sword taps against a chair. The room settles.

MACRAE: Friends, Ravenshore is larger than one clerk's error.

MILES: Thank heaven for that.

MACRAE: Its forests stand untouched. Its rivers carry wealth to the sea. Every acre subscribed tonight brings roads, harbours and honest employment to a young nation.

INVESTOR: And the interest?

MACRAE: Six per cent, paid by a treasury whose future revenue rests on millions of fertile acres.

SFX: Applause rises around the room.

MACRAE: Mr Bell gave London the opportunity. You, gentlemen, give Ravenshore prosperity.

BELL: And Macrae gives it a sovereign's honour.

SFX: Glasses raised. Applause grows.

NARRATOR: Ewan stood beside the refreshment table holding the crooked certificate. He was ashamed, and everyone in the salon knew it.

MILES: Kerr, since you are idle, fetch me another bond.

EWAN: I have been dismissed from the table.

MILES: Then you have time.

BELL: Take the spoiled sheet downstairs. You will not copy another certificate tonight.

EWAN: Mr Bell, the impression was damaged after it left my desk.

BELL: You are a junior clerk from a town my guests could not find on a map. Macrae has raised twenty-three thousand pounds in subscriptions. Which fact shall command the room?

EWAN: The true one.

SFX: A short, embarrassed silence. Someone coughs.

BELL: Your wages are twelve shillings a week. Do not spend all twelve on principles.

MACRAE: Bell, be kind. A nervous clerk can still become a useful clerk.

EWAN: I am not nervous.

MACRAE: Then learn the difference between being correct and being believed.

SFX: Another burst of applause calls Macrae away.

BELL: Downstairs, Kerr.

SFX: Door closes. Applause muffles. Footsteps descend narrow wooden stairs.

NARRATOR: Below the salon, belief was stored in drawers: bonds, receipts, maps and seals. Ewan knew every drawer because he had copied every label.

SFX: Desk drawer slides open. Papers shuffle quickly.

EWAN: Ravenshore national loan. Two hundred pounds. Issue three.

SFX: Pencil scratches.

EWAN: Plate mark seven. Lower seal blurred after pressing.

SFX: Another drawer opens.

PIPPA: You were told not to copy another certificate.

EWAN: I am recording why this one is not mine.

PIPPA: Bell will call that disobedience with unusually neat handwriting.

EWAN: Look at the paper. The embossing is crushed over the wet ink. Someone stacked it before it dried.

PIPPA: That clears the printer. It does not clear you.

EWAN: The serial will.

SFX: Bond lifted close to a gas flame.

EWAN: R-four-one-eight-seven.

PIPPA: That is not in tonight's subscription list.

EWAN: Then Miles was given an older sheet.

PIPPA: Or an unentered one.

EWAN: Bring the paid receipts.

PIPPA: Bell keeps those in his private drawer.

EWAN: You have the counterfoils.

SFX: Pippa unlocks a small cash box. Thick paper flicks past under her thumb.

PIPPA: Four-one-eight-three. Four-one-eight-four. Four-one-eight-five.

EWAN: Keep going.

PIPPA: Four-one-eight-six.

SFX: Paper stops.

EWAN: Read the next one.

PIPPA: Paid in full. Two hundred pounds. Assigned to a Bristol subscriber three weeks ago.

EWAN: The serial?

PIPPA: R-four-one-eight-seven.

SFX: The salon applauds Macrae overhead.

EWAN: The bond I was accused of misprinting carries a serial number already sold to a different investor.
