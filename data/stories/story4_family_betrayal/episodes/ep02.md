# EPISODE 2 — "The Gate at Nine"

SFX: A knife being washed. Water from a hand-pump, hard, over metal, on and on, longer than washing a knife takes.

CHHOTU: Didi. Didi, it's clean.

KESAR: There's chalk in the handle.

CHHOTU: Didi —

KESAR: Four strokes. He didn't even need four. My name is five letters and he did it in four.

SFX: The pump handle. Water.

CHHOTU: I have to go. Second bell. If I'm not at the second bell he takes it off my — second bell. Sorry, didi.

SFX: Boy's feet running on gravel, gone.

NARRATOR: Two thousand people at the fair that morning. By evening every one of them knew a story about a sweeper girl who put her hand on a rich man's bell. Not one of them knew what she was actually holding, and neither did she.

SFX: Interior. Low. A mud stove, one pot. A woman moving in the dark, deliberately not lighting the lamp.

KESAR: Say something.

BHANWARI: Forgive me. Eat.

KESAR: Ma. Say something.

BHANWARI: Forgive me, but eat first. It's gone nine.

KESAR: Thirty-one years you have swept that courtyard. Thirty-one. Have you ever once been paid?

BHANWARI: (a beat) Forgive me. That is not a question people ask.

KESAR: I'm asking it.

BHANWARI: Then you're a fool and I raised a fool.

SFX: The pot set down too hard.

KESAR: Who is mohari?

SFX: Nothing. Long.

BHANWARI: Where did you hear that name.

KESAR: Girdhari said it. Not to me. He said it to Tejmal behind the storeroom when they thought the door was shut. He said, "the girl has mohari's face and mohari's mouth, God help this village."

BHANWARI: Girdhari talks.

KESAR: Who is she?

BHANWARI: Nobody. Dead. Nineteen years. Eat.

SFX: A door. Bhanwari going out into the night. Kesar's chair scraping — following — and then stopping.

NARRATOR: She was ashamed to follow her mother. She followed her mother.

SFX: Village night. Dogs. A generator somewhere. Then a big gate — iron, oiled, expensive — and a chowkidar's stool.

BHANWARI: Sahib. Sahib, it's Bhanwari.

JORAWAR: (unhurried, from a chair, tea) At this hour. Look at that. Your mother came at this hour too, in the end.

BHANWARI: Forgive me.

JORAWAR: I'm not angry. Sit on the step. Not the chair, the step. Good. What a good sari that is. Is that the one from the fair year before last?

BHANWARI: Yes, sahib.

JORAWAR: You've kept it well. Say what you came to say.

SFX: Kesar's breathing, close, from the other side of the wall. She is going to hear this.

NARRATOR: She thought she knew what her mother had come to ask for. She had already decided to be angry about it.

BHANWARI: Sahib. I've swept that courtyard since I was nineteen. I have never asked you for one thing.

JORAWAR: You haven't. It's noticed.

BHANWARI: Then I ask now.

JORAWAR: Ask.

SFX: A long breath.

BHANWARI: The girl is nothing. Leave her scraped off. Put anyone you like on the board.

JORAWAR: (interested for the first time) ...Then what are you asking me for, Bhanwari?

BHANWARI: Don't open the sixty-eight page.
