# EPISODE 1 — "A Paying Day"

SFX: Dark. A rope creaking through an iron ring, fast, hand over hand.

SFX: A bell. One stroke. Iron, cracked at the lip, and it goes out over the fields for a long time.

KESAR: Four o'clock. Ninety-one lamps. Three of them are cracked and I have told them three times.

SFX: Bare feet on wet stone. Oil sloshing in a tin.

NARRATOR: She has rung that bell every morning for fifteen years. Nobody has ever paid her for it. Remember that.

SFX: Crowd noise rising outside the wall — cartwheels, hawkers, a loudspeaker being tested. The fair.

CHHOTU: (hissed) Kesar didi. Didi. Get down.

KESAR: I am not finished.

CHHOTU: It's the fair. It's the fair day. Second bell. I'm supposed to — second bell, didi, get down —

SFX: A gate swinging. Many feet. The crowd noise comes in with them and does not stop.

JORAWAR: What a good rope. Almost new, isn't it.

SFX: The rope going still.

KESAR: Manager sahib.

JORAWAR: Take it off her.

SFX: Hands. Cloth tearing at the shoulder. The rope slapping the wall.

KESAR: I ring it at four. I have always rung it at four.

JORAWAR: On four hundred and eleven days of the year, yes. Nobody minds. Today is not one of those days. Today is mine.

KESAR: It's a bell.

JORAWAR: It's a paying day. Everything that falls in that courtyard between this bell and the last one falls to me. And you put your hand on it in front of two thousand people.

SFX: The crowd. Somebody laughs. Somebody else shushes them and the shushing is worse.

MAN IN QUEUE: Who is she?

GIRDHARI: (flat, close, embarrassed) Nobody. Sweeper. The book doesn't have her.

NARRATOR: He was ashamed when he said it. That did not stop him saying it.

JORAWAR: Bhanwari. Your daughter.

BHANWARI: Forgive me. Forgive me, sahib, she's a fool, she doesn't —

JORAWAR: I'm not angry. Look at me. Am I angry?

BHANWARI: No, sahib.

JORAWAR: I'm not angry. I'm just going to make it clear, because clear is kinder. Girdhari. The board.

SFX: A wooden board being lifted off two nails.

KESAR: (small) There are only nine names on it.

JORAWAR: Eight.

GIRDHARI: Which knife, sahib?

JORAWAR: Whichever one is in your hand.

SFX: A coconut knife. Four strokes, dragged across chalk and wood — her name off the seva board — while the fair queue watches.
