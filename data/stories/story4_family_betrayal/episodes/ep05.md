# EPISODE 5 — "A Good Door"

SFX: A cloth-bound register being carried across stone by a man in no hurry. Forty people breathing. Then — a wooden lid closing on it.

JORAWAR: On second thought, put it back.

SFX: Every one of the forty people making a small sound at once.

GIRDHARI: Sahib, you said —

JORAWAR: I said properly. Properly means the day of the re-cut, with the sub-registrar's man sitting here and everybody's papers in their hands. Not by lamplight with the oil accounts half done. Rukmani. What have you got left?

RUKMANI: Oil: nine hundred. Board is short one name. I've written that down twice now.

JORAWAR: Write it a third time. Committee's finished. Go and eat.

SFX: Forty people getting up. The particular disappointed shuffle of a crowd denied.

NARRATOR: He had a courtyard full of witnesses and a girl standing in front of it with no answer, and he sent them all home. She thought she had been spared. Remember that.

SFX: Cut. A narrow lane. Two women walking, one fast, one slow.

KESAR: Ma. He was going to open it. In front of everyone. And then he didn't.

BHANWARI: Walk faster.

KESAR: Why didn't he?

BHANWARI: Because he's clever and you're not. Walk faster.

KESAR: Mohari was my father's mother.

SFX: A woman stopping dead in a lane at night.

BHANWARI: Who told you that.

KESAR: Girdhari kaka. He said she died in my room. Ma — she died in my room and you never once —

BHANWARI: Forgive me.

KESAR: Don't. Don't say that to me, I'm your daughter, don't say forgive me to your —

BHANWARI: Forgive me. There is nothing else I know how to say and there is nothing else that has ever worked.

SFX: Nothing. Long. A dog, far off.

BHANWARI: Nine days is not nine days, Kesar. Nine days is two fair days and seven ordinary ones and the two fair days alone are more money than this house has seen since your grandfather died. Do you understand what you have started walking towards?

KESAR: How much?

BHANWARI: What?

KESAR: How much are the two fair days.

BHANWARI: (quietly, and this is the first true thing she has said in five episodes) One lakh forty. Each.

SFX: Nothing.

KESAR: You know the number.

BHANWARI: I have known the number for nineteen years. Now come inside and shut the door.

SFX: A wooden door in a mud wall. Shut. Bolted. Then, four seconds later, knocked on.

SFX: Knocking. Three, polite, patient.

BHANWARI: (a whisper, terrible) Get in the back. Get in the back, get in the back —

SFX: The bolt. The door.

JORAWAR: Bhanwari. What a good door this is. Sagwan?

BHANWARI: I don't know, sahib.

JORAWAR: It's sagwan. Somebody put a sagwan door on a mud house. Somebody had a good year once. May I?

SFX: A man stepping into a one-room house. The room getting smaller.

JORAWAR: Sit. It's your house, sit. No, both of you. Kesar, come out from the back, I can hear you breathing, you were always a loud child.

SFX: Bare feet, reluctant.

JORAWAR: Good. Now. I am going to be kinder to you than anybody in this village would be, and I want you both to notice that I'm doing it with the door open.

SFX: The door being pushed wider. The lane sounds coming in. Somebody outside pretending not to listen.

NARRATOR: He opened the door on purpose. Everything this man does to a person, he does where two or three neighbours can hear it, because a thing done in front of witnesses is very hard to call a threat afterwards.

JORAWAR: Kesar. You want to be on the board.

KESAR: I want to be paid.

JORAWAR: (a real laugh) Ha. That's your grandmother's mouth. Everyone says it and it's true. Fine. Paid. Two hundred a month, backdated to Chaitra, and your name goes back on the board tonight in front of whoever you like. Girdhari can chalk it while the tea is still hot.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

KESAR: And.

JORAWAR: And your mother signs a paper.

SFX: Paper. One sheet. Town paper — stiff, typed, folded twice.

BHANWARI: (very fast) Give it. Give it to me, sahib, I'll —

KESAR: Ma. Read it first.

BHANWARI: I can't read it.

KESAR: Then I'll read it.

JORAWAR: Read it. I brought it to be read. Out loud, if you like. The lane's listening anyway.

SFX: Paper unfolding.

KESAR: (reading, slowly) "I, Bhanwari, widow, resident of Barkheri, state on oath that Mohari, widow, mother of my late husband, at no time held any turn of worship, share, day or part of a day in the circuit of the shrine of Baba Ghanteshwar, and that any entry to the contrary in the shrine register is an error of the writer."

SFX: Nothing.

KESAR: That's a lie.

JORAWAR: It's a paper. Papers aren't true or false, child, they're registered or they're not. That's the whole of the law and it took me thirty years to learn it.

KESAR: Girdhari kaka read me the book. Sixty-seven. Her name is in it.

JORAWAR: Yes.

KESAR: You're not even —

JORAWAR: I'm not denying it. Why would I deny it? I'm asking your mother to say it was a mistake, and your mother is going to, and then you'll be on the board, and in eleven years nobody will remember there was ever an argument. Bhanwari. Are you going to?

SFX: A woman breathing. A very long time.

BHANWARI: Sahib. Not in front of her.

JORAWAR: In front of her is the only place it's worth anything.

BHANWARI: Sahib —

JORAWAR: Bhanwari. Am I angry?

BHANWARI: (breaking) No, sahib.

JORAWAR: I'm not angry. Put your thumb on it.

KESAR: Ma, don't —

SFX: A tin of stamp-pad ink being opened. That small, obscene, blue-purple click.

KESAR: Ma. Ma, don't, look at me —

SFX: Nothing. Long. Then a low sound out of Bhanwari, not a word.

KESAR: (a change in her voice) Ma. Why aren't you — why are you looking at the bottom of the page?

SFX: Paper turned to the lamp.

KESAR: There's already a thumbprint on it.

JORAWAR: (mild) Ah. That's the second sheet. It got stapled in. My mistake.

KESAR: There is already a thumbprint at the bottom of this paper and it's my mother's, and the date under it is the fourteenth of Magh, nineteen years ago.
