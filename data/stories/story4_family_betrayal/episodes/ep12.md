# EPISODE 12 — "Persons Present"

SFX: A government office. Ceiling fan with a tick in it. A rubber stamp, hit twice, on somebody else's file. A queue that has been there since eight.

HUKUM: Next. No. Next.

CLERK: Sahib, he's been here three —

HUKUM: He has been here three days because he keeps bringing me a photocopy, and a photocopy is not a column I have. Next.

SFX: A file slapped shut. The queue shuffling.

NARRATOR: Nineteen months from his pension. He had said no to this district for thirty-one years and had never once been transferred, which in this state is the highest praise there is.

SFX: Heavy shoes. A door held open by somebody else. The queue going quiet the way a queue goes quiet.

JORAWAR: Hukum sahib. What a good fan that is. Original Usha?

HUKUM: Original Usha.

JORAWAR: They don't make it. Three minutes of your time.

SFX: A cloth bundle set on a desk. Big. It thumps.

JORAWAR: The Barkheri circuit. Re-cut, sealed on the full moon, Thursday. Fifty-one instruments, all in order, all drawn by Phoolchand vakil. I need the schedule endorsed before Thursday so nobody can say afterwards it was done in a village.

SFX: A knot untied. Paper.

HUKUM: Sit.

JORAWAR: I'll stand, it's three minutes.

HUKUM: Sit down, Jorawar sahib.

SFX: A chair.

SFX: Paper. Sheet after sheet after sheet, and the fan ticking, and nobody in the queue leaving.

HUKUM: This is a transfer.

JORAWAR: It's a re-cut.

HUKUM: It says the block of the second party is enlarged by nine days and the block of the fourth party is reduced by nine days. That is a transfer of a turn of worship. A turn of worship is immovable property.

JORAWAR: I know it is. I said so to two hundred people last month.

HUKUM: Then you know it requires registration.

JORAWAR: The re-cut requires registration. That is what I am here for. Endorse it.

HUKUM: I can endorse a re-cut that redistributes what the parties already hold. I cannot endorse one that redistributes what they do not.

SFX: Nothing. The fan.

JORAWAR: Meaning.

HUKUM: Meaning show me the registered instrument by which the second party's block acquired those nine days.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

JORAWAR: It's in the bundle. Sixty-eight.

SFX: Paper, gone through. A clerk called. More paper.

HUKUM: Sahib. There is a deed of sixty-eight in this bundle. It is a fair copy in Phoolchand vakil's hand and it is very beautiful and it has never been near this office. There is no serial. There is no book and page. There is no fee stamp. I have looked in the sixty-eight book, and the sixty-nine book, and I sent the boy to look in the seventy book, because it happens.

JORAWAR: And?

HUKUM: It has never been presented. Not in fifty-seven years.

SFX: A queue of forty people not pretending any more.

JORAWAR: Hukum sahib. Every paper in that bundle is like that. Every paper in this taluka is like that. Nobody registers a turn. Nobody has ever registered a turn.

HUKUM: (and this is the sentence he has waited thirty-one years to say to a man like this) I know, sahib. That is the whole difficulty, isn't it.

SFX: Nothing.

JORAWAR: Fifty-one instruments.

HUKUM: Fifty-one instruments and not one presentation. I am not saying they are false. I am not saying anything about them at all. I am saying I cannot write them into a book I have to sign, nineteen months before my pension, when the newspapers in Jaipur have started printing the word Barkheri.

SFX: A man standing up out of a chair, very slowly.

JORAWAR: Whose name is on this office, Hukum sahib?

HUKUM: Mine. That's rather my point.

SFX: Cut. The corridor. Loud. The queue talking all at once.

SFX: A different pair of feet — small, bare, on government stone.

CLERK: Ay. Ay! No slippers off, you can't — who are you?

KESAR: I have a document for registration.

CLERK: Executant?

KESAR: Him.

SFX: An extremely old man being helped up the last step by two people.

PHOOLCHAND: (to nobody) Thirty-one steps. Thirty-one steps.

CLERK: Phoolchand vakil?

PHOOLCHAND: Phoolchand vakil.

CLERK: Sahib, you haven't been up here since —

PHOOLCHAND: Since nineteen seventy-four. I know when it was. Move.

SFX: Cut. The office. The fan. Jorawar still standing.

HUKUM: What is it.

PHOOLCHAND: A declaration. Four paragraphs. I have drawn twelve thousand documents in this taluka and this is the shortest one and it took me nine days.

HUKUM: Read it.

PHOOLCHAND: One. That on the eleventh of January, nineteen sixty-eight, in my office at Jhilan, I drew an instrument by which the nine days of Mohari, widow, of Barkheri, in the circuit of the shrine of Baba Ghanteshwar, were expressed to be sold for rupees eleven hundred.

SFX: Nothing.

PHOOLCHAND: Two. That the said Mohari was unlettered, was identified by me personally, and affixed her mark in my presence.

PHOOLCHAND: Three. That the said instrument was never presented for registration in this or any office, and that I did not present it, and that I was not instructed to present it.

SFX: The fan.

PHOOLCHAND: Four. That a turn of worship, being an office, cannot be transferred by sale for money — and that I have known this since the year nineteen seventy-four, and have drawn one hundred and nine further instruments in this taluka since, and have never once said so aloud until today.

SFX: Nothing. Very long.

HUKUM: ...Vakil sahib. You understand what paragraph four does to you.

PHOOLCHAND: (mildly) I am eighty-one. It does very little to me and a great deal to everybody else. Register it.

HUKUM: Identifying witnesses.

NARMADA: Me.

SFX: A woman stepping forward from the back of a government office.

NARMADA: Narmada. Widow of Mohari's husband's younger brother. Forty-one years I knew her. I identify this girl as the daughter of Mohari's son, and I will say it in that courtyard on Thursday, which is where I have wanted to say it since nineteen sixty-eight.

HUKUM: Second witness.

PHOOLCHAND: Me. I identified the grandmother in this office in sixty-eight. I identify the granddaughter in this office today. It is the same identification, sahib, fifty-seven years apart, and I would like it recorded that way.

SFX: A very long pause. A pen taken out of a pocket.

HUKUM: Clerk. Book four. New entry.

CLERK: Sahib, book four is —

HUKUM: Book four is where declarations go and I have not opened it since March. Bring it.

SFX: An enormous ledger dropped on a desk. Pages.

HUKUM: (writing) Right. Read it back to me as I write, because when the newspapers come I want forty people in a corridor to have heard it.

SFX: A pen on government paper. Slow.

HUKUM: (reading as he writes) "Book four. Serial two hundred and six. Declaration. Nine days of the circuit of the shrine of Baba Ghanteshwar, Barkheri, twenty-ninth Chaitra to seventh Vaisakh —"

SFX: The queue. Forty people crowded in a doorway.

HUKUM: "— recorded in the name of Kesar, of Barkheri. Granddaughter of Mohari, widow."

SFX: Nothing.

JORAWAR: (from the side of the room, quietly) Hukum sahib. That is not a finding of title.

HUKUM: No, sahib. It is a date. It is a date in a government book, and it is Tuesday, and your full moon is Thursday, and I have found in thirty-one years that a date beats a finding nine times in ten.

SFX: The register turned around on the desk.

HUKUM: Column nine. Persons present at registration.

JORAWAR: I am not a party to this.

HUKUM: You are standing in the room. Column nine does not say party, it says present. Every person present signs, or the entry is defective, and if the entry is defective I cannot take your bundle either — today, or Wednesday, or ever, because Thursday is Thursday.

SFX: Nothing. Very long. A ceiling fan with a tick in it.

HUKUM: Sahib. The pen is by your hand.

SFX: A pen picked up. A signature. One line of ink, in a government book, in front of forty people in a doorway — Jorawar signing, as witness, under the words "in the name of Kesar, of Barkheri, granddaughter of Mohari, widow."
