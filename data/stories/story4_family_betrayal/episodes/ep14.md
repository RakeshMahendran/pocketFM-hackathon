# EPISODE 14 — "The First Line"

SFX: A brittle sheet of 1968 paper going into a cloth bag. A drawstring pulled. Rain in a bucket, four seconds apart, stopping.

GIRDHARI: Then be at the front. Not the side. The front, where they put you last year.

KESAR: Kaka. If I'm wrong about the date —

GIRDHARI: You are not wrong about the date. I have read dates for forty years and there is nothing else in this world I am sure of.

SFX: Cut. Full moon of Vaisakh. Four in the morning, and the fair.

SFX: Cartwheels. Hawkers. A loudspeaker being tested. Two thousand people. Exactly the sound of episode one, and the listener knows it.

SFX: A rope through an iron ring. One stroke. The bell — iron, cracked at the lip — out over the fields, for a long time.

SFX: Two thousand people. Nobody says anything at all.

NARRATOR: One year. The same courtyard, the same queue, the same knife on the same nail by the same board. She rang it at four, and nobody came to take her off the rope, and she would tell you afterwards that this was the worst part, because there is nothing in the world lonelier than not being stopped.

SFX: Benches. The committee sitting. A microphone tapped twice.

JORAWAR: Louder? Can the back hear? Good. Girdhari. Read the affidavit.

SFX: Paper. An old man's reading voice, forty years old.

GIRDHARI: "Affidavit. I, Tejmal, son of Bhairon Singh, aged fifty-eight — that in the month of Vaisakh, in the year nineteen sixty-eight, in the residence of Mohari, widow, in my presence, the said Mohari did orally and of her own will surrender the said nine days —"

SFX: Two thousand people.

JORAWAR: Thank you. Tejmal. Stand up so the back can see you. Do you affirm it?

SFX: Nothing. Very long. A hawker somewhere who has not been told to stop.

TEJMAL: (and he is nineteen years and thirty seconds getting to this) ...I affirm it.

SFX: The crowd.

JORAWAR: Then the nine days stand in the block, and the re-cut is sealed, and Girdhari will read the new roll and we can all go and eat. Girdhari.

KESAR: The eleventh of January.

SFX: Nothing.

JORAWAR: Sit down, child.

KESAR: The deed is dated the eleventh of January, nineteen sixty-eight. Sahib swears she gave them up in Vaisakh. Vaisakh is April.

SFX: Two thousand people working out a sum.

KESAR: The paper that sold them was written and thumb-marked three months before he says she opened her mouth. One of those two things is a lie and both of them are his.

SFX: The crowd.

JORAWAR: Phoolchand vakil is eighty-one and put the wrong month —

PHOOLCHAND: (from the front row, and the microphone catches it) Phoolchand vakil is here.

SFX: Two thousand people turning.

PHOOLCHAND: Thirty-one steps on Tuesday and a bullock cart this morning at two. Hand me that. No — the original, not the copy. Thank you.

SFX: Brittle paper. A microphone held to it.

PHOOLCHAND: Eleventh of January, nineteen sixty-eight. My hand, my ink, my table. I identified her. She could not read a bus timetable and she put her mark on it in front of me, and I was twenty-four and I was proud of the lettering.

SFX: Nothing.

PHOOLCHAND: And it says sold. For eleven hundred rupees. And a turn of worship cannot be sold. Not then, not now, not for eleven hundred and not for eleven lakh. I have known that since nineteen seventy-four and I drew one hundred and nine papers for this house afterwards and said nothing, and the girl asked me a question in April and I have not slept since, and I would like the whole fair to hear an old man say that he was a coward for fifty-one years.

SFX: Two thousand people.

NARRATOR: There it is. Remember it, because it is the only apology in this story that nobody asked for.

HUKUM: (a new voice, dry, from the side, and the crowd parts for a government shirt) Sub-registrar, Jhilan. I have not come to say anything about anybody's title.

SFX: An enormous ledger set down on a bench.

HUKUM: I have come to say four things and then I am going back, because I have a queue. One. Book four, serial two hundred and six, Tuesday: nine days of this circuit recorded in the name of Kesar of Barkheri, granddaughter of Mohari, widow. Two. Column nine of that entry, persons present, is signed by Jorawar sahib in his own hand and the whole corridor watched him do it.

SFX: Two thousand people.

HUKUM: Three. The bundle of fifty-one instruments this committee has been re-cutting the year with — I have been through every sheet. Not one of them has ever been presented for registration. A turn of worship is immovable property. An unregistered transfer of immovable property cannot be received in evidence by any court in this country. Not weak. Not doubtful. Cannot be received.

SFX: The crowd, and this time it does not settle.

MAN AT BACK: What about my father's days?

HUKUM: Four. That. Every man here holding days by inheritance holds them. Inheritance is not a transfer and needs no paper. Only the ones that were bought are worth nothing — and gentlemen, they were always worth nothing, from the day the money changed hands.

SFX: Nothing.

SITARA: (clear, from the middle of the crowd) Four lakh sixty thousand rupees.

SFX: Two thousand people finding her.

SITARA: Sitara, of Jhilan. Eleven days of this circuit, bought over two years, cash, counted on that man's table in that man's office, by that man's hands. No receipt. And I want it back this morning, in front of everybody, out of today's fair collection, because I have just been told in public that I bought weather.

KANHAIYA: (weakly) Sold is sold —

SITARA: Shut up, Kanhaiya.

SFX: Nothing. Very long. A microphone with nobody speaking into it.

JORAWAR: (and for the first time in fourteen episodes his voice is not level) Rukmani. Close the day-book.

RUKMANI: (and she says it the way she reads numbers, which is why nobody can argue with it) I write what happens, sahib. That's the whole of my job and you gave it to me.

SFX: A pen.

RUKMANI: Box to be counted here. On the bench. In front of the fair, where I can see it, which is the only thing I have asked for in four years.

SFX: A padlock. Coins onto a steel tray. And it goes on, and on.

NARRATOR: They counted it in the open. Eleven thousand and forty rupees before nine o'clock. It took Rukmani nineteen minutes and nobody left.

SFX: The tray, set down.

TEJMAL: (and this is the sound of a man standing up when he could have sat still) Girdhari. Take the affidavit off the record.

GIRDHARI: Sahib, you affirmed it forty minutes ago in front of —

TEJMAL: I know what I did in front of two thousand people and I'm going to undo it in front of the same two thousand. Take it off. I was eleven years old in sixty-eight and I was not in that room and nobody was, and my brother wrote the month for me because he knew I'd never checked a calendar in my life.

SFX: The crowd.

JORAWAR: Tejmal —

TEJMAL: And the other paper. Say the other paper or I will.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

TEJMAL: Fourteenth of Magh, nineteen years ago. There is a birth entry registered at Jhilan with my name in the father's column, and it is false, and I never touched that woman, and I did not know it existed until Wednesday night when he showed it to me over a plate of fish and told me what it would cost to keep it quiet.

SFX: Two thousand people. Somebody's mother saying God's name out loud.

TEJMAL: Bhanwari put her thumb on it because she was twenty-eight and widowed and could not read and a man with a jeep told her the child needed a father's column or she'd be nobody. And it was not for the child. It was to take out the one door between that girl and her grandmother's nine days. He said that to me in his own house on Wednesday. He was proud of it. It cost him forty rupees.

SFX: The crowd, very loud now, and then a woman's voice cutting it.

NARMADA: In that courtyard.

SFX: Two thousand people turning.

NARMADA: I have waited fifty-seven years to say a sentence in this courtyard and I am going to say it and then I am going to sit down. Mohari, widow, of this village, told me for eleven years that her days were lent for two seasons and she stopped talking in nineteen seventy-nine and she died in the room that girl sleeps in. And every one of you stood here and watched it and said nothing. I am not asking Jorawar for an apology. I am asking you. All of you. In this courtyard.

SFX: Nothing. Very long. Two thousand people, and a bell that nobody is ringing.

SFX: One pair of feet. Then a lot of them.

NARRATOR: About four hundred of them stood up. Not all of them. Never all of them. Four hundred was enough.

SFX: A microphone.

JORAWAR: (flat) Girdhari. Read the roll.

GIRDHARI: Which roll, sahib?

SFX: Nothing.

JORAWAR: ...The one the sub-registrar will take.

SFX: A cloth-bound register. Pages.

GIRDHARI: (the reading voice, and it does not shake) Barkheri circuit. Five hundred and forty days. Held by inheritance and by nothing else. Jorawar, son of Bhairon Singh — eleven days.

SFX: Two thousand people.

GIRDHARI: Tejmal, son of Bhairon Singh — sixty-one days, scattered, as his father left them. Sitara of Jhilan — no days, and four lakh sixty thousand rupees payable this morning from the box. The Jhilan house — nine days, by inheritance. Rukmani — seven, by inheritance, and the box is counted on the bench from today. Chhotu, son of nobody in particular —

CHHOTU: (a squeak) Sahib?

GIRDHARI: — the second bell, every morning of the year, with your name written against it, because you have rung it for two years and nobody has ever written your name against anything.

SFX: A boy crying, badly, in front of two thousand people.

GIRDHARI: And twenty-ninth of Chaitra to seventh Vaisakh. Nine days. Held whole, not by turn.

SFX: Nothing.

GIRDHARI: Sahib. It's your circuit. You read that line.

SFX: A microphone. A very long silence. The fair queue, exactly where it stood a year ago.

JORAWAR: (and he says it because there is no version of this morning where he does not) Twenty-ninth of Chaitra to the seventh of Vaisakh. Nine days. Kesar's. And the box on those mornings is hers.

SFX: Two thousand people, and it goes on a long time.

SFX: A board lifted off two nails. A coconut knife picked up off a stone — and set back down.

RUKMANI: Not the knife. Chalk. It's a board, not a stone.

SFX: Chalk on wood.

SFX: Chalk on wood, in a courtyard where two thousand people are watching a board being rewritten, and the first line reads: Kesar — nine days — by right of Mohari.
