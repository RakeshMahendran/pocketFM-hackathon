# EPISODE 8 — "The Same Hand on Both Sides"

SFX: A stairwell. Concrete. A compressor hissing below, over and over. A girl coming down it two steps at a time, and stopping.

SITARA: Thursday?

KESAR: Thursday.

SITARA: Ji, today is Monday.

KESAR: I know what day it is. I know exactly what day it is. There are twenty-two of them.

SFX: A car door. Town car. Air conditioning starting up, which is a sound Kesar has now heard four times in her life.

SITARA: Then between now and Thursday you will do something for me and I will do something for you. Get in.

SFX: Cut. A narrow lane in Jhilan town. A hand-pump. A radio. A woman shelling something into a steel plate, steadily, and not stopping when the door opens.

NARMADA: No.

SITARA: Narmada ji, I haven't —

NARMADA: No. Out.

SITARA: This is —

NARMADA: I know what it is. It's Mohari's face standing in my door after nineteen years. I've been waiting for it since Tuesday, the whole town has been talking about the girl who made Girdhari read out sixty-eight. Sit down.

SFX: Two women sitting on a floor. Steel plate set aside.

KESAR: Who are you?

NARMADA: I married her husband's younger brother. So I am nothing to you and I was everything to her, which is how it works in this country. Forty-one years I knew that woman.

KESAR: Nobody in Barkheri will say her name.

NARMADA: (flat) In that courtyard. That's where they wouldn't say it. In that courtyard, with two hundred people standing there, they took nine days off a widow and nobody said one word, and I have wanted one thing for fifty-seven years, and it is not money.

KESAR: What is it?

NARMADA: Somebody standing in that courtyard saying they were sorry. In that courtyard. Not in a court in Jhilan. In that courtyard.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

KESAR: Did she sell them?

NARMADA: (immediately) She could not read.

KESAR: That isn't what I asked.

NARMADA: It's the whole answer, girl. She could not read a bus timetable. In sixty-eight, Bhairon Singh's men came to this lane with a vakil's clerk and a stamp pad, and she put her thumb on something, and afterwards she told me — she told me, in this room, sitting where you're sitting — that it was for two seasons. Two seasons and the days come back.

SFX: Nothing.

NARMADA: Two seasons. She said it to me for eleven years, until she stopped saying anything.

NARRATOR: Remember that she said it. Remember also that she is dead, and that nobody wrote it down, and that a thing nobody wrote down is, in Jhilan, a thing that did not happen.

SFX: Cut. Barkheri. Night. A lane. Fast footsteps and a boy's voice, cracking.

CHHOTU: Didi! Didi — second bell — didi, sahib's driver went to Jhilan this evening. In the jeep. With a bag.

KESAR: Which way in Jhilan?

CHHOTU: The tyre shop road.

SFX: Nothing.

KESAR: Chhotu. What time on Thursday does the first bus leave?

CHHOTU: Five past five.

KESAR: I'll be on the one before it.

SFX: Cut. Wednesday. Not Thursday. Wednesday, four in the morning, before the bell. A bus. Empty. A radio not yet on.

NARRATOR: She did not go on Thursday. She was a servant for fifteen years and a servant learns exactly one strategy: be there before you are expected, because that is the only advantage that costs nothing.

SFX: The stairwell. The compressor is not running. It is too early for the compressor, and the silence where the compressor should be is enormous.

SFX: The rattling nameplate door. Knocking. Knocking. Knocking.

PHOOLCHAND: (through the door) Thursday.

KESAR: Sahib. Was the jeep here?

SFX: Nothing.

KESAR: Vakil sahib. Was Jorawar's jeep here yesterday evening?

SFX: A bolt.

SFX: A door opening on a very old man who has not slept.

PHOOLCHAND: The jeep was here.

KESAR: What did he offer you?

PHOOLCHAND: Offer. He didn't offer. He complimented my almirah. He said — he said, "what a good almirah, vakil sahib, Godrej, is it, nineteen sixty?" and then he asked after my son's posting in Kota. That's all. That's all he said. He drank one glass of water and he went.

SFX: An old man sitting down.

PHOOLCHAND: Fifty-seven years I have kept everything. Everything. I keep other men's dirt in that cupboard the way a doctor keeps other men's blood. And in fifty-seven years nobody has ever asked me about my son's posting.

KESAR: Sahib —

PHOOLCHAND: My son's posting. My son's posting. Sit down. Sit on the floor, the chair's got papers.

SFX: The key from around a neck. A steel almirah, and this time it opens all the way, and the sound goes on for a long time — a whole wall of tied bundles, cloth, string.

PHOOLCHAND: Sixty-eight is the fourth shelf. Red cloth is agreements. Blue is sales. I tie sales in blue because in sixty-two I put a sale in the red bundle and a man lost a well.

SFX: String being untied. Old string. It takes longer than it should.

SFX: Paper. One sheet. Thin, brittle, unfolding.

PHOOLCHAND: There.

KESAR: I can't read this hand.

PHOOLCHAND: No. Nobody can. That was rather the point of the hand.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

PHOOLCHAND: Give it here. Bring the lamp.

SFX: A lamp. A very old man breathing through his nose.

PHOOLCHAND: (reading, and his voice at twenty-four is somewhere underneath his voice at eighty-one) "Deed of transfer. Executed this day, the eleventh of January, one thousand nine hundred and sixty-eight, at Jhilan. By Mohari, widow, of Barkheri, hereinafter the transferor, who being unlettered has affixed her mark..."

KESAR: Two seasons. Look for two seasons. Is there anything about two seasons?

PHOOLCHAND: (reading on) "...transfers absolutely, and not for any term..."

SFX: Nothing.

PHOOLCHAND: "...not for any term, season or period, the whole of her nine days of the circuit of the shrine of Baba Ghanteshwar aforesaid..."

KESAR: Keep going.

PHOOLCHAND: "...and the transferor doth acknowledge receipt of the consideration hereinafter mentioned."

KESAR: What is the word, sahib.

PHOOLCHAND: The word is sold.

KESAR: And the money.

PHOOLCHAND: Rupees eleven hundred only.

SFX: A girl not moving.

NARRATOR: She had known both of those things for eleven days. Hearing them off the paper was still the worst sound she had ever heard, and she would not understand for another hour why the old man had gone quiet.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

PHOOLCHAND: Girl.

KESAR: Sahib.

PHOOLCHAND: Turn it over.

SFX: One sheet of brittle paper, turned.

PHOOLCHAND: What do you see on the back?

KESAR: Nothing. It's blank.

PHOOLCHAND: Yes.

SFX: The old man's chair, as he sits back in it.

PHOOLCHAND: Sold, for eleven hundred rupees. And on the back of it, no sub-registrar's endorsement, no serial number, no book and page, no fee stamp, no date of presentation. Nothing. In fifty-seven years that deed has never once been inside a government building.
