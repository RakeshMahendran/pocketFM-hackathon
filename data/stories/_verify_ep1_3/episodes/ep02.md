# Episode 2 — "Right To The Rupee"

SFX: A spring balance swinging on its hook and hitting the shutter every time it swings. A tin scoop going down into grain. Forty pairs of sandals on packed earth, shuffling one step forward.

BIRJU: Amma. Give me your hand.

PHULWA: Both my hands are holding the bag.

BIRJU: Give me the right one. I want to look at the thumb.

PHULWA: Birju, the queue is moving.

BIRJU: The queue is not moving, it has not moved since the shutter went up. What did you put it on?

PHULWA: A paper.

BIRJU: What kind of paper?

PHULWA: Beta, have you eaten?

BIRJU: Amma.

PHULWA: You have not eaten. Two days now, since the yard. You will stand in this queue and you will take the flour and you will eat.

SFX: The scoop going into the grain again. The shutter rattling up another notch.

SANICHARI: Leave her, munshi. She has told you the true answer. It was a paper. Nobody who has put a thumb on one has ever been told which one it was — that is the whole of the arrangement, beta, and you have been sitting on top of it for two months with a pen in your hand.

BIRJU: I did not know what was in that book.

SANICHARI: You know now. You are the only one of us who knows now. (pause) What will you do with knowing?

BIRJU: I will check it.

SANICHARI: (a laugh with nothing in it) Check it.

SFX: Somebody counting coins, badly, dropping one.

DEVLAL: (from inside the shop) Next. Name and house.

VOICE IN QUEUE: Sanichari's house.

DEVLAL: Five kilo. Written against the house.

SANICHARI: Written where?

DEVLAL: Where everything is written. Move.

SFX: A ledger — the same board covers, the same dry crack — being opened on a counter.

BIRJU: (low) Amma. That is my book. He has my book at the ration counter.

PHULWA: It is his father's book.

BIRJU: The flour goes in it. The flour goes in the same book as the bricks.

PHULWA: Where else would it go.

SUDAMA: (loud, from behind) Sudama. Cash. I am paying cash, I am not going in the book, count it in front of everybody — one hundred, two hundred, three, four, five. Five hundred rupees for five kilo of flour at a shop where a card would have got it for forty.

DEVLAL: Then take your five kilo and stop performing.

SUDAMA: I am not performing, I am buying. There is a difference and you have never once been on the wrong side of it. In Punjab a man puts money down and takes a thing away and it is finished. Finished, that same minute. Here nothing is ever finished.

VOICE IN QUEUE: Sit down, Sudama.

SUDAMA: I will not sit down. I am going to buy my mother out of that book before Baisakh and then I am going to stand at this crossing and be called by my own name.

DEVLAL: Your mother's house is nineteen thousand six hundred. Come with that and I will hear you.

SFX: The shutter. A silence with the balance still swinging in it.

CHAMELI: Birju.

BIRJU: Chameli. You should not be at this crossing.

CHAMELI: I know what your house is.

BIRJU: Everyone in the yard was there.

CHAMELI: I was not in the yard and I still know. That is what I came to tell you. It has come across the fields to our side by itself, in two days, without legs.

BIRJU: Who said it to you?

CHAMELI: My father said it to me. Last night, with the lamp out, in the voice he uses for the buffalo being sold. He said the marriage is not going to happen and he said the reason and the reason was a number.

BIRJU: Then let him say it to me.

CHAMELI: Birju—

BIRJU: I have read every house in bharwara out loud in front of forty people. Let one man say one number to my face. That is not very much to ask of a man who was going to be my father.

NARRATOR: He had wanted, since he was fifteen, three things. A letter with his name printed on it. A chair that was his. And her. He had been given the chair.

SFX: The crossing. A bus going through without stopping, and its dust arriving after it.

CHAMELI'S FATHER: I have been looking for you at the kiln and they said you were at the shop, and I thought, good, better, let it be at the shop.

CHAMELI: Baba, not here.

CHAMELI'S FATHER: Here is where it was arranged, girl. Two years ago, at this shutter, in front of these same people. It will be unarranged in the same place or it will not be honest.

BIRJU: Say it, then.

CHAMELI'S FATHER: I am a tenant. I hold four bighas that are not mine and I hold them one year at a time. I have nothing to leave her except a name that is not in anybody's book. That is all I have got. That is the entire property.

BIRJU: I keep the book. I am the munshi. I have a chair.

CHAMELI'S FATHER: You keep it. You are not out of it. (to the queue) Am I saying anything here that anybody is going to contradict?

SFX: Nobody contradicting anything.

CHAMELI'S FATHER: If she marries you, her children are born inside a number. I am not going to be the man who did that to her because he wanted the wedding he had already told people about.

CHAMELI: Baba. He read it out himself. He did not know.

CHAMELI'S FATHER: I know he did not know. That is not better, beti. That is worse. A man who did not know is a man they can use twice.

BIRJU: (steady, too steady) Say the number.

CHAMELI'S FATHER: Do not make me.

BIRJU: You have called it off in front of the crossing. Finish it properly. Say the number.

CHAMELI'S FATHER: Eighty-nine thousand, four hundred.

SFX: The balance, swinging, hitting the shutter.

BIRJU: Who told you that?

CHAMELI'S FATHER: Nobody at your kiln has told me anything.
