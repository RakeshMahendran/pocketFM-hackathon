# Episode 1 — "Call Your Own House"

SFX: Bricks going down into stacks, four at a time, close enough to hear the grit come off them. Behind that, forty people standing in a yard and not one of them talking.

DEVLAL: By house. Not by family. By house.

SANICHARI: In the year the well fell in, we were three houses.

DEVLAL: In the year the well fell in there was no book. You are one house. Move up.

SFX: A wooden chair dragged over dry ground. One leg is short and it knocks, twice, when it stops.

DHANIRAM: Birju. Beta. Sit.

BIRJU: Sir, I can stand. I have stood for two months.

DHANIRAM: The munshi sits. That is what a chair is for.

BIRJU: Sir — before that. The letter. At Holi you said there would be a letter with my name typed on it.

DHANIRAM: I said there would be. Did I say when?

BIRJU: No, sir.

DHANIRAM: Then nothing has gone wrong, has it. Sit. Take the book.

SFX: A cloth-bound ledger set down across a knee. Board covers. The spine cracks open like a dry stick.

BIRJU: (turning pages) Sir, this is blank. Page one is blank. All of it is blank.

DHANIRAM: Page one is where a man starts. You will write what I say and read what you write. Loud, beta. Let them hear their own numbers in their own boy's voice instead of in my son's. They have been asking for that for nine years. Today I am giving it to them.

SFX: Forty people going quiet at the same moment. A crow, somewhere above the chimney.

DEVLAL: First house. Sanichari.

SANICHARI: Ji.

BIRJU: Sanichari's house. Carry-forward — sixty-three thousand, two hundred.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

SANICHARI: Say it again, beta. Slowly, so I can put it somewhere.

BIRJU: Sixty-three thousand, two hundred.

SANICHARI: In the year my man's mother was burned we took four thousand eight hundred rupees. That is the whole of it. Four thousand eight hundred, and I have moulded here twenty-two seasons since.

DEVLAL: That is what is written.

SANICHARI: Then read the other paper. There is a paper that says a person is finished with a place. I was promised one twenty-two years ago, in this yard, standing about where that chair is.

DEVLAL: There is no such paper in this book.

SANICHARI: (to the whole yard) Twenty-two years.

VOICE IN QUEUE: Let the boy read.

NARRATOR: Pay-out at teghra more happens once a season, in the open, where everyone can watch it happen to everyone else. That is the point of the open yard. Remember that.

DEVLAL: Fines next. Read them where they fall.

BIRJU: The house at the end of the lane. Carry-forward, fourteen thousand. And against it — this cannot be right, sir — a fine of ten thousand.

DEVLAL: A hen of theirs walked over the drying bricks in Phagun. Two hundred bricks spoiled. Ten thousand.

SFX: A woman laughing, once, in the wrong place, and stopping.

LAKHAN: Ten thousand rupees for a hen.

DEVLAL: Ten thousand rupees for two hundred bricks. The hen is not fined. The house is.

LAKHAN: Then where is the cash? It is pay-out day. My hands are open. I am the fastest moulder in this yard and I want something put into them where people can see it go in.

DHANIRAM: Lakhan. Have I ever refused your house food?

LAKHAN: No.

DHANIRAM: A doctor? A roof in Sawan? The loan for your father's leg, the year the leg went bad?

LAKHAN: No.

DHANIRAM: Then why do you stand in front of your neighbours asking me for the one thing nobody here has ever needed. (softer) Beta. Next house.

SUDAMA: (from the edge, carrying) Sudama. Say it — Sudama, of bharwara. I am not in the queue. I am standing here because I like where I am standing.

DEVLAL: Then like it further back.

SUDAMA: There was a raid at Chandwa on the seventeenth. A magistrate, two buses, and a hundred and thirty-seven people walked out through the gate in one afternoon. Forty men. Forty-six women. Fifty-one children. They counted them onto the buses out loud, like bricks.

SFX: The yard moving all at once — feet, cloth, somebody's tin bowl going over.

DHANIRAM: The seventeenth was last Tuesday, and Chandwa is four hours from here, and you have come home with a belt on you and opinions in your mouth. (to the yard) Nobody is going anywhere. It is pay-out day. Beta, keep reading.

NARRATOR: He kept reading. Eleven houses, and against every one of them a figure that had gone up since the season before, and after every figure a silence with a shape to it.

DEVLAL: That is the lane finished.

DHANIRAM: Not finished. One house has not been called.

BIRJU: Sir, that is my — sir, my house is not a house of the kiln in that way, my mother only moulds—

DHANIRAM: Every house in this yard has stood here and heard itself. Yours has been standing here too. Call your own house.

SFX: Pages turning. Slower than they need to be turned.

PHULWA: (from the back, quiet) Birju. Have you eaten?

BIRJU: Amma. Not now.

PHULWA: You have not eaten since it was dark.

DHANIRAM: Let him work, mai-ji. He is very good at it.

SFX: One page. Then a young man trying to breathe normally in front of forty people, and being heard doing it.

NARRATOR: He was the first person born in bharwara to finish class ten. Two months he had kept this book, and in two months nobody had ever once asked him to read the P's.

BIRJU: Phulwa's house. Carry-forward — eighty-nine thousand, four hundred.

SFX: Forty people not moving at all.

SANICHARI: Say it again, beta.

BIRJU: (smaller) Eighty-nine thousand, four hundred.

VOICE IN QUEUE: That is more than the widow.

LAKHAN: Say the whole thing, Birju. You have made everybody else say the whole thing.

BIRJU: There is a mark above it. On the line above the figure there is a thumb mark.

DEVLAL: Then it was agreed. That is what a thumb is.

BIRJU: (nearly nothing) It is my mother's thumb.

DHANIRAM: Beta. Read the date beside it, and then we will all go and eat.

NARRATOR: The date beside his mother's thumb is nine years before Birju learned to hold a pen.
