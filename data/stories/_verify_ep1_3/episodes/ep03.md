# Episode 3 — "Four Hundred And Eleven"

SFX: Night at the kiln. The chimney pulling air, steady, like something large asleep. A chain being shaken against a metal gate, hard, over and over, by somebody who is not going to get tired of it.

TIRATH: (outside the gate) Open it. Open it, open it. I am not asking the gate, I am asking the men behind the gate. Forty of you sleeping in that shed and every one of you knows my voice.

WATCHMAN: Go home, Tirath.

TIRATH: Home. He says home. Nine years I sat inside this gate and now a man on eight thousand a month tells me where my home is.

WATCHMAN: You are drunk.

TIRATH: I am drunk and I am owed four months' pay, and of those two facts only one of them is going to be different in the morning.

SFX: The chain again. Bodies moving in the shed — men sitting up on their charpoys, cloth, someone spitting.

DEVLAL: (coming out) Leave the chain.

TIRATH: Ah. The son. The son comes out, and see how he comes out — fully dressed at midnight, chappals already on. You were awake. You have been lying awake in your father's house waiting to see if I would come, and I have come, and here we are, and I am not going to be the one who is embarrassed.

DEVLAL: You were paid off in Aghan.

TIRATH: I was sacked in Aghan. Those are two words and they are not the same word. Sacked with four months standing against my name — Bhado, Kuar, Katik, Aghan — and I stood in that office and I said, Devlal babu, at least give me a paper, and you said—

DEVLAL: I said go home.

TIRATH: You said go home! (to the shed, loudly) You hear it? He remembers what he said. He has been carrying it around for four months, same as me, only his side of it weighs nothing.

SFX: The shed. Nobody speaking, but the sound of forty people who have all decided to be awake.

DEVLAL: There is nothing standing against your name. I have looked.

TIRATH: You have looked. In what?

DEVLAL: In the book.

TIRATH: (delighted, terrible) In the book. In the book. Which book, Devlal babu? Say the name of it. Say which one you looked in.

DEVLAL: Get him off the gate.

SFX: The chain coming loose. Feet. The gate swinging in against its own weight.

LAKHAN: Nobody is putting hands on him. He is fifty years old and he is standing in the road.

DEVLAL: This is not your business, moulder.

LAKHAN: It is my road.

TIRATH: (finding him) Lakhan. Lakhan, brother. And the widow — Sanichari, mai-ji, you are up. Of course you are up, you have not slept a whole night since the well fell in. And — there. There he is. There is the new one.

BIRJU: I am not part of this.

TIRATH: He says he is not part of this! Beta, come to the gate. Come to the gate where the lamp is, let them see the two of us in the same light, the one who sat in the chair and the one who is sitting in it now. Come.

BIRJU: I have work in the morning.

TIRATH: Nine years, I had work in the morning. Do you know what I got at the end of nine years of work in the morning? I got a sentence. Go home. (beat) How much are they paying you?

BIRJU: That is not—

TIRATH: How much.

BIRJU: Four thousand two hundred.

SFX: A sound going through the shed. Somebody says it again further back — four thousand two hundred.

TIRATH: Four thousand two hundred. They were paying me nine when I started, and I started when you were in class four. Ask yourself why the price of the chair went down.

DHANIRAM: (arriving, unhurried) Tirath.

SFX: Everything getting quieter, immediately, without anybody being told to.

TIRATH: Malik.

DHANIRAM: You have woken the shed and they mould at four. Whatever you have come for, ask me for it now and let them sleep.

TIRATH: Four months. Bhado, Kuar, Katik, Aghan. Twenty-eight thousand and some.

DHANIRAM: And how would we know that, beta.

TIRATH: Because I wrote it. I wrote it myself, in my own hand, on the last page I ever wrote on.

DHANIRAM: And where is that page?

SFX: Nothing. Long.

DHANIRAM: (gently) You see the difficulty. A man says a number and there is nothing to check it against, and then it is only a man saying a number in a road at night. I have known you since your father's time. Come to the shop tomorrow, eat, and we will look at what there is to look at. (to the yard) He was our munshi for nine years and he is not a thief, whatever anybody has been saying. I want that heard.

SFX: The shed hearing it. Not one person saying anything.

TIRATH: (after a moment, ruined) Whatever anybody has been saying.

DHANIRAM: Go home, beta.

SFX: Sandals turning on grit. The gate being pulled to. The chain going back on, link by link, very slowly, so that everybody has to listen to all of it.

TIRATH: (close, suddenly, at Birju's shoulder) Give me your hand.

BIRJU: Tirath-ji—

TIRATH: Your hand, munshi-ji, I am not going to hurt it, it is worth more than mine now. (pressing) There. Take it. Do not open it here. Do not open it in the office. Do not open it anywhere he can hear paper.

BIRJU: What is it?

TIRATH: It is a question. When you have opened it you will have a question and there will be exactly one man alive who can answer it and I will be at the crossing, sober, at eight o'clock, and you will come to me, and for the first time in four months somebody will be careful with me.

SFX: A man walking away in the dark, on the road, not fast.

NARRATOR: He carried it in his shirt for two hours and did not open it, and he would say later that those were the last two hours of the part of his life he could still call a job.

SFX: A lamp turned up in a room with one window. Paper unfolding — old paper, softened at the crease, coming open in three panels.

LAKHAN: What did he give you?

BIRJU: A page.

LAKHAN: From what?

BIRJU: Same hand as mine. Same ruling. Same columns — house, days, deduction, carry-forward. Nine entries. This one has been done properly, Lakhan, this is a clean hand, this is better work than I do.

LAKHAN: Is our house on it?

BIRJU: I am looking at the top. There is a number written at the top corner. That is not a rupee figure. That is—

LAKHAN: That is what?

BIRJU: That is the page number.

SFX: Paper, turned to the lamp.

BIRJU: Four hundred and eleven.

LAKHAN: So?

BIRJU: My book starts at page one.
