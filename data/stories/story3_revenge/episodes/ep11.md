# Episode 11 — "Line Nine"

SFX: A cloth money-belt unbuckled and dropped on a table. It lands heavy and does not slide.

SUDAMA: Count it.

BIRJU: Sudama —

SUDAMA: Count it, munshi. Thirty-nine thousand six hundred. In Punjab they pay weekly and they pay in the hand and there is no book, there is a man with a phone and he sends it and you see it arrive. Eleven months. Count it.

SFX: Notes. Somebody counting fast in bundles, then stopping.

BIRJU: Sit down.

SUDAMA: I don't want to sit, I want my mother's line closed. Today. In front of you, with the paper. And then I want to walk to the crossing and I want that boy with the boots to say my name, because for four years he has called me "Punjab" like it is a joke, and it is not a joke, it is a place, and it is a place where they weigh things in front of you.

BIRJU: What is your mother's figure?

SUDAMA: Forty thousand. Roughly. It was forty in Chait.

BIRJU: (nothing)

SUDAMA: Munshi. It was forty in Chait.

BIRJU: It is seventy-one thousand, two hundred.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

SUDAMA: That's not — I sent money. I sent money from Punjab, through the man at the crossing, twice, eight thousand each time —

BIRJU: I know. I have it in front of me. Both of them are in here, on this page, as receipts of the shop. The shop received sixteen thousand rupees of yours.

SUDAMA: Then take it off.

BIRJU: Off what?

SUDAMA: Off the number!

SFX: Pages. A boy turning a book round on the table so another man can look at it.

BIRJU: Look at the page. Not the figures — the lines. Name. Date. Head. Amount. Balance carried. Five columns. Sudama, show me the one I am supposed to write your sixteen thousand into.

SFX: Nothing at all.

SUDAMA: Rule one.

BIRJU: I can rule one. Anybody can rule one. And then in Baisakh the licensee's son opens this book and says, what is this column, who ruled this, and it is one hand in that book and everybody knows whose hand.

SUDAMA: So the money —

BIRJU: The money went into the shop. And the shop is in the same book. And the same book has no —

SUDAMA: SAY IT.

BIRJU: (quiet) There is nowhere to write that you paid. There never has been. Not since 1974.

SFX: A money-belt being picked up off a table. Slowly, then all at once.

SUDAMA: (at the door) You know what the worst of it is, munshi-ji? I believed you were the good one. All of Punjab knew it. I told men in Ludhiana that in my hamlet the boy who reads is one of ours.

SFX: A door. Feet on gravel, fast.

NARRATOR: Thirty-nine thousand six hundred rupees left teghra more in a cloth belt that afternoon. Nobody has ever asked Birju what he did for the twenty minutes after it went, and the answer is nothing, he did nothing, he sat with his hands flat on the table, which by now was a thing he did.

SFX: The yard. Late. Bricks stopping. And then a sound that does not belong at a kiln — a woman's voice, carrying, addressing a number of people at once.

HEMWATI: Thirty-one. There were thirty-one of us at Purnibathan when they came and thirty-one of us walked out and I have thirty-one names on this and I want the thirty-second here.

DEVLAL: You are on private land.

HEMWATI: I am on the track. The track is the panchayat's. Send for your munshi, he has been to school.

SFX: Ninety steps of gravel.

BIRJU: Sister — you should not come here in the evening. There is no bus after seven.

HEMWATI: I walked from Purnibathan. It is nine kilometres and I have done it four times this month and twice with a child on my hip, so do not tell me about the bus. You are the one who struck nine dead men off a book in front of four hundred people.

BIRJU: (carefully) The arithmetic was wrong.

HEMWATI: I do not care why you did it. I care that it is the only time it has happened in this district in my life. Sign this.

SFX: Paper unfolded. Thin paper, folded and unfolded many times.

BIRJU: What is it?

HEMWATI: A complaint. Thirty-one households. Names, kiln, years worked, and what each of them is told they owe. It goes to the district on the ninth. It needs one person who has seen the books to say the figures are the figures. That is all. Not an opinion. A signature saying: I have read them and they say this.

SFX: Nothing. The kiln.

BIRJU: No.

HEMWATI: Say it louder, there are people behind you.

BIRJU: No. I will not sign it.

HEMWATI: Why?

BIRJU: Because on the ninth I will be forty pages into a reconciliation of nine volumes and if my name is on your paper on the ninth the almirah is locked on the tenth and there is no reconciliation, ever, for anybody. Because the only reason I can reach the 1974 book at all is that he believes I am building him a total. And because —

HEMWATI: Say the last one. The first two were good. Say the last one.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

BIRJU: Because I have a letter with my name printed on it.

HEMWATI: (not unkindly at all) There it is.

BIRJU: Sister —

HEMWATI: No, I am glad you said it. Every man who has ever refused me has given me the first two reasons and gone home, and I have never once got the third one. (a pause) Do you know what happens on the ninth without your signature?

BIRJU: Tell me.

HEMWATI: The officer reads thirty-one figures with nobody behind them. He asks the kiln for its accounts. The kiln produces a beautiful book, ruled, headed, initialled, in one hand, with the reasons written out in words — because you did that, munshi, that is your work — and the officer says, this is the finest kiln book in the block, there is nothing adverse. And thirty-one households go back on Monday.

SFX: Nothing.

HEMWATI: I am not asking you to be brave. Brave is not a thing I have any use for. I am telling you that the book you are making is going to be used, and you do not get to choose by whom.

BIRJU: The ninth is too early. Give me until Chait.

HEMWATI: Chait.

BIRJU: I will have the total. Every chain, every page number. And then it is not thirty-one people's word, it is a document.

HEMWATI: And if he takes the book off you in Poos?

BIRJU: (nothing)

HEMWATI: Mm.

SFX: Thin paper being folded once and set down on a table.

BIRJU: Take it with you.

HEMWATI: I have four copies. This one lives here.

BIRJU: Sister —

HEMWATI: Read it before you decide what it is. That is not a request, that is the only thing I have ever asked anybody for. (walking) Nine kilometres. If somebody gives me tea at the crossing I will say something nice about this place in Purnibathan.

SFX: Footsteps going down the track. The kiln. A lamp being lit in an office.

SFX: Thin paper unfolded on a table, flattened under a palm.

BIRJU: (reading, low) Purnibathan. Teghra more. Bharwara hamlet. Household. Years. Amount stated by the site.

SFX: A finger going down a column. It stops.

BIRJU: (reading) Line nine.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

NARRATOR: Line nine is his mother's name, spelt correctly, with the hamlet, with the years — twenty-nine of them — and the figure right to the rupee. Hemwati has never been to bharwara. She has never met Phulwa. She could not have taken it out of the book, because the book has not left the almirah in six weeks and the key to the almirah is in Birju's pocket.

NARRATOR: And the handwriting on line nine is not his.
