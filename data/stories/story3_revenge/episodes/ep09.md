# Episode 9 — "There Is No Column"

SFX: A kerosene lamp turned up. The wick catching, then settling. Old paper clicking, one page every few seconds, for a long time.

NARRATOR: He copied for three nights. Not the whole of it — nobody could copy the whole of it — but the spine of it: entry one to entry sixty, and then every line in fifty-one years that carried the word he had begun to hate.

BIRJU: (low, to himself, writing) Carry-forward. Carry-forward. Carry-forward, Aghan. Carry-forward.

SFX: A dog outside, twice. The kiln, always.

PHULWA: (from the doorway) Have you eaten?

BIRJU: Amma, go and sleep.

PHULWA: There is dal from the evening. It is not much but it is warm still if you come now.

BIRJU: In a while.

PHULWA: In a while it will be cold and then you will eat it cold and then you will say the food in this house is cold.

SFX: A stove being lit anyway. A pot. The particular noise of somebody doing a thing they have been told not to do.

BIRJU: Amma. Sit down a minute.

PHULWA: I am doing the dal.

BIRJU: Sit down.

SFX: Somebody sitting on the floor, slowly, on a knee that does not like it.

BIRJU: In 2009. In Aghan, 2009. You put your thumb on a paper.

PHULWA: (nothing)

BIRJU: I know you did. I've seen it. Amma, I have seen the paper.

PHULWA: You were twelve.

BIRJU: I know how old I was.

PHULWA: Your father had been dead five months and there was the thirteenth-day feast still unpaid and the school wanted two hundred and forty rupees for the examination and you had come home and not asked me for it. That is what I remember. That you did not ask me.

BIRJU: What was on the paper?

PHULWA: A paper.

BIRJU: What did it say?

PHULWA: (simply) How would I know what it said, beta? You read it to me.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

SFX: The dal, boiling over. Nobody moving to take it off.

NARRATOR: He was twelve years old and Dhaniram had handed him a sheet and said, read it to your mother, you are the one who goes to school. He had read out the top line, which said RECEIPT, because that is the word that was printed at the top, and it was printed, and printed words are true. Then he had folded it and been proud of himself for four days.

BIRJU: Amma. I'm sorry.

PHULWA: Eat the dal.

BIRJU: Amma —

PHULWA: (and this is all she will say about it, ever) Eat the dal, Birju.

SFX: A door. A road at night. Somebody walking who does not want to be at home.

SFX: A different door — thin, badly hung — and a smell you can hear in a man's voice.

TIRATH: MUNSHI SAHIB. At my house. At my actual house. Wife! Wife, there is nobody to make tea, she left in Chait, never mind. Sit on the cot. The chair has a purpose and the purpose is not sitting.

BIRJU: Uncle. Eleven years you kept that book.

TIRATH: Eleven years and four months.

BIRJU: I want to ask you one thing and then I'll go.

TIRATH: Ask three. Nobody has asked me anything since Baisakh and I have started answering the goat.

BIRJU: When a house paid — when anybody, ever, in eleven years, brought money and put it on the table — where did you write it?

SFX: Nothing. A bottle set down on a floor without being picked up again.

TIRATH: (entirely sober, the way he was at the gate) Say that again.

BIRJU: Where does a payment go?

TIRATH: (a laugh that goes wrong halfway) He gave the boy two hundred pages and the boy is asking about payments.

BIRJU: Uncle. Answer it.

TIRATH: In eleven years and four months, munshi sahib, I never wrote one.

BIRJU: Not one?

TIRATH: Not one. Not because nobody ever paid — Sudama's mother paid, in the year of the storm, she came with eleven hundred rupees knotted in her sari and she put it on the table in front of me and she stood there while I wrote. And I wrote it. I wrote it in the margin. In pencil. Because there was nowhere else to write it, and by Chait the margin had been cut when they trimmed the binding.

BIRJU: You could have ruled a column.

TIRATH: (very gently) Yes. I could have ruled a column. So could you. You have ruled one hundred fine slips, I hear. Beautiful, they say. The best-looking fine slips this district has seen.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

TIRATH: I asked for my four months in front of two men and he told me to go home and rest. So I went and I rested, and here I am resting, and every man who used to be careful around me now steps over my legs at the crossing. That is what I got for eleven years of not ruling a column. Go on. Go and look.

SFX: A steel almirah. The lamp. Nine volumes on a table, all of them open at once.

NARRATOR: He went through them from the beginning. Volume one, 1974, entry one — five thousand rupees, purpose marriage, thumbprint, his grandfather's name. He followed the number across fifty-one years the way you follow a river on a map, and every ten pages or so it stopped being the same number and became a bigger one, and every time it changed there was a reason written beside it in somebody's hand.

BIRJU: (reading, fast, flat) Aghan, interest. Baisakh, advance for illness, six hundred. Interest. Fine, absence. Interest on fine. Grain issued against card, valued. Interest. Transfer charge — transfer to whom, we are not told —

SFX: Pages, faster.

BIRJU: 1991. Divided. Divided into three. My grandfather's three sons, one third each, and the smallest of them is my father.

SFX: A pen dropped.

BIRJU: 2009. Consolidated. All three back into one house. Ours. Because the other two houses had gone to Punjab and to God, and a number does not care which.

SFX: The kiln.

NARRATOR: And then Sanichari's line, because he had promised. Ramnath, Sanichari's, second house, entered 2007. He went into the water in 2011 and there is a paper at the police station saying so and his mother keeps it in a bundle with eleven others.

BIRJU: (barely) Twenty-two thousand, four hundred. He's been dead fifteen years and he's been earning them four hundred rupees a year.

SFX: Nothing.

NARRATOR: It was somewhere around three in the morning, on the third night, that he stopped looking for entries and started looking at the shape of the page. He had ruled a hundred slips. He knew what a page looks like when it is designed. He turned volume one to entry one and laid volume nine beside it, open at today, and he put the lamp between them, and he looked at the two pages for a long time without writing anything down.

SFX: A single sheet of paper being smoothed flat under a palm. The fan is not on. The lamp.

BIRJU: (very quietly) Name. Date. Head. Amount. Balance carried.

SFX: Nothing.

BIRJU: Name. Date. Head. Amount. Balance carried.

NARRATOR: Fifty-one years. Nine volumes. Four hundred and eleven pages in the first alone, and eleven thousand entries, and not one rupee has ever been written against the debt — and Birju had spent three nights hunting for the payments before he understood that he had been hunting for a place that does not exist.

NARRATOR: The book has no credit column. It was never ruled with one. There is nowhere on the page a payment could be written.
