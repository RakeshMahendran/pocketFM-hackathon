# Episode 10 — "Divisible by Three"

SFX: Nine ledgers going down on a table, one at a time, hard. Board covers. Nine separate thumps in a small room.

DHANIRAM: Beta. My table.

BIRJU: Sir, you asked me for one figure you could divide by three.

DHANIRAM: I asked you in Baisakh.

BIRJU: It's Aghan. I have it.

SFX: A chair pulled up. Two more men coming in behind — the white shirts, the ones from Sohanpur. Boots last.

DEVLAL: (from the doorway) Papa, the loading —

DHANIRAM: Stand there. Both of you also. Sit if you like, there is one chair and the munshi is in it. Go on, beta.

BIRJU: Sir, before the figure. One question about the partition.

DHANIRAM: Ask.

BIRJU: When you divide the site between your three sons, what is it that you are dividing?

DEVLAL: The kiln. The pit, the shed, the chimney, the land under it —

BIRJU: The land is eleven bighas and it is worth what land is worth. Devlal-ji, you know what this place earns in a season. It is not eleven bighas of earning.

SFX: Nothing. The fan.

BIRJU: You are dividing the book.

DHANIRAM: (mildly) Go on.

BIRJU: Forty-one households carry a balance. That balance is the asset. It is bigger than the land, it is bigger than the shed, and it is the only thing here that grows on its own without anybody firing a kiln. And you cannot divide it.

DEVLAL: Of course we can divide it. Fourteen houses, fourteen houses, thirteen houses —

BIRJU: On what document?

DEVLAL: On — on a paper. A paper saying these houses to me, these to him.

BIRJU: And when a house says, I do not owe you, what do you show them?

DEVLAL: The book.

BIRJU: Which book? There are nine. The house's figure is not in any one of them; it is the sum of a chain that runs from 1974 to Tuesday through nine volumes, four hundred and eleven pages in the first alone, and a great deal of it is in the handwriting of men who are dead. There is no total anywhere. Not one. In fifty-one years nobody has ever written down what any house owes and shown how they got there.

SFX: An old man not moving.

BIRJU: Sir, you know this already. You have known it for years. That is why you have never divided anything.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

DHANIRAM: My father used to say the book is the kiln and the kiln is the book. He said it the way people say things that they have not examined. (a chair creaking) Say the rest.

BIRJU: The moment you write a total, you have to be able to defend it. And the day after you write it, one of your sons will ask the other two how his fourteen houses were chosen, and the three of them will each go and get their own man to read it, and by Baisakh there will be three readings of the same book in this district and the first person to find that out will be a clerk in Sohanpur with a despatch number.

SFX: One of the white shirts shifting against the wall.

DEVLAL: He is threatening you, Papa.

DHANIRAM: He is not threatening me. He is describing my sons. (a pause) Sit down, Devlal. On the floor is fine.

SFX: Boots, sitting.

DHANIRAM: What do you want, beta? You did not carry nine volumes up here to teach me arithmetic.

BIRJU: I can produce the total. One reading, one hand, one document — house by house, chain by chain, with the page number of every step written beside it so that anybody who checks it arrives where I arrived. It will take me until the end of Poos. When it is done you will have one figure. It will divide by three or it will divide however you tell it to divide, and it will hold, because it will be checkable.

DHANIRAM: And your price.

BIRJU: Three things.

DHANIRAM: A boy with three things.

BIRJU: One. An appointment letter. Typed, not written. My name on it, printed. Munshi, both sites, with the pay written on it in figures and in words.

DHANIRAM: Done. What else.

BIRJU: Two. Nobody else's hand in that book. Not one entry, not a slip, not the middle of a slip. If it is checkable it has to be one hand or it is worth nothing.

DEVLAL: Papa —

DHANIRAM: I said that already in this room six weeks ago and I do not enjoy saying things twice. Third thing.

BIRJU: The dead come off.

SFX: The fan. Four turns of it.

DHANIRAM: Say that properly.

BIRJU: There are nine names in the nine volumes still being carried forward who are dead. I have the dates. Ramnath, Sanichari's second house, went into the water in 2011 and there is a paper at the thana. He is at twenty-two thousand four hundred and he has gone up four hundred every year since he drowned. There are eight more like him. Sir — if a checker finds one dead man in my total, the whole total is finished, and so is the partition, and so is the paper your sons are going to sign.

DHANIRAM: (slowly) How much do the nine come to.

BIRJU: One lakh, sixty-one thousand, eight hundred.

DEVLAL: One lakh sixty-one —

BIRJU: Which is not money, Devlal-ji. It has never been money. It is nine lines in a book that make the other four hundred lines worthless.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

SFX: An old man standing up, in stages, and walking to a door and opening it.

DHANIRAM: (out into the corridor, raised) EVERYBODY. In the yard. Now. Stop the loading. Sanichari — where is Sanichari — somebody get the old woman, and bring her slowly, she has a knee.

SFX: The yard. Four hundred people. Bricks not going down.

DHANIRAM: Sanichari. Come to the front. Munshi. Read out what you are about to do.

BIRJU: (loud) Ramnath. Sanichari's second house. Entered in the book in 2007. Died in the year they widened the culvert. Twenty-two thousand four hundred rupees.

SANICHARI: Say it again.

BIRJU: Twenty-two thousand, four hundred.

SFX: A pen. A line drawn through a name, hard, and then through a figure, and then a second line under both.

BIRJU: Struck. Reason written in words: deceased 2011, police record produced by the mother. Initialled by me, dated today, countersigned by the licensee.

DHANIRAM: I countersign it.

SFX: Four hundred people making a sound they have not made in fifty-one years and do not have a shape for.

BIRJU: And eight more. Budhni's husband. Mohar, first house. Ram Ekbal —

SFX: The names going on under the yard's noise. A woman crying somewhere and not being quiet about it.

SANICHARI: (over all of it, to nobody, to everybody) Fifteen years I have been feeding my son bricks.

NARRATOR: One lakh sixty-one thousand eight hundred rupees came off the book at teghra more on a Tuesday afternoon in Aghan, in front of everybody, because nine dead men were bad arithmetic. Not because they were dead. Because they were bad arithmetic. Birju understood this while he was doing it and did it anyway and let them cheer, and afterwards he sat in the office for a while with his hands flat on the table.

SFX: The office. A typewriter — an actual typewriter, brought from the shop at the crossing, being fed a sheet.

SFX: Keys. Slow, two-fingered, one letter at a time. A carriage return. More keys.

DHANIRAM: (from the corridor) It is done. Take it.

SFX: A sheet pulled out of a roller.

NARRATOR: He had wanted this since he was seventeen years old. Printed, not written. His own name, made by a machine, which is the only way a name in this district becomes a fact.

BIRJU: (reading it aloud, and his voice is not steady) "Appointed munshi, teghra more site and crossing shop, from this date, both premises."

SFX: Paper. A thumb going over raised type.

NARRATOR: And below the name, in the same typing, on the same sheet, is the line Dhaniram dictated last, which Birju did not ask for and did not see being typed.

BIRJU: (reading) "Responsible for the recovery of eleven lakh forty thousand rupees, being the outstanding of forty-one households."
