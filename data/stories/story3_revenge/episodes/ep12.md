# Episode 12 — "Four Households"

SFX: A stove being raked out. A stone lifted off a tin lid and set down on the floor. The lid coming off.

PHULWA: It is not here.

BIRJU: Look under the cloth.

PHULWA: I have looked under the cloth. There is the school paper and your father's photo and the eleven — the papers — and the tin is otherwise empty. Have you eaten?

BIRJU: Amma. The ration card. When did you last see it.

PHULWA: Poos. Devlal took it at the shop to write on it and gave it back and I put it here and I put the stone on it. I always put the stone on it.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

BIRJU: He told me to move it. In Baisakh he told me to move the tin.

PHULWA: Who did?

BIRJU: (not answering) Amma, sit down. Sit.

PHULWA: I will make tea.

BIRJU: SIT DOWN.

SFX: Somebody sitting on a floor. A knee that does not like it.

NARRATOR: In Baisakh an old man had stood in a doorway and told a boy to move a tin because a stove is a bad place for paper. Birju had heard it as a threat, and had been frightened by it, and had done nothing, and had told nobody. He had heard it correctly. He had simply heard it four months early, which is the same as not hearing it at all.

BIRJU: Bhaiya. Where's Bhaiya?

PHULWA: He went out before light.

BIRJU: Where?

PHULWA: He has been going out before light for eleven days.

SFX: A road. Early. Birds and one pair of feet moving fast.

SFX: A different yard — smaller, no chimney — and a hand pump being worked.

HEMWATI: You again. It is nine kilometres, munshi, and you have done it in the dark, which is more than you did for my paper.

BIRJU: Line nine.

HEMWATI: Line nine.

BIRJU: My mother is line nine. You have never met my mother.

HEMWATI: No.

BIRJU: The figure is right to the rupee and it is not in my hand and it is not out of the book. So somebody said it to you. Out loud. And there are exactly four people alive who could have.

HEMWATI: Three of them cannot read.

BIRJU: That is not an answer.

HEMWATI: It is the whole answer. Sit down and drink this and then I will tell you, because it will hurt less sitting.

SFX: A steel tumbler set down on a step.

HEMWATI: He came on the second night. He walked. He would not come inside and he would not give a name for a long time and then he said, I am the moulder from teghra more, and I said, which one, and he said, the one whose brother writes the book.

SFX: Nothing.

HEMWATI: He could not read the paper I put in front of him. He would not put a thumb on it, either, because he said thumbs are what got his house here. So he stood in that doorway and he said the numbers to me. All of them. Sixty-one houses at your site, munshi, and the figure against each one, and the years.

BIRJU: From memory.

HEMWATI: From one afternoon. He said his brother read them out in a yard and he has not been able to put them down since. He said — and I wrote this at the bottom of my copy, so I would say it right — he said, "I don't do paper. But I can hold a number. Somebody should hold them."

SFX: A hand pump. A crow. A young man not saying anything for some time.

BIRJU: His ear is still not right.

HEMWATI: I know. I asked him about it. He said a scale is heavier than it looks.

SFX: Feet on gravel. Fast, arriving.

CHAMELI: Birju!

BIRJU: Chameli — how did you —

CHAMELI: Sudama's cycle. Nine kilometres. Get up.

BIRJU: Your father —

CHAMELI: My father is at the crossing watching a truck load and that is why I am here, so get UP.

SFX: A cycle turned round on gravel. Two people getting onto it badly.

SFX: The crossing. A diesel engine idling. A tarpaulin. Twenty people standing at a distance, the way people stand when they have decided not to be involved.

MAQSOOD: Munshi. You are early. Nobody is ever early for this.

BIRJU: Who is on that truck.

MAQSOOD: Four households.

BIRJU: WHO.

MAQSOOD: (unhurried) Munshi, I am the transport. I am not the reason. Somebody is always shouting at the transport.

SFX: A tarpaulin flap thrown back. Inside a truck: bedding, tin, a child not crying because it has been told not to.

SANICHARI: (from inside, flat) In the year the well fell in, they moved us at night. Now they move us at eight in the morning. That is the progress.

BIRJU: Aunty —

SANICHARI: Do not say anything, munshi-ji. You said anything last time and it was worth six thousand rupees. Say something.

SFX: Boots on tar.

DEVLAL: He can't. He is not on this paper.

BIRJU: Devlal-ji. Does your father know?

DEVLAL: My father has three sons and one book, and he gave the book to a twenty-one-year-old from bharwara, so my father is not currently a man I consult about my own yard.

BIRJU: You are selling his workers.

DEVLAL: I am transferring four households at valuation to a site in Kaimur, plus fifteen per cent, which is the rate, ask anyone. Kaimur pays teghra more, teghra more's balance reduces by four lines, and I take the fifteen. Do you know what fifteen per cent of four households is, munshi? Because you would, wouldn't you. You'd know it to the rupee.

BIRJU: Forty-eight thousand, six hundred.

DEVLAL: (genuinely delighted) There it is. There he is. God, you are useful.

SFX: The engine. Somebody's tin trunk sliding.

BIRJU: Which four households.

MAQSOOD: It is written on the slip. It is always written on the slip, that is the one thing that is always written.

SFX: A paper taken out of a shirt pocket. Unfolded once. The engine going quiet as somebody's hand goes to the key.

NARRATOR: Four names. Sanichari's, first house, which is the only house she has left. Budhni's. Mohar's. And the fourth is at the bottom, and the fourth has twenty-nine years written against it, and the fourth is his mother's.

BIRJU: (to devlal, quietly) You need the licensee's signature for a transfer.

DEVLAL: I know.

BIRJU: You do not have the licensee's signature.

DEVLAL: I don't need my father's. As per the lady from Sohanpur — as per, that's the phrase, isn't it — the site's accounts are maintained by a named person and it is that person's certification that makes an entry good. She wrote it in her own file. My father shouted it in his own office in front of my brothers. One hand in the book. Only that person should maintain it.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

DEVLAL: You made yourself the only signature that is worth anything, munshi. I only had to go and get it.

BIRJU: You cannot forge my hand. Nobody in this district can write my hand.

DEVLAL: Forge? (a laugh, short) Look at it.

SFX: Paper, held out. Taken. Held very close.

NARRATOR: It is the transfer slip. Ruled, headed, site — teghra more, four households listed with the years and the figures, and at the foot the certification block: signature of the munshi, over the printed line, in ink.

NARRATOR: It is his signature. Not an imitation of it — his, the real one, the one he practised at seventeen and has written eleven thousand times since, with the loop under the j.

NARRATOR: And the date beside it is the ninth of Aghan. Which is the afternoon he sat down in the office with a typewritten letter in his hand and signed, one after another, without reading a single one of them, the forty-one blank certification slips a new munshi is required to sign on the day he takes the chair.
