# Episode 2 — "The Queue at the Crossing"

SFX: A tin measure scraping the bottom of a grain drum. Twenty people in a queue that is not moving, and the shutter of a shop half down against the sun.

DEVLAL: Card. Card. Thumb here. Card.

SUDAMA: In Punjab they weigh it in front of you.

DEVLAL: In Punjab. Yes. And here you are, standing in this queue, in Punjab.

SUDAMA: I am telling you what a scale looks like.

DEVLAL: Thumb.

SFX: A thumb pressed into an ink pad, and the small wet sound of it going down on paper.

NARRATOR: The ration shop at the crossing and the kiln at teghra more belong to the same man and are written in the same hand. Nobody in bharwara has ever found that strange, because nobody in bharwara has ever seen the two books apart.

BIRJU: Amma, give me the card. I'll stand for you.

PHULWA: You have not eaten since yesterday.

BIRJU: Give me the card.

PHULWA: There is rice at home. Come home and eat it and then stand.

BIRJU: (sharp) Amma. The card.

SFX: Cloth unfolding. A tin box being opened somewhere near the ground.

NARRATOR: He was ashamed of shouting at her and he was more ashamed of why. For two months he had walked past this queue on his way to a chair. He had liked walking past it.

SFX: Footsteps on gravel. A cycle set down against a wall.

CHAMELI: Birju.

BIRJU: You should not be here. Your father —

CHAMELI: My father is why I am here. Come out of the line.

BIRJU: I'll lose the place.

CHAMELI: Lose it.

SFX: The queue shuffling forward without him. Somebody's laugh, held in.

CHAMELI: He has said no.

BIRJU: To what?

CHAMELI: To you. To Baisakh, to the marriage, to all of it. He said it last night and he said it again this morning at the tea shop, so it is said in two places now.

BIRJU: I have a post. I have a chair. There is nobody else in this hamlet who has —

CHAMELI: I know what you have.

BIRJU: Then tell him what I have.

CHAMELI: I told him. He asked me a question and I could not answer it, so now I am asking you. Is your house in the book?

SFX: Nothing. Long.

CHAMELI: Birju.

BIRJU: My house works at the kiln. Every house here works at the kiln.

CHAMELI: That is not the question and you know it is not.

BIRJU: (flat) It is in the book.

CHAMELI: Then say what for.

BIRJU: Carry-forward.

CHAMELI: What does that mean?

BIRJU: It means — it is brought forward. From before. From an earlier page.

CHAMELI: From which page?

BIRJU: (nothing)

CHAMELI: You are the munshi and you do not know which page.

SFX: The tin measure again, further off. The queue moving.

SANICHARI: Girl. Let him alone. In the year the well fell in, my husband could not say which page either, and he was the one who put his thumb on it.

CHAMELI: Then he should have asked.

SANICHARI: He asked. They showed him the page. He still could not read it. That is the whole trick, daughter, and it is not a very clever one.

SFX: A man's voice, further up the queue, raised so that it carries the length of it.

CHAMELI: (quietly, to birju) That is him. He has come to collect our grain himself so that everyone sees him do it without me.

BIRJU: Chameli —

CHAMELI: He is going to say it now. He said he would.

SFX: The queue going quiet in sections, the way a field goes quiet.

CHAMELI: (repeating it flatly, as it reaches her) "Eighty-nine thousand, four hundred. I am not putting my daughter into that."

SFX: Twenty people turning to look at one boy.

NARRATOR: Her father has never worked at teghra more. He has never held the book, never stood at a pay-out, never been inside dhaniram's office. Birju read that figure aloud for the first time in his life yesterday evening, to forty people, in a yard three kilometres from this queue. And her father has it right to the rupee.
