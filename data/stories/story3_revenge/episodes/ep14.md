# Episode 14 — "The Credit Column"

SFX: A steel ruler laid on old paper. A pen drawn down it, slowly, the full length of the page. Then again. Then again.

NARRATOR: He ruled it on the eighth, at three in the morning, in a room with the door bolted. One line, down the outside edge, on the last page of volume nine — and then a heading, in the same hand that ruled a hundred fine slips, in letters he made bigger than they needed to be.

BIRJU: (writing, saying it) Paid. By. The. House.

SFX: The kiln. Only the kiln.

SFX: The yard. Eleven o'clock. Four hundred people, and a table brought out into the open, and nine ledgers on it, and a scooter, and a white government jeep that nobody in bharwara has ever seen at teghra more.

DHANIRAM: Beta. Come here a minute before it starts.

BIRJU: Sir.

DHANIRAM: Whatever you are going to do, do it inside. Not in the yard.

BIRJU: The notice says in the presence of the workers.

DHANIRAM: The notice says a great many things. I have read notices for forty years. (a pause, and it is not a performance) Birju. I typed your name on a letter with my own hands because my sons cannot type. Do you know what I have never done in sixty-three years? Raised my voice at a man in that yard. Twice this season. Both times for you.

BIRJU: I know, sir.

DHANIRAM: Is your mother keeping well?

SFX: Nothing. Long.

BIRJU: My mother is in a truck at Kaimur, sir. As of Tuesday.

DHANIRAM: (and this is true, and everyone later agrees it was true) I did not know that.

BIRJU: No, sir. I believe you. It is the first thing about this book you have not known.

SFX: A table. Papers. A file bag unzipped.

RAJNI: As per the notice, the accounts are produced by the named munshi in person. Munshi — the volumes.

BIRJU: Nine, madam. 1974 to today. Four hundred and eleven pages in the first alone.

RAJNI: The officer's questions are three. One: what is the outstanding of each household. Two: how was it arrived at. Three: has any payment ever been received and where is it recorded. Answer on oath.

SFX: Four hundred people. The kiln. Nothing else.

BIRJU: (and he says it to the yard, not to the table) Answer to question three. In fifty-one years, eleven thousand entries, nine volumes: no payment has ever been recorded. Not one.

DEVLAL: Because nobody ever paid!

BIRJU: Sudama's mother paid eleven hundred rupees in the year of the storm and Tirath wrote it in the margin in pencil and the margin was trimmed off at the binder's. Sudama sent sixteen thousand from Punjab through the shop at the crossing and the shop entered it as a receipt of the shop. Sanichari brought four hundred rupees in Poos 2016.

SANICHARI: Four hundred and twenty.

BIRJU: Four hundred and twenty. It is not that nobody paid, Devlal-ji. It is that there was nowhere to write it. Madam — hold the book up. Show them the page. Five columns. Name, date, head, amount, balance carried. There is no column for a payment. There has never been a column for a payment. It was ruled that way in 1974 and every volume since has been ruled to match.

SFX: A sound going through four hundred people, in a wave, from the front.

BIRJU: Answer to question two. How was it arrived at. It was arrived at by adding. Only by adding. For fifty-one years this book has been able to do one arithmetic operation, and I have been doing it too, and I was good at it, and Dhaniram-ji told the government in Aghan that I was correct, and he was right, I was correct — the way a scale is correct when it only has one pan.

RAJNI: (writing) Continue.

BIRJU: Answer to question one. The outstanding of each household. Madam, I have it. Every chain, every page number, from 1974 to Tuesday. Forty-one households, eleven lakh forty thousand rupees, and I will stand behind every rupee of it because I built it.

SFX: Paper. A great deal of paper set down.

BIRJU: And I have the other side.

SFX: Nothing.

BIRJU: On the eighth I ruled a column. One column, on the last page, and I have filled it, and it took me three days and two nights and Tirath sat with me for both of them because he is the only other man alive who has read all nine.

TIRATH: (from the crowd, and he is entirely sober and has been for six days) Eleven years and four months, munshi sahib. Say what we counted.

BIRJU: Bricks.

SFX: Four hundred people.

BIRJU: Every firing since 1974 is in these volumes, because he had to know what he was selling. Every house's hands are in them, because he had to know who to fine. So the bricks are countable. I have counted them. And the government prints a rate — nine hundred and sixty rupees a day, it is on the wall of the block office, madam typed it herself — and against that I have set every rupee this site can prove it ever handed over, including the grain, at the shop's own valuation, in his own hand, in his own book.

RAJNI: Give the figure to the officer, not to me.

BIRJU: (and he turns and says it to the yard) Against forty-one households, over fifty-one years, at the printed rate, this site is short by one crore, nine lakh, forty-six thousand rupees.

SFX: The yard, and it is not a cheer, it is a noise with no name.

DEVLAL: That is not real money! That is a sum on a paper!

BIRJU: Yes, Devlal-ji. That is exactly what it is. That is what every figure in this book has always been. Eighty-nine thousand four hundred was a sum on a paper. It broke my mother's thumb into ink and it cancelled my marriage in a ration queue and it is standing in a truck at Kaimur this morning. You have all spent your lives being paid in real bricks and charged in sums on a paper.

NARRATOR: The officer did not speak once. He is not important and he did not want to be, and when it was over he took his copy and got into the jeep. What he did before that took eleven minutes and it is the whole of the ending, so it is worth going slowly.

RAJNI: (reading aloud, flat, official) On the material produced — being the site's own accounts, produced on oath by the named munshi — the arrangement at teghra more and at the crossing shop is a bonded labour system. Finding recorded. Sixty-one workers. Certificates to issue forthwith.

SFX: A file bag. A rubber stamp. Once. Then again. Then very many times, fast, and it is the best sound in fourteen episodes.

RAJNI: Sixty-two from Chait that have been in my almirah since Baisakh, and sixty-one from here. I brought all of them. My pending count as of this afternoon is zero and I would like that noted.

SFX: Paper going into hands. Hands that do not know how to hold paper. Somebody laughing, wrongly, and this time not stopping.

RAJNI: Sanichari.

SANICHARI: Twenty-two years.

RAJNI: Twenty-two years. Sign or thumb.

SANICHARI: (fiercely) Thumb. And write my name with the long i, and read it to me first.

BIRJU: (reading it to her, out loud, all of it, and it takes a while)

SANICHARI: (when he has finished) Again.

BIRJU: (reading it again)

SANICHARI: Now the thumb.

NARRATOR: A release certificate cancels the debt. Not reduces — cancels. Forty-one households at teghra more went to zero at about a quarter past twelve, and the four households at Kaimur went to zero at the same moment, because a thing that does not exist cannot be transferred, and Maqsood, who is not a bad man and is not a good one, drove them back on the Thursday and did not charge for the diesel.

SFX: The crossing. Later. A different crowd, or the same one moved four kilometres.

DHANIRAM: Lakhan.

LAKHAN: I don't do paper.

DHANIRAM: No paper. Hold out your hand.

SFX: Notes counted into a palm. Out loud. In the open. Twenty people watching.

DHANIRAM: Four days at five hundred that were taken off you in Chait. Two thousand for the insolence that my son invented. The wage for eleven days you were not credited. Eight thousand, four hundred rupees, in cash, at the crossing, in front of everybody. Count it.

LAKHAN: I don't count either. Birju counts.

BIRJU: It is eight thousand four hundred, Bhaiya.

LAKHAN: Then it is right.

SFX: Somebody in the crowd saying something, and being shushed, and saying it anyway.

CHAMELI: That is my father. He is trying to work out how to say something to you without saying it.

BIRJU: He doesn't have to say anything.

CHAMELI: He does, actually. He said the number out loud in this queue in Chait and this is the same queue. (louder) Papa. Say it here or don't say it at all.

SFX: Nothing. Then a man's voice, further up the queue, raised so that it carries the length of it, saying one short sentence.

CHAMELI: (repeating it flatly, as it reaches her, and she is not crying, Chameli does not cry) "Baisakh, then. Before the firing."

NARRATOR: Tirath was paid his four months on the Monday — nineteen thousand two hundred, and he asked for it in the office, with the door open, so that men who had stepped over his legs could see him take it. Sudama's mother's line went to zero with the rest and he stood at the crossing and made three separate people say his name, and then he went back to Punjab anyway, which surprised everybody except Chameli. Hemwati got her thirty-second signature at four o'clock, on the bonnet of the jeep. Devlal was named in the finding along with his father and his matter is somewhere in a court, and courts are slow, and he is still in this district, and there is nothing tidy to say about him. Phulwa's page — 2009, Aghan, the word RECEIPT printed at the top — was produced, cancelled and struck through by the officer's own hand, and her thumbprint on it is now a thumbprint on a cancelled paper, which is not the same as it never having happened, and Birju has never pretended otherwise.

SFX: The yard at teghra more. Late afternoon. Almost empty. A table still standing in the open with nine ledgers on it.

BIRJU: Sir. One more thing to sign.

DHANIRAM: (an old man sitting down slowly, in stages) There is always one more thing. What is it.

BIRJU: Entry one. Volume one. 1974. Five thousand rupees, purpose marriage.

DHANIRAM: Your grandfather.

BIRJU: My grandfather. The certificate cancels what my house owes. It does not touch what was taken. Fifty-one years of my family's hands at the printed rate, against every rupee and every kilo of grain this site can prove it gave us, in your book, in your handwriting.

DHANIRAM: How much.

BIRJU: One lakh, sixty-two thousand.

SFX: Nothing. Long. The kiln — and then, for the first time in fifty-one years, the kiln going out, because nobody has fired it since Tuesday.

DHANIRAM: (quietly) You know I could fight it.

BIRJU: You could. For nine years, in Sohanpur, with three sons who each want their own man to read the book. Or you could sign it here, at this table, in front of the eleven people still standing in this yard, and be finished with it today.

DHANIRAM: (a long breath) Give me the pen. Not that one. The good one.

SFX: A pen. A single signature on a single sheet. It is a very small sound.

DHANIRAM: There. It is done. Take it and go home to your mother.

BIRJU: Not yet, sir.

DHANIRAM: What now?

BIRJU: Now I read it to you.

SFX: Nothing.

DHANIRAM: I can read, beta.

BIRJU: I know you can.

SFX: Paper being picked up off a table.

BIRJU: (reading, out loud, unhurried) "Received from Dhaniram, licensee, teghra more site and crossing shop, the sum of one lakh sixty-two thousand rupees, being the amount due to the house of Phulwa in settlement of the account opened in 1974. This account is closed."

SFX: Nothing. Long.

BIRJU: Once more, sir. Both times out loud. That is how it should have been done in this yard in 2009, to my mother, by a boy of twelve who could read and did not.

SFX: Paper. The kiln, cold.

BIRJU: (reading it again, out loud, all the way to the end) "...This account is closed."
