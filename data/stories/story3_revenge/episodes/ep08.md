# Episode 8 — "Volume One"

SFX: A rubber stamp coming down on a page. Again. Again. A woman counting under her breath between them.

RAJNI: Forty-one. Forty-two. As per the notice, sir, the inspection covers both premises. The shop at the crossing and this site. Same licensee, same accounts.

DHANIRAM: Same accounts, madam. I have never hidden that.

RAJNI: Nobody said hidden. I said same. Forty-three.

SFX: The office, too full. Boots. Two other men standing who do not speak.

NARRATOR: Dhaniram has three sons. The eldest runs the yard and wears boots. The other two came that morning from Sohanpur in a white shirt each and stood against the wall for four hours, and this is the only part they play in this story, but they were watching, and later everyone will agree on what they saw.

RAJNI: Grain issued against cards, Chait to Aghan. Sixty-one families. I want the issue register and the outstanding column, and I want them to agree with each other.

DEVLAL: They agree.

RAJNI: They did not agree at Purnibathan and Purnibathan is sealed as of Tuesday, so you will forgive me if I look.

SFX: A ledger opened. Pages, methodically, one every four seconds.

SFX: Nothing. A long stretch of nothing but pages and the fan.

RAJNI: Who ruled this?

DEVLAL: Ruled?

RAJNI: The columns are ruled. Every page. There is a carry-forward at the head and a total at the foot and the total at the foot of page nineteen is the carry-forward at the head of page twenty. Do you know how many sites I have been to this year where the foot of one page has anything at all to do with the head of the next?

DEVLAL: (carefully) We keep it correct.

RAJNI: There is a correction on page seven, initialled, dated, with the reason written out in words. Six thousand struck off. In words.

DEVLAL: That is standard.

RAJNI: It is not standard. It is not remotely standard. As per my file, it is the only one in this block. Sir — licensee — who writes this book?

SFX: An old man taking his time. Chappals on concrete. A chair.

DHANIRAM: Birju. Stand up. Come here where the lady can see you.

BIRJU: Sir.

DHANIRAM: This is my munshi. Class ten. Twenty-one years old. He found the six thousand himself, he found it in front of forty people and he told me about it in front of forty people, which is a thing no grown man in this district has ever done to me.

RAJNI: (writing) Name and father's name for the file.

DHANIRAM: Write his name. His father is dead. (a pause exactly one beat too long) His father worked here.

SFX: The fan. The pages.

RAJNI: Then as per the inspection I have nothing adverse at this site. I will take a copy of pages one to twenty-two and I will note that the accounts are maintained by a named person. Sir, one thing.

DHANIRAM: Madam.

RAJNI: Whoever maintains it — only that person should maintain it. Two hands in one book is how a book becomes evidence.

SFX: Papers into a file bag. A zip. Boots going out. A scooter, refusing, catching.

SFX: The office. Four men and a fan.

DHANIRAM: Devlal.

DEVLAL: Papa.

DHANIRAM: You heard the lady.

DEVLAL: I heard a clerk from Sohanpur on a scooter with a broken —

DHANIRAM: You heard the lady. (and now he raises his voice, and he has raised it twice in six weeks and both times this boy was standing there) FROM TODAY THE BOOK IS BIRJU'S. Do you understand me? Not yours. You will not open it, you will not write in it, you will not fill in the middle of a slip and you will not touch the pad. You will bring your figures to him and he will decide what goes down. In front of your brothers I am saying it, so that nobody has to ask me later what I said.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

DEVLAL: He is twenty-one.

DHANIRAM: He is twenty-one and he is correct, and I have discovered this year that those are two different qualities and only one of them is rare.

DEVLAL: Papa —

DHANIRAM: Say it to him. Not to me. You put a scale in my yard and you put your hand on my best moulder in front of four hundred people two days after the government wrote my name in a file. Say it to him.

SFX: Boots. Not moving. Then moving.

DEVLAL: (flat, quiet, and every man in the room hears it) The book is yours, munshi.

SFX: A door. Boots going away fast down a corridor.

NARRATOR: He said it. That is all that matters and it is the whole of what Birju had wanted since the day the scale came out, and he had imagined it several times, and in none of the imagined versions did he feel like this.

SFX: A key on the table. Small. Brass. It slides an inch and stops.

DHANIRAM: Almirah.

BIRJU: Sir.

DHANIRAM: You have been pulling at that door since Baisakh like a goat on a rope. Take it. Everything in there is the shop's and the site's going back to my father's time and it is a great deal of dust and no gold, and now it is your problem. Reconcile it. Give me one figure at the end that I can divide by three.

BIRJU: Divide by —

DHANIRAM: I am sixty-three, beta. I have three sons and one book. Work it out.

SFX: Chappals. A door. An old man's voice from the corridor, without turning round.

DHANIRAM: And Birju. Your brother's ear. Get it looked at properly, not by the man at the crossing. Send me the bill. Not the book — me.

NARRATOR: He waited until the yard had gone. He waited until the shift hooter and then an hour past it, until the kiln was only the kiln and the roar of it had become, as it does at night, almost companionable.

SFX: A key in a steel lock. It does not turn. It turns.

SFX: A heavy door swinging on one hinge that has never been oiled. Dust. A great deal of dust.

BIRJU: (a breath) Oh.

NARRATOR: Nine of them. Cloth-bound, board covers, stacked on their sides on the bottom shelf where the damp gets in, tied in threes with jute string. He cut the string with the office scissors. It took him some time, because his hands were not steady, and he noticed that they were not steady and could not make them stop.

SFX: A board cover opening. Very old paper — it does not rustle, it clicks.

BIRJU: (reading) "Teghra more. Account of advances." (a pause) There's no year on the cover.

SFX: A page turning. One page. The first page.

BIRJU: (reading, and his voice does something on the date) Nineteen seventy-four.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

NARRATOR: Fifty-one years. Four hundred and eleven pages in the first volume alone, which is why the page in his mattress was numbered four hundred and eleven, and Tirath had known exactly what he was doing when he tore it, and had chosen the last page of the first book because the last page of a book tells you it had a first.

SFX: A fingertip on old paper, moving down.

BIRJU: Entry one. Advance. Five thousand rupees. Purpose — marriage.

SFX: The kiln. Just the kiln.

NARRATOR: There is a thumbprint beside it, pressed so hard that the ink has spread into the fibres of the paper and gone brown. And there is a name, written in a hand nobody at teghra more has used for forty years, in the space where the debtor's name goes.

NARRATOR: It is his grandfather's.
