# Episode 6 — "Six Thousand Off"

SFX: A scooter, badly silenced, coming up the kiln track and being switched off. A stand kicked down. A file bag unzipped in the sun.

RAJNI: Is the licensee present? As per the letter, the licensee is required to be present.

DEVLAL: My father is inside. Which letter?

RAJNI: The letter that was sent on the fourth. Registration of kiln workers, block-level, sixty-one names for this site, thumb impressions against each. As per the circular it should have been done in Chait.

DEVLAL: Nobody here got a letter.

RAJNI: It was despatched. I have the despatch number.

DEVLAL: A despatch number is not a letter, madam.

RAJNI: As per the register, it is.

SFX: A door. Chappals on concrete, unhurried. The kiln behind everything, that road that never arrives.

DHANIRAM: Sixty-one names. Sit, madam, sit, we will give you sixty-one names and tea. Birju! Bring the book out. Bring the chair also, the lady will not stand.

NARRATOR: The book had never before left the office. Remember that.

SFX: The ledger set down on a table in the open. Forty people who are supposed to be working, not working, at a distance where they can hear.

RAJNI: Name, age, house, and the outstanding against the house. The outstanding column is new this year.

DHANIRAM: The outstanding is a private matter between me and my workers.

RAJNI: As per the format, sir, it is column six.

DHANIRAM: (pleasantly) Then it is column six. Birju. Read them out.

BIRJU: All of them, sir?

DHANIRAM: All sixty-one. The lady has come from Sohanpur on a scooter, we will not make her come twice.

SFX: Pages. A crowd shifting closer without anybody appearing to move.

BIRJU: Chhotu's house. Fourteen thousand, plus ten thousand fine, Chait.

RAJNI: (writing) Twenty-four thousand.

BIRJU: Nepal's house. Forty-one thousand six hundred.

RAJNI: Forty-one six.

BIRJU: Sanichari. Sixty-three thousand, two hundred.

SANICHARI: Say it again.

BIRJU: Sixty-three thousand, two hundred.

SANICHARI: (to rajni) Write it. Write it properly with my name spelt with the long i. Twenty-two years I have been in that book and nobody from any office has ever written my name down.

RAJNI: As per the register, I am writing it.

SFX: A pause. A boy looking at a page for slightly too long.

BIRJU: Madam. One moment.

DHANIRAM: Beta.

BIRJU: Sir, it will take one moment.

DHANIRAM: The lady has other sites.

RAJNI: I have Purnibathan at three. But I am here now and the column is not filled, so.

SFX: Pages going backwards. Three of them. Then a fourth.

BIRJU: (loud enough for the yard) Sanichari's Aghan fine. Three thousand rupees. Entered Aghan, twenty-two years ago — it comes forward on this page here, at the top, where the carry-forward is written.

RAJNI: Yes.

BIRJU: It also comes forward here. Second book. Same three thousand, written again as a fresh entry, not as a carry-forward.

SFX: The crowd. Not talking. Getting closer.

BIRJU: And here. Third time. Same fine, same words, same amount, in a third year.

DEVLAL: That is how carry-forward works.

BIRJU: No it isn't. A carry-forward carries. It does not multiply. If it carried three times then Chhotu's hen is going to be worth thirty thousand by Aghan and forty by Baisakh and by the time the boy is your age, Devlal-ji, that hen will have cost this hamlet more than the shed.

SFX: One person laughing. Then more. Forty people laughing in a brick yard, and it is not a pleasant sound, it is the other kind.

DEVLAL: Quiet!

DHANIRAM: Let them laugh. (to birju, mildly) How much, beta.

BIRJU: Two duplications. Three thousand each. Six thousand rupees, sir. It should not be on her line.

DHANIRAM: Six thousand.

BIRJU: Six thousand.

SFX: The fan is not here. There is only the kiln, and forty people, and one old man taking his time.

RAJNI: Sir, shall I write sixty-three two, or shall I —

DHANIRAM: You will write fifty-seven thousand, two hundred. (raising his voice, which he never does, so that it carries the length of the yard) SANICHARI. Six thousand comes off your house. Today. In front of the government. Because my munshi found it, and my munshi is correct, and I do not keep a book with errors in it.

SFX: A sound from forty people that is not quite a cheer, because they do not know if they are allowed.

DHANIRAM: Birju. Strike it. Initial it. Give the lady the corrected figure.

BIRJU: Yes, sir.

SFX: A pen. A line drawn through a number, hard enough to be heard.

RAJNI: (writing) Fifty-seven thousand two hundred, as per correction by the site munshi, dated today. Sign here, and here, and your thumb on this one, mother.

SANICHARI: My thumb has been on eleven papers.

RAJNI: This is the twelfth. As per the scheme, the twelfth one gets you a card with a number on it.

SANICHARI: Will the card take anything off?

RAJNI: The card is a card, mother. It is not a remedy.

SANICHARI: Then I will keep it with the others.

NARRATOR: Six thousand rupees came off a line in a book on a Thursday afternoon, in the open, in front of the government and everybody who works at teghra more. It was the first time in twenty-two years that the number had moved the correct way. It is worth saying plainly, because it will not happen again for a long time.

SFX: The scooter starting. Not catching. Starting.

RAJNI: (over it) Munshi. Your correction is on my file now. Once it is on the file it cannot be taken off the file. That is either useful to you or it is a problem for you and I am not the person who decides which.

SFX: The scooter going away down the track.

SFX: The yard, emptying. Bricks starting again, one stack, then twenty.

SANICHARI: Munshi-ji.

BIRJU: Aunty — don't. I'm Birju. I sat in your courtyard, you used to —

SANICHARI: MUNSHI-JI. (loud, to the whole yard, twice) Munshi-ji. Let them hear me say it. Let that boy with the boots hear me say it.

SFX: Somebody in the yard saying it back, once, testing it.

SANICHARI: Now. You will do one more thing for me.

BIRJU: Anything.

SANICHARI: Do not say anything. People who say anything mean nothing. Say you will do this one thing.

BIRJU: I'll do it.

SANICHARI: Check my son's number.

SFX: Bricks. Bricks.

BIRJU: (carefully) Aunty. Your son.

SANICHARI: Ramnath. In the book he is Ramnath, Sanichari's, second house. He went into the water in the year they widened the culvert. That is 2011. There were people who saw it and there is a paper at the thana about it and I have that paper too, in the same bundle as the eleven.

BIRJU: Then why would he —

SANICHARI: Because when I could not work in Poos, they told me my house was short, and I said I have no other hands, and Devlal said, mother, you have two houses in the book.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

SANICHARI: He is still being carried forward, munshi-ji. Fifteen years. Go and read what my dead son owes.
