# Episode 13 — "The Eleventh"

SFX: A ceiling fan in a government room, slower than the one at the kiln. Files — not one file, a wall of them, tied with red tape. A rubber stamp somewhere down the corridor, patiently.

RAJNI: Sit. Not that chair, that chair is broken, that chair has been broken since I joined. As per the counter, you should have a token.

BIRJU: I don't have a token.

RAJNI: Then as per the counter you are not here. What is it?

BIRJU: A truck left the crossing yesterday morning with four households in it and it is standing at Kaimur tonight.

SFX: A pen being put down.

RAJNI: Written transfer?

BIRJU: Written. Ruled, headed, listed, certified.

RAJNI: Certified by whom?

SFX: Nothing. Long.

BIRJU: By me.

RAJNI: (evenly) Say the rest of it.

BIRJU: Forty-one blank slips. My first afternoon in the chair. He put them in front of me and said every munshi signs the certification block on the day he takes over, and I signed them, all of them, in eleven minutes, and I did not read one, because I had a typed letter with my name on it in my other hand and I was twenty-one years old and very pleased with myself.

RAJNI: Mm.

BIRJU: Madam, stop the truck.

RAJNI: On what.

BIRJU: On the fact that it is my signature and I say it is not good.

RAJNI: Munshi. Listen to me carefully, because I am going to be honest with you and it takes about four minutes and then I have a hearing. A transfer of workers between two kilns is not an offence. Men move for work. That is the whole economy of this district and if I stopped a truck for that I would be stopping every truck. What is an offence is bonded labour, and bonded labour is a finding, and a finding is made by an officer at an inquiry, and an inquiry needs a complaint, and a complaint needs figures that somebody with knowledge of the accounts will stand behind.

BIRJU: There is a complaint. Thirty-one households, Purnibathan and bharwara, with the figures.

RAJNI: I know. It came in on the ninth.

BIRJU: And?

RAJNI: And nobody has signed it who has ever seen a book.

SFX: The fan. Somebody in the corridor asking for a token.

BIRJU: What happens to it?

RAJNI: As per the practice — and I am telling you the practice, not the rule, because you are asking me a real question — it goes to the site for a reply. The site produces its accounts. If the accounts are bad, we have something. If the accounts are good, the complaint is disposed of as not substantiated and everybody goes back on Monday.

BIRJU: (flat) And the accounts at teghra more are good.

RAJNI: The accounts at teghra more are the best in this block. They are ruled and headed and initialled and corrected in words. I wrote that. I wrote it in a file in Aghan and it cannot come off the file, and I told you at the time that it was either useful to you or it was a problem for you and that I was not the one who would decide which.

SFX: A boy putting his head in his hands. It is audible. A chair takes his weight differently.

RAJNI: There is one more thing and then my hearing.

BIRJU: Madam.

RAJNI: Sixty-two release certificates.

BIRJU: I don't know what that is.

RAJNI: No. Nobody does, which is convenient for everybody except the sixty-two. When a person is found to have been in bonded labour, the law says a certificate is issued within twenty-four hours. It cancels the debt. Not reduces — cancels. The debt does not exist afterwards, and no court will hear a suit on it, and no kiln can transfer it, because there is nothing to transfer.

BIRJU: (very quietly) Say that again.

RAJNI: The certificate cancels the debt.

BIRJU: Then why does anybody in this district still owe anything?

RAJNI: Because I have sixty-two of them in that almirah, unsigned, from a rescue in Chait, and they have been sitting there for four months, and every week somebody's file goes up and comes back with a query about the annexure. I am one clerk. I have a transfer coming through in Baisakh and I would very much like my pending count to be zero when it does, and I am telling you that quite plainly because you look like a person who thinks the delay is a conspiracy.

BIRJU: Isn't it?

RAJNI: (tiredly) It is a queue, munshi. Sometimes a queue is a queue. It is not less effective for being one.

SFX: Papers. A drawer.

RAJNI: Now. You came here to ask me to stop a truck. I am going to tell you the only thing that would actually work and then you are going to leave, because I have a hearing and because after this I do not want to have had this conversation.

BIRJU: Tell me.

RAJNI: The inspection.

BIRJU: There was an inspection. In Aghan. You did it.

RAJNI: That was a ration inspection. This is the labour inquiry on the complaint, and it is fixed. It was fixed the day the complaint came in and the notice went out on the same despatch. Both premises, the site and the shop, sixty-one workers, all volumes.

BIRJU: When?

RAJNI: The eleventh.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

BIRJU: That's six days.

RAJNI: Five and a half. The officer comes at eleven o'clock and he does not wait, because he has Purnibathan at three, and I know that because I make his route.

BIRJU: And the licensee will hand him the beautiful book and the officer will say there is nothing adverse.

RAJNI: The licensee will not hand him anything.

BIRJU: Madam?

RAJNI: (and now she says it slowly, once, and does not repeat it) The accounts of a site are produced by the person who maintains them. Not by the owner. By the named munshi, in person, on oath, and he answers the officer's questions himself and signs the record of what he has said. That is in the notice. That is in every notice. Nobody reads the notice.

SFX: The fan. Four turns of it.

BIRJU: He can dismiss me before the eleventh.

RAJNI: He can dismiss you in an hour. But the notice names the site's munshi as on the date of the notice, which was the ninth of Aghan — and I typed it myself off the appointment letter he sent me, because he was so pleased with it he sent me a copy.

BIRJU: (barely) He sent you a copy.

RAJNI: He is very proud of you. It is the only genuinely nice thing I know about that man and I am telling it to you because you are about to do something and it will be easier for you if you have to think about it.

SFX: A chair. A file bag. A woman going out through a door and then stopping in it.

RAJNI: Munshi. One correction to what I said earlier, and then it is off the record entirely, because the record is that room and we are in a doorway.

BIRJU: Madam.

RAJNI: I said the accounts have to be good or bad. That is not right. The accounts have to agree with whatever the officer is told on the day. That is all an inquiry is. Whatever is in that book on the eleventh becomes the government's figure. It goes on a file, and it gets a number, and after that no kiln, no court, no son of anybody, and no clerk — including me — can move it by one rupee for as long as any of us are alive.

SFX: Nothing.

RAJNI: Eleven o'clock. He does not wait.
