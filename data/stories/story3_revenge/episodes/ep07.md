# Episode 7 — "The Weighing"

SFX: A hand-scale swinging on a chain, empty. Somebody flicking the pan with a fingernail, twice, to hear it ring.

DEVLAL: From today we weigh.

SFX: Four hundred people stopping at once. It sounds like wind dropping.

DEVLAL: You have been paid per thousand. A thousand what? A thousand of anything, apparently. Half of what came out of the Poos firing was light, and light bricks come back from the buyer, and when they come back they come back onto me. So. From today we weigh. Twelve bricks at random from every stack. Under weight, the stack is a second. Second is half rate.

LAKHAN: Bricks are light when the clay is light.

DEVLAL: Then bring better clay.

LAKHAN: The clay comes from your pit.

DEVLAL: Then mould it better.

SFX: The scale. Bricks going into a pan, four, eight, twelve. Chain creaking.

NARRATOR: Two days after a government clerk wrote a correction on a file in front of the whole yard, Devlal put a weighing scale in the middle of it. He did not explain the connection. He did not have to; a yard understands a scale the way it understands weather.

SFX: An office. The bent fan. A door not being knocked on.

BIRJU: Sir, the weighing —

DHANIRAM: Devlal's yard, Devlal's scale.

BIRJU: It is my column. Half rate has to be entered by me, sixty-one times, twice a week.

DHANIRAM: Then you will be busy. Busy is good for a young man. (a pause, quite kind) Beta. You did well on Thursday. You did very well, and I said so in front of everyone, and I meant it. But you did it on Devlal's ground and now Devlal has to stand on it again. That is not a thing I can take away from him. He is my eldest.

BIRJU: Sir, he is going to —

DHANIRAM: He is going to do what a man does when he has been laughed at in his own yard. And then it will settle. Things settle, beta. That is the only thing I have learned in sixty-three years and I am giving it to you free.

SFX: The yard. The scale again. The particular silence of people watching one man being made to wait.

DEVLAL: Lakhan's stack. Twelve.

SFX: Bricks into the pan. The chain. A held breath.

DEVLAL: Light.

LAKHAN: Weigh it again.

DEVLAL: Light.

LAKHAN: There are eleven hundred in that stack and I moulded every one with these. Weigh it again in front of the munshi.

DEVLAL: The munshi is in the office where he belongs.

LAKHAN: Then send for him.

DEVLAL: (delighted) Send for him! Do you hear this? Send for the munshi. Should I also send for the Collector? Should I send a despatch number?

SFX: Laughter — three or four men, the ones who load, the ones who eat first.

LAKHAN: I don't do paper. I do bricks. That stack is not light.

DEVLAL: Say that again.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

LAKHAN: That stack is not light.

SFX: A scale pan hitting a man in the side of the head. Once. The chain, wild, after it. A body going down onto hard-baked ground.

SFX: Four hundred people not moving.

NARRATOR: Birju heard it from ninety steps away. He knew what it was. He has been asked since — by his mother, by Chameli, by a man from a district office with a form — how long it took him to cover ninety steps, and he has never given anybody a number, because the number is bad. He stood in the office doorway for the length of it. He was frightened, and he was frightened in a very ordinary way: he did not want to be hit.

SFX: Gravel, fast, at last. A boy arriving too late to have arrived.

BIRJU: Bhaiya. Bhaiya, get up —

LAKHAN: I'm up.

BIRJU: Your ear —

LAKHAN: I'M UP.

SFX: A man standing, and the sound of four hundred people deciding whether to look.

DEVLAL: Good. Now, since the munshi has honoured us. Stack, second grade, half rate. And insolence at the weighing. Fine.

BIRJU: Devlal-ji —

DEVLAL: You want to check my arithmetic? Check it. Everyone, watch, the munshi is going to check my arithmetic, this is the entertainment now. Insolence at the weighing, five hundred. Doubled, because I said on Monday that everything is doubled and nobody was listening. One thousand.

BIRJU: There is no head called insolence.

DEVLAL: There is now. Write it.

BIRJU: I will not write it.

SFX: Nothing at all. The kiln, and nothing else.

DEVLAL: (softly, enjoying it) You will not write it.

BIRJU: No.

DEVLAL: Fine. Fine! I do not need you to write it, that is the beautiful part. Do you know what I have in my hand, munshi? Do you know what this is?

SFX: A pad of paper slips. A thumb riffling the edge of it, fast.

DEVLAL: Fine slips. One hundred of them. Ruled. Headed. Site — teghra more. House. Head of fine. Amount. Date. Signature of the munshi.

BIRJU: (a beat too late) Those are —

DEVLAL: Those are the pad you made. In Baisakh. Because you told my father the old slips were untidy, and he said, what a good boy, what a boy with a hand, and you sat in the office for two days and ruled one hundred slips.

SFX: One slip torn off a pad.

DEVLAL: I only fill in the middle bit.

SFX: A pen scratching. Four short strokes. A slip held out.

DEVLAL: Lakhan's hands. Insolence. One thousand. Today.

SFX: Nothing.

LAKHAN: Give it to me.

DEVLAL: It goes to the house.

LAKHAN: I am the house. Give it.

SFX: Paper changing hands.

NARRATOR: He held it at arm's length first, the way a man holds a thing he cannot read, turning it once to find the top of it. Then he raised it. He raised it above his head, at the centre of a yard with four hundred people in it, and turned all the way round, slowly, so that every one of them got a clean look.

LAKHAN: LOOK AT IT.

SFX: Four hundred people looking at a piece of paper.

LAKHAN: Not the number. I don't do numbers. Look at the writing.

SFX: A crow. The kiln. Nobody speaking.

LAKHAN: Every line on that paper. The lines, the words, the headings, my house at the top — all of it. That is not his hand.

SFX: A brick, somewhere, dropped and not picked up.

LAKHAN: That is my brother's.
