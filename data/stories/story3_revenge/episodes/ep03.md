# Episode 3 — "Page Four Hundred and Eleven"

SFX: A steel almirah door being pulled. It does not open. It is pulled again, harder, and rattles.

BIRJU: Sir. The almirah.

DHANIRAM: What about it.

BIRJU: It's locked.

DHANIRAM: Yes.

SFX: A ceiling fan with a bent blade, three feet above a small office. Outside it, the kiln — a low continuous roar, like a road that never arrives.

BIRJU: Sir, I need the earlier book.

DHANIRAM: Which earlier book.

BIRJU: The one the figures came from. Sanichari is sixty-three thousand two hundred. Chhotu is fourteen. My — the last house is eighty-nine four hundred. I wrote all of them down yesterday from a slip you gave me. The slip has no page numbers on it.

DHANIRAM: And?

BIRJU: And if somebody asks me where eighty-nine thousand four hundred came from, I have nothing to show them.

DHANIRAM: Who is going to ask you, beta?

SFX: A drawer opening. Paper, unhurried.

DHANIRAM: Here. A new book. Two hundred pages, good binding, forty rupees, and I am not putting the forty against you. Start it clean. Every munshi likes a clean book. Tirath liked a clean book so much he kept three of them.

BIRJU: Sir, a new book still has to begin somewhere. The figure has to come from —

DHANIRAM: It comes from the carry-forward.

BIRJU: The carry-forward comes from where?

DHANIRAM: From the book before.

BIRJU: And that book —

DHANIRAM: Is in the almirah. Which is locked. Which is why we are having this conversation twice.

SFX: The fan. The kiln. A boy deciding not to speak.

NARRATOR: He decided not to speak. Write that down somewhere, because in nine weeks he will not be able to remember making the decision, and he will want very badly to remember it.

DHANIRAM: Beta. Sit. You are standing like a man waiting for a bus.

SFX: The chair with the short leg, knocking as it takes weight.

DHANIRAM: You are educated. Class ten. Do you know how many people in this block finished class ten in your year?

BIRJU: No, sir.

DHANIRAM: Nor do I. But it is not many, and one of them is sitting in my office on my chair with my book on his knee, and the other ones are in Punjab loading onions. Is your mother keeping well?

BIRJU: She is well.

DHANIRAM: Good. Good. Write the fines for Chait. Devlal will read them to you.

SFX: A door. Devlal's boots — he wears boots, nobody else at teghra more wears boots.

DEVLAL: Ten thousand, Chhotu's house. Hen.

BIRJU: A hen.

DEVLAL: A hen walked across two thousand green bricks. Do you know what a hen's foot does to a green brick? It puts a hole in it. Two thousand bricks, and I sold them as seconds at half.

BIRJU: Then the loss is on two thousand bricks.

DEVLAL: The loss is what I say the loss is. Write ten.

BIRJU: (writing) Ten thousand. Chait. Chhotu's house.

DEVLAL: See, that is why I like an educated munshi. Tirath used to argue. Tirath used to argue for forty minutes and then write exactly what I told him, so it was forty minutes of my life for nothing.

SFX: A hooter from the chimney end. The shift changing. Hundreds of bricks going down at once, all over the yard, in no rhythm at all.

NARRATOR: Between the office and the gate there are ninety steps. Birju had counted them in his first week, because he was pleased with himself for having a reason to walk them.

SFX: Gravel. Then, at the gate, a man's voice — loud, elaborate, unsteady.

TIRATH: MUNSHI SAHIB. Munshi sahib is coming. Make way, make way, the accounts of the world are approaching on two legs.

DEVLAL: (off) Get him off the road.

TIRATH: I am on the road. The road is the government's. Ask your munshi, he has been to school. Beta! Is the road the government's?

BIRJU: Uncle, go home.

TIRATH: Uncle. Uncle! He calls me uncle. Two months in the chair and already so polite. I sat in that chair eleven years. Eleven years and four months, and the four months is the part I would like to discuss.

DEVLAL: He has been paid.

TIRATH: I have been paid for eleven years. The four months we do not talk about because it is not in any book, and if it is not in a book then it did not happen. Is that right, munshi sahib? Is that the rule?

SFX: Two men taking hold of somebody. Cloth tearing slightly. Feet skidding.

TIRATH: Do not — I am going. I am going on my own legs. Beta. Beta, come here.

BIRJU: Uncle —

TIRATH: (close, suddenly quiet, entirely sober) How many pages in your book?

BIRJU: Two hundred.

TIRATH: (laughing, and it is not a good laugh) Two hundred. Two hundred. He gave the child two hundred pages.

DEVLAL: Out.

SFX: A body going through a gate. The gate closing. A bicycle bell, receding, ringing far too long.

NARRATOR: In his fist, pressed there in the half second the two of them were close, Birju finds a piece of paper. It has been torn out along the binding, so the tear runs down the inside edge and takes half a column of figures with it. He carries it back the ninety steps without opening it, and opens it in the office with the door shut.

SFX: Paper unfolding. One sheet. It takes a long time for one sheet.

NARRATOR: There are eleven entries on it and he does not know any of the names. In the top right corner, in ink that has gone brown, is the page number.

NARRATOR: It is four hundred and eleven. The book Dhaniram put in his hands this morning begins at page one.
