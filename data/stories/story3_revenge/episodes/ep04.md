# Episode 4 — "Five Hundred a Day"

SFX: A single sheet of paper being smoothed flat under a palm, over and over, on a wooden table. The fan above it. Nothing else.

NARRATOR: He had kept page four hundred and eleven under the mattress for six days. On the seventh he brought it to work folded inside his shirt, because a thing under a mattress is a thing your mother finds.

BIRJU: (to himself, low) Four hundred and eleven. Four hundred and eleven.

SFX: Boots. The door shoved rather than opened.

DEVLAL: Your brother has not come in.

BIRJU: He's ill.

DEVLAL: He was ill yesterday and he was ill the day before and on both those days he was standing outside the tea shop being ill in a very upright manner.

BIRJU: I'll speak to him.

DEVLAL: You will not speak to him. You will write him. There is a difference and it is the whole of your job.

SFX: A ledger dragged across the table.

DEVLAL: Absent, Lakhan's hands, four days.

BIRJU: Four? He was here on Monday.

DEVLAL: He was here on Monday and he moulded nothing on Monday. A man who moulds nothing is absent whether or not his body is standing in my yard. Four days. Five hundred a day.

BIRJU: Five hundred a day is more than he earns in a day.

DEVLAL: Yes.

BIRJU: Then in four days he has gone backwards by —

DEVLAL: You are the one with class ten. You tell me.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

BIRJU: Two thousand. And he has lost the four days' earning as well.

DEVLAL: See how fast that was. Tirath would have taken forty minutes.

SFX: Pen on paper. Slow. Deliberately slow.

NARRATOR: He wrote it. It took eleven seconds. He would have said, if anyone had asked him at that moment, that he wrote it because refusing would not have unwritten it — that Devlal would simply have called the next man with a hand. That is true, and it is also the sentence people say afterwards, and he knew that too.

SFX: The yard. Wet clay slapped into moulds — a hundred hands doing it, a sound like slow rain on tin. Somebody singing four lines and giving up.

LAKHAN: You wrote it.

BIRJU: Bhaiya —

LAKHAN: I am asking. Did you write it or did he.

BIRJU: I wrote it.

LAKHAN: Good. At least you say it.

SFX: A mould knocked out. Clay landing.

BIRJU: You should have come in.

LAKHAN: I don't do paper. I do bricks. Bricks I understand. A brick is one brick. You cannot look at a brick and say it is four bricks because you feel like it.

BIRJU: Bhaiya, listen to me —

LAKHAN: One thousand a day is the number. I do one thousand. Some days eleven hundred. Amma does eight hundred stacking. That is nineteen hundred bricks out of one house every day since Chait and the number on our house went up, Birju. Up. You said it out loud in front of everybody. It went up.

BIRJU: It went up because of the carry-forward.

LAKHAN: What is a carry-forward?

BIRJU: It is — from an earlier book.

LAKHAN: Which earlier book?

SFX: Clay. Clay. Clay.

LAKHAN: You don't know.

BIRJU: I will know.

LAKHAN: (not unkindly) You have a chair.

BIRJU: I have a chair.

LAKHAN: Then use the chair for something. I don't want your pity, I want the number to move the correct way for one season. One. That's all. And when it moves I want to be paid in cash, in the open, at the crossing, where Chameli's father can see it happen, so that man has to shut his mouth in the same place he opened it.

SFX: A mould scraped clean with the edge of a hand.

LAKHAN: Say you'll do that.

BIRJU: I'll do that.

LAKHAN: Then go and sit in your chair.

NARRATOR: He was ashamed. It is worth stating plainly because he never stated it to anybody: standing in the yard in a shirt with a collar, in front of a brother whose forearms were grey to the elbow, he was ashamed, and part of what he was ashamed of was that the shirt was clean.

SFX: The office. The fan. A cup of tea set down — the good cup, the one with a saucer.

DHANIRAM: Sit, beta. Drink it while it is hot. Devlal says you finished the Chait fines in one afternoon.

BIRJU: Yes, sir.

DHANIRAM: Tirath used to take a week. Tirath used to take a week and then there would be an entry missing and we would find it in Aghan. Did you enter the four days?

BIRJU: I entered them.

DHANIRAM: Against?

BIRJU: (evenly) Against Lakhan's hands. Two thousand rupees.

DHANIRAM: Two thousand. Mm. Sad. He is the best moulder I have had at teghra more since the shed was built, and I include his father in that, and his father was very good.

SFX: A spoon in a cup, twice. Set down on the saucer.

BIRJU: Sir. I want to ask something.

DHANIRAM: Ask.

BIRJU: If a house pays, where is it written?

DHANIRAM: Where is what written?

BIRJU: The paying. If Sanichari brings six thousand rupees tomorrow and puts it on this table, which column does it go in?

SFX: The fan. Four full turns of it.

DHANIRAM: Sanichari is not going to bring six thousand rupees.

BIRJU: I know she isn't. I am asking about the column.

DHANIRAM: You are asking a very good question, beta, and you are asking it at four o'clock on a Thursday when I have a truck coming.

BIRJU: It's a small question.

DHANIRAM: All the small questions in that book took forty years to write. (warmly) Drink your tea. It is going cold and I told them to make it with milk, not water, because you are the munshi now. Did you notice that? Milk.

BIRJU: I noticed.

DHANIRAM: Good. A man should notice.

SFX: Chair legs. An old man standing up slowly, in stages.

DHANIRAM: Your brother wants to be paid in cash at the crossing.

BIRJU: (too quickly) He didn't —

DHANIRAM: The yard has ears, beta, and every one of them eats out of my shop. It is not a bad wish. It is a young man's wish. I had it myself once. (at the door) Tell me — does your mother still keep her card in that tin behind the stove?

SFX: Nothing.

DHANIRAM: The green tin. With the lid that does not sit properly, so she puts a stone on it.

BIRJU: (barely) Yes.

DHANIRAM: Tell her to move it. A stove is a bad place for paper. Things burn near stoves and then people say, oh, the card burnt, oh, the papers burnt, and then where are we. Go home early. You have worked hard.

SFX: Boots — no. Chappals, an old man's, going away down a corridor, unhurried.

NARRATOR: Birju has worked at teghra more for two months and eleven days. He has never spoken to Dhaniram about his house. He has never mentioned his mother's card, or the stove, or the shelf it stands on.

NARRATOR: He has never told anyone at all about the green tin.
