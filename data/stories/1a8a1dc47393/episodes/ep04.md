# Episode 4 — "The Date He Should Not Know"

SFX: committee gavel strike, villagers murmuring

RAGHAV: That photograph proves only one thing. Meera left Dharmapur with me because she wanted to abandon her marriage and her children.

MEERA: Let me ask him one question.

COMMITTEE HEAD: You will speak when the committee permits it.

KAVITA: This is a police verification, not a punishment meeting. She may question the man in the photograph.

RAGHAV: Certainly. A truthful man does not fear questions.

MEERA: Where was this photograph taken?

RAGHAV: Kota bus stand.

MEERA: Which platform?

RAGHAV: There are no platforms at that counter. Only benches.

KAVITA: So you recognise the place.

RAGHAV: I have worked in transport for twelve years. I recognise hundreds of counters.

DEVRAJ: Ask why my daughter was standing beside him.

MEERA: Your daughter? Yesterday, I was a stranger bringing scandal to your courtyard.

VOICE IN CROWD: He called her an impostor inside the police station.

SECOND VOICE IN CROWD: Now he needs her to be his daughter, because the photograph is public.

DEVRAJ: The committee did not summon the street to judge me.

KAVITA: No. The evidence did.

COMMITTEE HEAD: Raghav, explain the photograph from the beginning.

RAGHAV: Meera approached me privately. She said she was unhappy at home. She asked for transport to Kota.

MEERA: That is a lie.

RAGHAV: You asked me to speak. Please allow me the courtesy of finishing.

MEERA: Courtesy? You watched while I was taken away.

KAVITA: Meera, taken by whom?

MEERA: I don't... I did not know his name then. Raghav spoke to him near the tea counter.

RAGHAV: She means Shahid. He was arranging labour transport that week.

KAVITA: You know which man she means without hearing a description.

RAGHAV: Because she has made this accusation before. Confused stories travel quickly in a village.

MEERA: I have not spoken Shahid's name in this village.

RAGHAV: Then perhaps your story reached Dharmapur before you did.

COMMITTEE HEAD: Answer plainly. Did Meera travel with you willingly?

RAGHAV: Yes. She sat beside me for three hours. She drank tea. She could have called for help.

MEERA: You kept my phone.

RAGHAV: You said your battery had failed.

MEERA: You removed the battery.

RAGHAV: Modern phones do not have removable batteries. Listen to how her memory changes.

MEERA: It was my old blue phone. The back cover was tied with tape.

TARA: She had a blue phone like that.

DEVRAJ: Many people did.

TARA: Hers had white tape across the back. I put it there.

RAGHAV: A daughter repeating a detail does not prove force.

MOHAN: Why would Ma leave without us?

TARA: Mohan, don't call her that here.

MOHAN: You cannot stop me thinking it.

COMMITTEE HEAD: Children should be taken inside.

KAVITA: They are the children Raghav says she abandoned. Their presence is relevant.

RAGHAV: It is also cruel. Tara's school is close to the transport yard, isn't it? Public gossip can be difficult for a girl her age.

MEERA: Do not bring my daughter's school into your mouth.

RAGHAV: I was expressing concern.

KAVITA: Powerful men often call a warning concern. Continue with your account.

RAGHAV: Meera wanted work in Kota. She said her family watched every rupee. She said she wanted a life where nobody counted her steps.

MEERA: I wanted to buy Tara exam guides and Mohan new sandals. That is what I told you.

RAGHAV: You told me many things.

MEERA: Then tell them what happened after the bus stand.

RAGHAV: You left with Shahid.

MEERA: You put me in his vehicle.

RAGHAV: I introduced two adults. I did not put anyone anywhere.

KAVITA: Was money discussed?

RAGHAV: Transport costs money.

KAVITA: That was not my question.

RAGHAV: No illegal payment was discussed in my presence.

MEERA: He counted notes inside the vehicle.

RAGHAV: She was outside the vehicle. Now she was inside. Another change.

MEERA: I was beside the open door. I saw bundles under the front seat.

RAGHAV: Bundles can contain invoices. Transport businesses still use paper.

DEVRAJ: Meera, if this happened, why did you not return?

MEERA: Because I was not allowed to return.

DEVRAJ: For eighteen months? Not one message? Not one neighbour?

MEERA: Baba, I tried—

DEVRAJ: Do not use that word to make an answer disappear.

MOHAN: She came back. That is an answer.

VOICE IN CROWD: The boy has more sense than this committee.

SFX: wooden staff striking floor

COMMITTEE HEAD: Silence, or I will clear the courtyard.

KAVITA: Raghav, did you receive money for arranging Meera's travel?

RAGHAV: A small brokerage fee. Legal and ordinary.

KAVITA: How much?

RAGHAV: Two thousand rupees, perhaps. It was a long time ago.

MEERA: That is not the payment he is afraid of.

RAGHAV: There. An accusation without a number, a receipt or a witness.

KAVITA: Did Shahid pay you?

RAGHAV: No.

KAVITA: Did anyone connected to Shahid transfer money to you, your business or your relatives?

RAGHAV: You may inspect every account for which you have legal authority.

MEERA: Ask him when it happened.

KAVITA: When did what happen?

MEERA: The payment. He knows the date.

RAGHAV: This is becoming theatrical.

COMMITTEE HEAD: Then end the theatre. Give the committee the date of your two-thousand-rupee brokerage.

RAGHAV: I cannot remember an ordinary fee after eighteen months.

KAVITA: But you remember how long she sat, where she stood and what she said about her family.

RAGHAV: Because those details became important later.

MEERA: The larger payment was important immediately.

RAGHAV: There was no larger payment. The five lakh rupees were not even paid to me. They were paid on the third of May, twenty twenty-one.

KAVITA: Meera has not told police the amount. She only reported that money changed hands.

KAVITA: Yet you have just named the exact five-lakh payment date before Meera told police either the amount or the date.
