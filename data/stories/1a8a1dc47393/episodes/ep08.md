# Episode 8 — "The Number on the Complaint"

SFX: courtroom bell and handcuff chains

JUDGE: These men will not be returned to silence inside the old murder file.

SFX: official stamp strikes paper

COURT CLERK: Your Honour, the prison transfer order is ready.

JUDGE: Read it aloud. Their families have waited long enough to hear it.

COURT CLERK: All four accused are to be produced in the reopened identity inquiry.

NARESH: Does that mean we leave jail today?

JUDGE: It means the old accusation may no longer bury new evidence.

SALIM: And my missing statement?

JUDGE: The police will reconstruct its custody and record your account again.

IRFAN: In open court?

JUDGE: Under protection, if the investigating officer finds a credible risk.

IRFAN: I need protection for my brother, not for myself.

KAVITA: Give me his details before you return to custody.

PINTU: We came to court as four murderers.

NARESH: We are still in cuffs.

PINTU: Yes. But now the court has to ask who sent us there.

SFX: gallery erupts in cries and applause

WOMAN IN GALLERY: Naresh, your daughter can hear you!

SFX: gavel, three firm strikes

JUDGE: Order. The four accused will be brought into the reopened inquiry, not hidden inside the old case.

SFX: single paper slides into metal tray

NARRATOR: Before noon, an old missing-person complaint reached the court records tray. No named witness saw who placed it there.

SFX: court veranda crowd and approaching bus horn

COURT GUARD: Clear the doorway. The next matter cannot enter through your shoulders.

SFX: hurried footsteps, cloth bag buckle rattles

JANKI: Please, move. I came from the neighbouring district for the burned woman.

COURT GUARD: Which burned woman?

SFX: crowd murmurs rise

JANKI: The woman cremated as Meera. She may be my sister.

MEERA: What is your sister's name?

JANKI: Kesar.

MEERA: Come inside. Do not tell this story in a doorway.

JANKI: You are Meera?

MEERA: Yes.

JANKI: Then whose ashes did they give your family?

DEVRAJ: Nobody has established that those ashes belonged to her sister.

JANKI: Are you the man who identified the body?

DEVRAJ: I am her father.

JANKI: That was not my question.

SFX: side-room door opens

KAVITA: Janki, come to the side room. Bring every document you have.

SFX: paper envelope opens

JANKI: I brought her missing-person complaint and three photographs.

KAVITA: When did Kesar disappear?

JANKI: In June, twenty twenty-one. She left for a hospital appointment and never returned.

KAVITA: What made you connect her to this body?

JANKI: A court notice said the woman wore a black thread around her wrist.

DEVRAJ: Many women wear black thread.

JANKI: Kesar's had three knots and a small copper bead.

SFX: hospital file opens

KAVITA: The original hospital note mentions a black thread. It does not mention a bead.

JANKI: Then look at the photograph before they cut it away.

KAVITA: The photograph is disturbing. Confirm that you still want to view it.

JANKI: I have imagined worse every night for seventeen months. Show me.

MEERA: You don't have to prove your love by looking at a burned face.

JANKI: No. I have to prove the police searched under the wrong woman's name.

SFX: photograph sleeve slides across table

KAVITA: Only the wrist photograph first.

JANKI: The thread is twisted twice here. Kesar always did that after bathing.

DEVRAJ: That cannot identify a person.

JANKI: Neither could you, apparently.

DEVRAJ: You arrived ten minutes ago. Do not judge what that day was.

JANKI: My sister vanished. Your daughter returned. I know exactly what these days are.

SFX: file closes sharply

KAVITA: Enough. We test records, not grief against grief.

KAVITA: Did Kesar have a tattoo?

JANKI: No permanent tattoo. A childhood burn mark below her left thumb.

MEERA: The body photograph showed a tattoo on the hand opposite mine.

DEVRAJ: The skin was damaged. The mark was difficult to read.

JANKI: Was her face visible?

DEVRAJ: This interview is being conducted by the police.

JANKI: You answer very carefully for a grieving father.

KAVITA: Janki, what hospital appointment was Kesar attending?

JANKI: A wrist dressing. The burn had reopened after field work.

KAVITA: Which hospital?

JANKI: The district civil hospital. I wrote it in the complaint.

SFX: metal cupboard opens

KAVITA: Babulal, bring the unidentified-body intake sheet from the original file.

SFX: papers rustle inside plastic sleeve

BABULAL: The original is brittle. I will place it in a clear sleeve.

JANKI: Why is there a red line across the name box?

BABULAL: The patient arrived unidentified. Meera's name was added after Devraj's identification.

DEVRAJ: After police asked me to identify my daughter.

MEERA: You identified a thread and a damaged mark. Not me.

DEVRAJ: I identified what I could.

KAVITA: The intake sheet records one female patient, severe burns, no usable facial identification.

JANKI: Read the number at the top.

KAVITA: D H P, twenty-one, zero six one four, thirty-eight.

JANKI: No... read it once more.

KAVITA: D H P, twenty-one, zero six one four, thirty-eight.

JANKI: That number was written by the hospital clerk on Kesar's appointment slip.

SFX: paper pulled from metal tray

BABULAL: Your missing-person complaint is in the records tray. It has the same number in the reference box.

KAVITA: Show her the complaint.

JANKI: D H P, twenty-one, zero six one four, thirty-eight. This hospital intake number is the number on Kesar's missing-person complaint.
