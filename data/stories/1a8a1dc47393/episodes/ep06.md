# Episode 6 — Alive Before the Village

# Episode 6 — "Alive Before the Village"

SFX: crowd pressing against rope, police whistle

KAVITA: Nobody touches the evidence table. The first person who crosses this rope leaves the verification.

DEVRAJ: Then remove Mohan. He is already beside her chair.

MOHAN: I will sit with my Ma.

KAVITA: He may sit there. He may not touch the documents.

MEERA: I want my death certificate placed in front of me.

BABULAL: It is an official record, not an object for public handling.

KAVITA: Place it inside the transparent cover, Babulal.

BABULAL: If the cover tears, I will record who ordered this.

KAVITA: Record my designation correctly.

VOICE IN CROWD: Yesterday she was locked outside her house. Today her death certificate gets a police chair.

SECOND VOICE IN CROWD: Paper has always received more respect than women here.

DEVRAJ: This is supposed to be evidence, not entertainment.

KAVITA: Correct. That is why you will not interrupt each item.

SFX: sealed envelope sliding across floor

NARRATOR: One hour earlier, a sealed fingerprint envelope had fallen through the station slot into an empty records room. Nobody saw it arrive.

SFX: paper envelope placed on wooden table

NARRATOR: Now Kavita placed that envelope beside the certificate recording Meera as dead.

KAVITA: The open verification has five parts. Documents. Fingerprints. Family testimony. Physical identifiers. Contradictory records.

BABULAL: And no final declaration until every objection is entered.

KAVITA: Agreed. Read the first document.

BABULAL: School admission register. Student name, Meera. Father's name, Devraj. Original left-thumb impression attached.

DEVRAJ: That register is old. Ink spreads.

KAVITA: Your objection is noted before the result has even been opened.

MEERA: He is frightened.

DEVRAJ: I am frightened of another mistake being made in public.

MEERA: The first mistake was made in public too. You called a burned body your daughter, then called your living daughter a stranger.

VOICE IN CROWD: She has spoken the truth plainly.

DEVRAJ: You saw no face in that hospital either.

MEERA: I was not asked to identify the body.

KAVITA: Enough. Babulal, the second document.

BABULAL: Ration-card amendment form. Signed by Meera. Right-thumb impression taken two years before her disappearance.

KAVITA: The forensic unit compared both archived impressions with the fresh prints taken at the station.

DEVRAJ: Was the unit told what result the police expected?

KAVITA: The samples were coded. The examiner received no names.

TARA: What does the envelope say?

KAVITA: You may come closer, Tara. Do not cross the rope.

TARA: I can hear from here.

KAVITA: The fresh left-thumb impression matches the school register impression at fourteen corresponding ridge characteristics.

VOICE IN CROWD: Fourteen points. Say what that means.

KAVITA: It means the old and new left-thumb impressions were made by the same person.

MOHAN: Ma's thumb remembers her, even if Nana does not.

DEVRAJ: A child should not humiliate his grandfather before a crowd.

MOHAN: You humiliated Ma before a bigger crowd.

MEERA: Mohan, sit down.

MOHAN: I am sitting.

MEERA: Then sit quietly.

MOHAN: All right, Ma.

KAVITA: The right-thumb impression also matches the ration-card form.

BABULAL: Both archived documents existed before the disappearance. Their dates and storage entries are verified.

DEVRAJ: What about the Kota ledger? It carries her signature after the cremation.

KAVITA: That is the contradictory-record section. We will reach it.

DEVRAJ: Conveniently last.

KAVITA: Conveniently in the order I announced before you objected.

SFX: camera shutter clicks

KAVITA: Physical identifiers. Meera, show both hands.

MEERA: The tattoo is on my left hand. It has always been on my left hand.

BABULAL: The hospital photograph shows the burned body's tattoo beside the right wrist.

KAVITA: The photograph number and hospital intake number are legible. There is no reversal in the image.

DEVRAJ: The black thread looked identical.

KAVITA: A black wrist thread is not unique. A tattoo on the opposite hand is a material discrepancy.

MEERA: You did not touch the body, Baba. You would not even touch my hand at the station.

DEVRAJ: The body was burned. I was told not to disturb it.

KAVITA: Who told you?

DEVRAJ: A hospital worker. I don't... there were several people speaking at once.

KAVITA: Name or description?

DEVRAJ: No.

KAVITA: Then your statement remains that an unidentified worker discouraged a physical check.

DEVRAJ: I was grieving.

MEERA: And I was alive.

KAVITA: Family testimony. Tara, what did Meera know inside the house?

TARA: She knew where my first school ribbon was hidden.

DEVRAJ: Someone could have told her.

TARA: I did not tell anyone the tin was behind the loose brick.

KAVITA: Did she find it without directions?

TARA: Yes.

KAVITA: Do you recognise her as your mother?

TARA: I recognise things she knows. I am not ready to say more.

MEERA: She does not have to say more today.

KAVITA: Mohan?

MOHAN: She sang the sweater song. Only Ma knew the fourth head kisses both her cheeks.

KAVITA: Did she prompt you?

MOHAN: No. I tested her, and she finished it.

VOICE IN CROWD: He called her Ma in Kota. We all heard about it.

MOHAN: I will call her Ma here too. Ma. Ma. Ma.

KAVITA: The repetition is unnecessary, but the answer is recorded.

MEERA: Let him repeat it. They repeated that I was dead for eighteen months.

SFX: ledger pages turning

KAVITA: Now the Kota rental ledger.

BABULAL: The tenant name is Sarla. The signature appears to say Meera. The date is the twenty-first of June, twenty twenty-one.

KAVITA: Meera provided six fresh signatures under observation this morning.

DEVRAJ: A person can alter a signature.

KAVITA: Yes. That is why the examiner also compared letter formation, pressure and stroke order with older records.

TARA: And?

KAVITA: The Kota signature appears copied. Its strokes stop and restart where Meera's natural signature remains continuous.

MEERA: Someone signed my name.

KAVITA: That is the present forensic opinion. The ledger does not disprove your identity.

DEVRAJ: Opinion is not certainty.

KAVITA: Neither was your identification of the body, yet it was accepted as certainty.

BABULAL: Madam, the register cannot simply be rewritten during a village meeting.

KAVITA: I am not ordering a rewrite. I am ordering the death certificate marked disputed pending formal correction and further inquiry.

BABULAL: That requires recorded grounds.

KAVITA: Record them. Two fingerprint matches. Correct private family knowledge. A physical tattoo discrepancy. A copied rental signature. Consistent identification of the Kota room.

DEVRAJ: You are declaring her genuine because the crowd wants a miracle.

KAVITA: No. I am announcing that the evidence strongly supports Meera being alive and genuine.

VOICE IN CROWD: Say it again. Let the people at the back hear.

KAVITA: The woman standing here is strongly supported by documentary, fingerprint and family evidence as Meera.

MEERA: Babulal, bring me the paper that buried my name.

BABULAL: It remains government property.

MEERA: Then let the government correct itself where everyone can see.

BABULAL: I have a red disputed-record seal. Once stamped, the certificate cannot be used as an uncontested proof of death.

SFX: ink pad opening

KAVITA: Apply it.

DEVRAJ: Wait. If you stamp that, the original murder case—

KAVITA: Will be reviewed on evidence. Not protected from embarrassment.

BABULAL: The seal is inked.

SFX: heavy rubber stamp striking paper

MEERA: Do it.

KAVITA: The police officially acknowledge that the original identification of the burned body may have been wrong.

BABULAL: Meera's death certificate is now sealed and formally marked disputed.
