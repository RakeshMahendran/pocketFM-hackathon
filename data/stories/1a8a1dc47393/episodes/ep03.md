# Episode 3 — Behind the Locked Door

# Episode 3 — "Behind the Locked Door"

SFX: police jeep brakes, iron house gate rattles

MEERA: Open the gate. My children are inside that house.

SFX: jeep engine cuts out

KAVITA: You enter under police guard, and you do not force either child to approach you.

MEERA: I am not forcing them. I am coming home.

SFX: walking stick strikes stone

DEVRAJ: This has not been her home since the cremation.

MEERA: You burned another woman's body and gave away my room. Neither act changes whose house this is.

KAVITA: The body identification is under review. Do not state conclusions before the evidence does.

MEERA: Then let the evidence open the gate.

SFX: gate latch lifts from inside

TARA: I will open it.

MEERA: Tara.

TARA: Do not call me like that.

MEERA: Like what?

TARA: Like you have been searching every room and finally found me.

MEERA: That is exactly how I am calling you.

TARA: Sub-inspector, you said she was only coming to look at the house.

KAVITA: She is here to identify private details. You may stay, or you may leave the room.

TARA: I am staying.

MEERA: Where is Mohan?

TARA: He does not want to see you.

SFX: boy shouts behind wooden door

MOHAN: That is not what I said!

MEERA: Mohan? Open the door, little wheel.

MOHAN: No!

TARA: He locked himself in the storeroom when the jeep came.

MEERA: He is frightened.

TARA: He is confused. There is a difference.

DEVRAJ: This is why I refused. The boy has already mourned once.

MEERA: Because you told him I was ashes.

DEVRAJ: Because I identified his mother's body.

SFX: courtyard footsteps begin

KAVITA: Enough. Tara, show us the rooms without giving her clues.

TARA: Fine. If she is my mother, she should not need clues.

MEERA: I do not.

SFX: pigeons flutter under roof tiles

TARA: Then start here. What was kept in that wall niche?

MEERA: The brass lamp. Except during exams, when you hid your school timetable behind it.

TARA: Anybody could have seen the lamp.

MEERA: Nobody saw the sums you wrote on the wall behind the timetable. Seven times eight was fifty-four until I made you correct it.

TARA: I was small.

MEERA: You cried because the wrong answer looked neater.

DEVRAJ: Tara, you do not have to listen.

TARA: I said I am staying.

SFX: wooden threshold creaks

KAVITA: Next room.

TARA: What was under the bed?

SFX: small movement behind locked door

MEERA: A steel trunk, one broken sandal and Mohan whenever he stole jaggery.

MOHAN: I did not steal it.

MEERA: No. The jaggery climbed into your pocket by itself.

MOHAN: Maybe.

MEERA: Open the door. I will not come close. I only want to see how tall you are.

MOHAN: If I open it, will the police take you away?

MEERA: I don't... I cannot promise what the police will do. But I will not leave without telling you.

KAVITA: No one is taking her away today unless evidence requires it.

MOHAN: That is not a promise.

KAVITA: No. It is the truth.

MEERA: Mohan, remember the tune I used when your chest hurt?

TARA: Do not sing it.

MEERA: Why?

TARA: Because he wants you to be her. He will believe anything if you use her voice.

MEERA: My voice did not die because you were told I did.

TARA: You know stories. You know rooms. You even look like the photographs. But I will not call you mother.

MEERA: Not today?

TARA: Not until I decide.

DEVRAJ: You have heard her. This visit should end.

MEERA: No. My room is left.

DEVRAJ: That room is used for storage now.

MEERA: Then your storage is sleeping in my room.

SFX: key enters old lock

KAVITA: Open it.

DEVRAJ: There is nothing relevant inside.

SFX: lock turns, door groans open

KAVITA: You do not decide relevance.

SFX: footsteps enter dusty room

MEERA: The blue wall is gone.

TARA: It was painted after the funeral.

MEERA: My mirror was on that nail. The cot faced east. I kept Tara's first school ribbon inside the mirror frame.

TARA: The mirror broke years ago.

MEERA: Then the ribbon may still be behind the loose brick below the nail.

KAVITA: Tara, were you aware of that hiding place?

TARA: No.

DEVRAJ: There are dozens of loose bricks in an old house.

MEERA: Third brick from the corner. It has a white scratch shaped like a hook.

SFX: brick scrapes against plaster

KAVITA: Do not help her. I will check.

SFX: small tin box opens

TARA: There is something inside.

KAVITA: A faded school ribbon, blue and white.

MEERA: You cried on your first morning, Tara. So I cut my own blue scarf and tied a matching ribbon around my wrist.

TARA: A hidden object proves you knew this house. It does not prove why you left it.

MEERA: I did not leave you willingly.

DEVRAJ: There. Now comes the accusation that protects her from every question.

KAVITA: She has not given the allegation yet.

MEERA: I will. But not while he turns every word into shame.

TARA: Then answer me without him. Why did you not come back?

MEERA: Because I could not. Because every time I thought I had a road home, someone closed it. I tried to remember your faces exactly, but Mohan kept growing in my head and you... you stayed thirteen.

TARA: I was fourteen when you disappeared.

MEERA: No. Your birthday was eleven days later. I had hidden your present in the grain drum.

TARA: What present?

MEERA: Red glass bangles. You said you were too old for dolls and too young for gold. I never got to give them to you.

DEVRAJ: Tara, enough. Pain is not proof.

TARA: I know.

SFX: fingers touch door from inside

MOHAN: Is she crying?

TARA: Stay inside, Mohan.

MOHAN: I want to see her.

TARA: Then why did you lock the door?

MOHAN: Because if she is not Ma, I will lose her again.

MEERA: You do not have to open it today. I will sit outside.

DEVRAJ: You will not occupy this courtyard until the village accepts you.

KAVITA: The village does not issue identity certificates.

DEVRAJ: The village will still remember who brought scandal to its doors.

SFX: rapid knocking at outer gate

KAVITA: And it will remember who refused evidence when it was placed in his hand.

VOICE AT GATE: Police! I have come from the Kota bus stand. I need the officer handling the returned woman's case.

SFX: courtyard gate opens

KAVITA: Who are you?

SFX: paper photograph unfolds

BUS STAND VENDOR: I run the tea counter beside the departure bays. Her face was in today's paper.

MEERA: Where did you get that?

BUS STAND VENDOR: It was behind an old timetable at my counter. I remember the day because these two waited there for three hours.

KAVITA: Which two?

BUS STAND VENDOR: This photograph shows Meera standing beside Raghav.
