# Episode 2 — "The Kitchen Fire"

SFX: A bus door folding open. Gravel. A dog that does not get up.

CHAITRA: (to nobody, flat) Fourteenth of August, nineteen ninety-four.

SFX: A gate. Wet cloth being wrung out into a bucket in a yard.

SAVITHRI: You're early. Eat first.

CHAITRA: Amma.

SAVITHRI: There is rice. There is the huli from yesterday, it is better today.

CHAITRA: Amma, look at this paper.

SAVITHRI: Eat first, then paper.

SFX: A steel plate set down harder than it needs to be. A stool pulled.

CHAITRA: Three questions. That's all. One — whose name is Lokanath.

SAVITHRI: Your father's name.

CHAITRA: Two — where were you on the fourteenth of August, nineteen ninety-four.

SFX: The wringing stops.

CHAITRA: Three — why did the clerk at counter four say it in front of ninety people.

SAVITHRI: Give me the paper.

CHAITRA: You'll read it?

SAVITHRI: Give me the paper, Chaitra.

SFX: Paper changing hands. Two steps. A kitchen door. The dull whoomp of a wood fire being fed.

CHAITRA: Amma—

SFX: Paper catching. That specific sound, quick and greedy.

CHAITRA: (shouting) That is a certified copy! That is forty rupees and eleven days!

SAVITHRI: Then it is forty rupees.

CHAITRA: I am twenty-two years old and I stood first in the taluk and today a man read out that my father is dead. He was ashamed to say it and I was more ashamed to hear it. Do you understand what I am telling you? I was ashamed in front of ninety people.

SAVITHRI: Stir that. It will catch.

SFX: A ladle in a vessel. Deliberate. Wood shifting in the fire.

NARRATOR: Her mother had been married into that house in 1999. In twenty-three years she had never once been heard to say her husband's name aloud in front of a stranger. Remember that.

SFX: A cycle bell in the lane. A boy's chappals on the step.

PRAKASH: There's a man at the gate.

SAVITHRI: Who.

PRAKASH: Harsha's mother's brother. He's not coming in. He's standing outside with the tray.

SFX: Silence in the kitchen. The fire.

CHAITRA: The tray with the coconut?

PRAKASH: And the shawl. He says he'll wait at the gate only.

CHAITRA: Then he's returning it.

PRAKASH: Akka—

CHAITRA: He's returning it, Prakash. At the gate, so the lane can see. Nobody returns sweets inside a house.

SFX: The gate. A tray set down on the low wall. Footsteps that do not stop or turn.

SAVITHRI: (calling, steady) Tell your mother the huli was good this year.

SFX: A moped starting. Going.

PRAKASH: What did you do?

CHAITRA: I went to be verified.

SFX: A door. Bare feet on a floor that has been swept a thousand times. A trunk being dragged out from under a cot, the sound of steel on cement.

SAVITHRI: (from the yard, sharp) Chaitra.

CHAITRA: You burned mine. This one is yours.

SFX: The trunk hasp. Cloth. Old paper — the crackle of paper that has been folded in the same three places for decades.

CHAITRA: Ration card. Ration card. House tax. House tax. Amma's sari receipts, why do you keep sari receipts—

SFX: The paper sounds stop.

CHAITRA: (quiet) This is department letterhead.

SFX: One sheet, unfolded slowly.

CHAITRA: Order of appointment. Assistant teacher, government lower primary school. Nineteen ninety-four.

SFX: Footsteps in the doorway. Stopping there. Not coming closer.

NARRATOR: There is a photograph gummed to the top right corner of a departmental appointment order. Half an inch square. It is stamped across one edge so it cannot be lifted and swapped.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

CHAITRA: Amma. Come and stand where the light is.

SAVITHRI: Put it back.

CHAITRA: Come and stand where the light is and tell me why the photograph on my father's appointment order is not my father's face.
