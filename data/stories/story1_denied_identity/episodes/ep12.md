# Episode 12 — "Spelling"

SFX: A school hall with the benches pushed to the walls. A table carried in and set down. A jug of water. Forty adults not talking, which is louder than forty adults talking.

NAGARAJ: (flat, unhurried) Departmental enquiry, Government Higher Primary School, Nerlige. Present — enquiry officer, block education officer, headmaster, eight members of staff. Public are permitted to remain at the back since the school has been closed for the day. Anybody who speaks out of turn will be asked to leave and it will be recorded that they were asked. Is the almirah key here?

SHANKARAPPA: Sir. (a key on a table)

NAGARAJ: Open it at the end, not now. First the oral part. (paper) Article one. That the person working against post number two hundred and six of this school since November nineteen ninety-four is not the person appointed to it.

SFX: The specific sound of forty adults deciding not to look at each other.

NAGARAJ: Block education officer. Your office submitted a report in two thousand and nineteen on an anonymous complaint. The report says no irregularity.

PUTTARAJU: Sir, the report was prepared by the establishment section and put up to me for signature. Establishment section prepares, I sign. That is the system, sir. If I read every file that came for signature I would sign four files a day.

NAGARAJ: How many did you sign that day?

PUTTARAJU: (a pause) I don't remember, sir.

NAGARAJ: Sixty-one. It is in your own movement register. (writing) The block education officer states he signs what is put up to him. Spelling of your name, please.

PUTTARAJU: Sir?

NAGARAJ: Your name. Spell it. I write what people say and I spell it the way they spell it. It saves argument afterwards.

SFX: A man spelling his own name into a silence, and getting quieter with each letter.

NAGARAJ: Headmaster.

SHANKARAPPA: Sir. I stated it in writing on the seventh, registered post, acknowledgement due, and I will state it again here in front of my staff. I compared the joining photograph in the service register with the man in front of me in two thousand and eleven. I saw that they were not the same man. I locked the almirah. I did not report it for eleven years. I had a building sanction pending and I had a daughter to marry and I have nineteen months to retirement, and none of those three things is a reason, they are only the things I thought about instead.

SFX: Forty adults. A woman crying at the back, briefly, and stopping herself.

NAGARAJ: (writing) Noted. You understand you are article two.

SHANKARAPPA: I understand, sir.

NAGARAJ: (turning a page) Article one, then. The person against post two hundred and six. Stand.

SFX: A chair. One man standing, on a wooden floor.

NAGARAJ: Name.

MALLESHA: (nothing)

NAGARAJ: I will ask three times. It is procedure and it is also a courtesy, because the third one goes into the record as refusal. Name.

MALLESHA: (nothing)

SFX: Nothing. Long. A ceiling fan. Somebody's phone vibrating and being killed.

NAGARAJ: Third time. Your name.

MALLESHA: (very quietly) The fourth standard has arithmetic at nine forty. They are weak in carrying over. That is—

CHAITRA: (from the back of the hall) His name is Mallesha.

SFX: Forty heads. That sound.

NAGARAJ: Who is speaking?

CHAITRA: Chaitra. His daughter. I am not staff, I am the public at the back, and you said the public may remain.

NAGARAJ: I said remain. I did not say speak.

CHAITRA: Then ask me to leave, sir, and record that you asked me, because I am going to say it anyway and I would rather it was in the record twice.

SFX: A pen being set down.

NAGARAJ: (a beat) Come to the front.

SFX: Chappals on a wooden floor. Twenty steps, through forty people, and nobody moving out of the way quickly.

CHAITRA: (and she says it to the room, not to the table) His name is Mallesha. He was born in Nerlige. He passed the seventh standard from this building, in the room behind you, sir, the one with the broken window, and he was second in his class and the boy who was first is now a lorry driver in Mysuru and will say so. He has a training certificate and a second prize from the district workshop on multiplication methods, two thousand and nine, and the certificate is in a plastic cover in a trunk under a cot because it is made out to a dead man and he could never hang it on the wall.

GIRIJA: (from the staff row, and she is not gossiping, she is shaking) Nineteen years.

CHAITRA: Say it, teacher.

GIRIJA: Nineteen years I have taught in the next room and when this girl asked me his name I said Master. I said Master, because I did not know. (louder, to the room) His name is Mallesha!

SFX: Nothing. Long.

PUTTARAJU: Sir, this is becoming a function—

NAGARAJ: Sit down. (to Chaitra) Continue.

CHAITRA: Nine thousand six hundred children, sir. Four hundred a year, twenty-four years. The arithmetic of two taluks is in their hands and every one of them will tell you they were taught by Lokanath sir, and not one of them was. That is what has to go in the record. Not that a man took a name. That nine thousand six hundred children learnt to carry over from a man whose name they were never told.

NAGARAJ: (after a long moment; flat as ever) Is any of that disputed by anybody in this hall?

SFX: Nothing. Long. Forty adults.

NAGARAJ: Nobody. (paper; a pen) I am writing. The person working against post number two hundred and six states his name through his daughter and does not contradict her. Name recorded as Mallesha. Spelling.

CHAITRA: M, A, double L, E, S, H, A.

NAGARAJ: Village.

CHAITRA: Nerlige.

NAGARAJ: Father's name.

CHAITRA: (a pause) The same as the dead man's. They were brothers, sir. That is the whole of it. Two sons of one man, and one order of appointment.

SFX: A pen, writing, for longer than is comfortable. Then stopping.

NAGARAJ: Article one is admitted. That is not a small thing and I am not going to pretend to anybody in this hall that it is. There will be a criminal reference, there will be a recovery, and the recovery will be for the entire pay drawn against that post since November nineteen ninety-four. I am saying it now so that nobody in this family hears it first from a notice.

SFX: A chair, at the back. Savithri, standing up, and not saying anything, and sitting down again.

NAGARAJ: (sliding paper across a table) Now. The statement is written. It has to be signed by the person who made it. He made it through his daughter but the hand has to be his.

SFX: Paper on wood. A pen set on top of it, rolling half a turn and stopping.

NAGARAJ: Not the name that is on the register. The name that is in the statement. Sign your own name, please.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

MALLESHA: (and something goes out of his voice that has been in it for twenty-eight years) Sir. There is one difficulty.

NAGARAJ: Say it.

MALLESHA: I have signed six thousand pages in this department. Attendance, admission registers, transfer certificates, examination bundles, the mid-day meal stock book every single morning at eleven. Six thousand. And every one of them was signed slowly, because it was not my hand's name and a hand that is going fast makes its own name without asking permission.

SFX: A pen being picked up. The scrape of a chair being pulled closer to a table.

MALLESHA: I have not written my own name since the fourth of November, nineteen ninety-four. I don't know if the hand still has it.

SFX: A pen touching paper. Stopping. Touching again.

NAGARAJ: Take your time. Nobody in this hall is going anywhere.

SFX: Nothing. Long. Forty adults, and four hundred absent children, and a ceiling fan.

SFX: A pen, moving. Four letters. Stopping. Starting again from the beginning.
