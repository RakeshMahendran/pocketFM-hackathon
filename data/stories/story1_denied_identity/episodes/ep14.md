# Episode 14 — "Counter Four, Last Time"

SFX: A ceiling fan turning too slow to matter. Ninety people breathing in a corridor. A rubber stamp being set down on wood, and not picked up again.

BASAVARAJ: (to the corridor, uncomfortable) Nobody sits on the window ledge. This is a re-verification sitting, not a bus stand. Deputy director sir is coming at eleven.

VOICE IN QUEUE: Is it the Nerlige matter?

BASAVARAJ: It is the district's matter.

VOICE IN QUEUE: My sister was standing in this line the day they read it out.

BASAVARAJ: Then your sister may stay and she may keep her mouth shut, like the rest of us have been doing for twenty-four years.

SFX: Chairs. A table brought in. Water glasses. The specific hush of a government corridor that has decided something is going to happen in it.

REVANNA: Sit. Everybody sit. This is not a court, I am not a judge, and nothing said here binds anybody. It is a departmental sitting on a recruitment file, and I am holding it in this corridor and not in my chamber for one reason: because what was done to this candidate was done in this corridor, in front of these people, and I am told there were ninety of them.

SFX: Ninety people. A murmur that moves like water and stops.

REVANNA: Enquiry officer. Read the position.

NAGARAJ: (flat) Article one admitted. Article two admitted by the headmaster in writing and orally. The statement of the first division assistant, establishment section, was recorded on the nineteenth, in which he states he was the two thousand and nineteen informant and a recipient of monthly payment, and denies everything before September nineteen ninety-four. The candidate has asked to be heard on that denial. I have no objection. She is not a witness in my enquiry, so anything she says here she says in front of everybody, which is the only protection I can offer her.

REVANNA: Chaitra.

SFX: Chappals. Twenty steps through a corridor of ninety people, and this time they move out of the way.

CHAITRA: Three papers, sir. That is all I have and it is all I need.

REVANNA: Then hold up the first one.

CHAITRA: (and she is speaking to the corridor, at volume, because the volume is the point) One. The seva register of the Nerlige temple, volume four, page ninety-one. Twenty-fifth of August, nineteen ninety-four. Rites for the late appointee. Eighty people fed. Sixteen hundred and one rupees paid in cash on the day, and the payer's column — which the priest fills only when the payer is not the family — reads Thammanna, first division assistant, block education office.

THAMMANNA: (from a chair, pleasantly) A priest's book, amma.

CHAITRA: Sir has already recorded that you called it that. Two.

SFX: Paper, unfolded, thick — the crackle of stamp paper.

CHAITRA: General power of attorney, registered at the sub-registrar's office, Hosakere, two thousand and eleven. Executant — my mother. And a registered document, sir, as you know better than I do, is not valid on the executant's signature alone. It requires an identifying witness. A person who stands in front of the sub-registrar and certifies, in his own hand, who these people are.

SFX: Nothing. Long. A fan.

CHAITRA: The identifying witness on that deed describes my mother as — and I am reading it exactly — wife of Lokanath, assistant teacher, Government Higher Primary School, Nerlige, known to me personally. Signed and dated the nineteenth of June, two thousand and eleven.

REVANNA: (very quietly) Seventeen years after that man died.

CHAITRA: Seventeen years, sir. In the state's own register. Not a priest's book. Not a family's story. A registered instrument in a sub-registrar's strong room, in which a serving government servant personally certified the identity of a dead man — and then, on the fourth of this month, used that same instrument to sell four acres by the canal, sale deed one one four seven, and the mutation was entered at ten in the morning by the village accountant of Nerlige, who is standing at the back of this corridor and who will say so.

KEMPANNA: (from the back, seventy-one and unhappy) I will say so. I entered it. Paper came, I wrote. Forty-one years and this is the only entry of mine I want reopened.

SFX: A murmur, ninety strong.

THAMMANNA: (still pleasant, but for the first time he is speaking faster than he wants to) Sir, an identification is a formality, everybody does identifications, I have identified two hundred people at that office—

CHAITRA: Then two hundred people will be checked. Three.

SFX: A hardbound receipt book, dropped flat on a table.

CHAITRA: The counterfoils he produced himself. Two hundred and eighty-eight entries. He told the enquiry officer that every one bears the signature of the person who paid, and that is true, and I have never once disputed it. But the signature is four words at the bottom of an entry, and above every signature there are nine lines of writing — date, serial, purpose, amount in figures, amount in words. Two hundred and eighty-eight times, in one hand, over twenty-four years.

REVANNA: Whose hand?

CHAITRA: The same hand that wrote the inward entry on my correction application on the nineteenth of last month, sir. I have the office copy. It says: received from — daughter of Lokanath, assistant teacher, Nerlige. Written in his own hand, in his own inward register, this month. Three years after he says he informed the department that no such person existed.

SFX: Nothing. Long. Ninety people.

REVANNA: (flatly) Enquiry officer.

NAGARAJ: Sir. On the identification alone, this is not a departmental matter any more. It is section four sixty-five and four seventy-one and it is a registered document, which means the sub-registrar has to move it and not me. The Hosakere police have a head constable in this building since morning because I asked for one on Tuesday.

THAMMANNA: (and the warmth is gone, and what is underneath is very small and very ordinary) Sir. Thirty-one years. I have never once been transferred.

REVANNA: I know. I have spent four years wondering why. (to the corridor) Head constable.

SFX: Boots. Not many. Just two men crossing a corridor, which is somehow worse. A chair pushed back. A man being asked to stand up, in front of ninety people, by somebody who does not raise his voice.

SFX: Ninety people. And this time the murmur does not stop; it goes on for a while.

NARRATOR: He had spent thirty-one years making sure nobody in that district ever had to spell his name. In the end they spelled it twice — once in the enquiry record, and once on the arrest memo, and the second one was read out.

SFX: A corridor settling. A fan.

REVANNA: Sit down, all of you. There is a list and I am going to go through it, because I am told by a young lady from a Kannada daily that if I do not settle every single item today then this becomes a series and not a story, and I have no wish to be a series.

INCHARA: (from somewhere in the ninety, unabashed) Above the fold, sir.

REVANNA: (dry) One. The sale deed of the fourth and the mutation of the nineteenth are referred for cancellation, the identification being of a deceased person. The four acres by the canal stand restored to the entry that stood before, and Kempanna will make that entry today and it will be the last one of his career.

KEMPANNA: (very quietly) Thank you, sir.

REVANNA: Two. The family pension already sanctioned to the widow of the appointee stands, and the arrears — which I told her would take four years — are recommended for release out of the sum recoverable from the first division assistant, and not from the family, because the money went to his house and not to theirs.

RATNAMMA: (from the second row, standing, twenty-eight years of counting in her voice) Sir. Say the number of the order.

REVANNA: Nine four seven stroke twenty-two.

RATNAMMA: (to the corridor, to nobody, to everybody) Nine four seven stroke twenty-two.

REVANNA: Three. The headmaster of Nerlige is placed under censure, with the entry recorded permanently in his service book, and he retires in nineteen months with that in it and nothing removed. And the second building block sanctioned to that school in two thousand and eighteen and pending since — the file is in my office and I signed it at nine o'clock this morning, because eleven years of a man's silence is his to answer for and four hundred children's classrooms are not.

SHANKARAPPA: (a long, audible sigh, the last one) Sir.

REVANNA: Four. The block education officer is suspended pending enquiry, and he may explain sixty-one files to somebody senior to me.

SFX: A chair, somewhere, and a man leaving a corridor without being asked to.

REVANNA: Five. The person who served against post two hundred and six. (a pause) There is no version of this where he is not prosecuted, and I am not going to stand in a corridor and pretend otherwise to a family. He will be dismissed from service, the criminal reference goes today, and the recovery stands — reduced to the pay of the twenty-four years less the amount proved to have been extorted from him, which the enquiry officer puts at three lakh forty-five thousand, six hundred rupees. That is the law and the law is not a kindness.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

REVANNA: But there is a service record to be closed, and a service record has to be closed in some name. Enquiry officer — read what he signed.

NAGARAJ: (paper) Statement of the eighteenth, signed at the foot in a hand that took four minutes. Mallesha. Son of Nerlige. Assistant teacher, twenty-four years, nine thousand six hundred children, no leave taken beyond entitlement in the whole period, one district prize, two thousand and nine, multiplication methods.

SFX: A woman's voice from the back of the corridor. Not loud. But it is the first time in the story that it has been used on a stranger.

SAVITHRI: My husband's name is Mallesha.

SFX: Ninety people turning.

SAVITHRI: (again, and it is steadier) I am Savithri and I have been married twenty-three years to a man called Mallesha and I have never said it in front of people and I am saying it now.

PRAKASH: (beside her, seventeen and furious with the whole world) And nobody told me anything until nine days ago and I want that in the record too.

REVANNA: (a beat) Put it in the record.

SFX: A pen.

SFX: Footsteps at the corridor door. A woman's, and behind her the small awkward sound of a steel tray being carried by someone who does not want to be carrying it.

HARSHA: (miserable, apologising before he speaks, as he always does) Sorry — sorry, I know this is not — Amma wanted to — we brought the tray back. The coconut and the shawl. She says it was a misunderstanding in our house and she wants to give it at the gate again properly, in front of people, and she has come in an auto and she is waiting outside because she is ashamed to come in.

SFX: Nothing. Long. Ninety people, enjoying this considerably more than they should.

CHAITRA: Harsha.

HARSHA: Yes.

CHAITRA: When it was returned, it was returned at the gate, so the lane could see. That was your mother's choice and it was a good choice, she knew exactly what she was doing. So I will answer the same way she asked. In front of ninety people. (clearly) No.

HARSHA: (after a moment) ... Okay.

CHAITRA: Not because of you. Because on the fourth of the month before last a man read a date out in this corridor and everyone in my house went quiet, and I have spent sixty days finding out that quiet is what this family is made of. I am finished being quiet, and I am not going to be married into a house that sends a tray back at a gate. Tell your mother the huli was good this year.

SFX: A tray, set down on a bench. Footsteps leaving, unhurried, not humiliated — just leaving.

REVANNA: (dry) Is that item on my list?

CHAITRA: No sir. That one was mine.

SFX: A file being opened. The specific sound of a stapled list being flattened with a palm.

REVANNA: Last item, then. Basavaraj.

BASAVARAJ: Sir.

REVANNA: The candidate's six documents were re-verified on the twenty-ninth. Birth entry corrected on the second, column four, by order of the tahsildar. Certified copy of the study certificate, forty rupees, eleven days, received. The appointment list of this district was approved by me this morning. You read the rejection in this corridor on the sixteenth. Read the list.

SFX: A man standing up behind a counter. A chair pushed back. Ninety people going quiet in one movement, the way a corridor does.

BASAVARAJ: (and he takes his time, because he has been at that counter for twenty-two years and he knows exactly what this is) Appointment list. Assistant teachers. District office. Serial one to nine.

SFX: Paper.

BASAVARAJ: Serials one to eight I will read after. I am reading serial nine first and the deputy director sir can take it out of my confidential report if he wants.

REVANNA: Read it.

BASAVARAJ: Twenty-two years I have stood at counter four. In all twenty-two years no register in this district has carried that man's own name. Not one line. Not one column. Not on a school roll, not on a ration card, not on his own daughter's birth entry. It carries it now, and I am the one reading it out, and I read the other thing out too, so this is only fair.

SFX: A page, held. Ninety people.

BASAVARAJ: Serial nine. Assistant teacher, Government Higher Primary School, Nerlige. Chaitra — daughter of Mallesha.
