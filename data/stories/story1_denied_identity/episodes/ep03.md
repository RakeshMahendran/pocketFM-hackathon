# Episode 3 — "The Seventh"

SFX: A cot creaking under a man's weight at five in the morning. Bare feet. A steel tumbler filled from a pot, and the long swallow.

CHAITRA: You slept in the cowshed.

MALLESHA: The buffalo is close to calving. Somebody has to be there.

CHAITRA: The light was on in there all night. Nobody watches a buffalo with a light on.

MALLESHA: (a pause of exactly two seconds) Sums were being corrected. Fourth standard. Forty-one notebooks.

CHAITRA: Look where the light is and tell me whose photograph that is.

MALLESHA: The school gate opens at nine. That is all.

SFX: Chappals. A door. The gate, and it closes properly, the way a man closes a gate when he does not want to be followed.

NARRATOR: In twenty-four years he had never once been late. Not for a strike, not for a flood, not for his own mother's fortieth-day ceremony, which he attended at four in the afternoon after school hours. The department has a word for men like that. The word is regular.

SFX: Morning yard. A broom on cement. Crows.

PRAKASH: Akka. The date.

CHAITRA: What date.

PRAKASH: It's the seventh.

CHAITRA: So?

PRAKASH: You've never noticed? Every month on the seventh, Amma keeps twelve hundred rupees folded in the rice tin. Not in her purse. In the rice tin. And on the seventh she sends me to Nanjappa's shop for something we don't need.

SFX: The broom stopping.

CHAITRA: Since when.

PRAKASH: Since I was small enough to be sent.

SFX: A moped, four-stroke, slowing in the lane. Cutting out. The stand going down with a click.

SAVITHRI: (from inside, fast, low) Prakash. Shop. Now.

PRAKASH: I'm not going.

SAVITHRI: Prakash.

CHAITRA: He's not going. Neither am I.

SFX: The gate. Slippers — leather, not rubber — on the path. A throat cleared politely.

THAMMANNA: Savithramma. Good morning, good morning. The buffalo has not calved yet, isn't it. Nanjappa was saying.

SAVITHRI: (from the doorway, formal) Sir. Sit. I'll bring coffee.

THAMMANNA: No coffee, no coffee. Two minutes only. Establishment section is very busy this month, the audit circular has come, all schools, thirty-one years of registers. Very tiresome for everybody.

SFX: A plastic chair being unfolded on the veranda.

THAMMANNA: And this is the daughter. The topper. Congratulations, amma. First in the taluk, everybody was saying at the office. Everybody.

CHAITRA: You work at the block office.

THAMMANNA: Establishment. First division assistant. Small chair, small table.

CHAITRA: Then you can tell me why counter four in the district office has my father marked deceased.

SFX: Nothing. Long. A crow.

THAMMANNA: (unhurried, warm) Deceased? These computers, amma. Last year they marked one headmaster in Hosakere as female. Retired man, sixty years, mustache like this. Female. We laughed for one week.

CHAITRA: They didn't laugh at me.

THAMMANNA: No. At the district office they don't laugh. Different culture there. (to Savithri) Savithramma, the rice tin.

SFX: Silence. Then movement inside. A steel lid coming off. Coming back on.

SFX: Notes being counted, thumb and finger, the small dry sound of it. Twelve times.

THAMMANNA: Correct. And the book.

SFX: A hardbound receipt book opening flat. A page turning. A pen uncapped.

THAMMANNA: Master will sign in the evening as usual. Tell him the same page, below the last one, not on a fresh page, the fresh page is for next month. He knows.

CHAITRA: What is he signing for?

THAMMANNA: Welfare fund, amma. Teachers' welfare. Death benefit, marriage assistance, all that.

CHAITRA: Twelve hundred rupees a month for a welfare fund, in cash, at our gate, on the seventh, for how many years?

THAMMANNA: (pleasant) You are a teacher now, no. Almost. Teachers ask like that. It is a good habit.

SFX: The plastic chair. A man standing. Slippers on stone.

THAMMANNA: Savithramma. That four acres at Nerlige — the land by the canal, the survey number ending in eleven — you have not done the mutation yet, isn't it. After the old lady passed. It is still in her name.

SAVITHRI: There is time.

THAMMANNA: There is time. Of course. Only, mutation needs the son's identity documents, and identity documents come from the establishment section, and the establishment section is myself. (a small laugh) It is a small district, amma. Everything comes back to a small table.

CHAITRA: Are you threatening my mother?

THAMMANNA: I am telling her the procedure. (to Savithri, gently) The huli smells good. Send some.

SFX: The moped. The stand coming up. It does not start immediately; he lets it sit.

THAMMANNA: (over the idle) Amma. One thing. At the district office, when they read something out — don't ask them to read it twice. Once, everybody hears and forgets. Twice, they write it down.

SFX: The moped going. Fading into the lane. Gone.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

SAVITHRI: Go inside.

CHAITRA: He left the book.

SAVITHRI: Chaitra. Go inside.

SFX: Two steps on stone. A hardbound book being picked up off the step. Pages — a lot of them — riffling backwards under a thumb.

NARRATOR: Two hundred and eighty-eight entries. She counted them twice, standing on the step, because the first count seemed to her like a mistake.

CHAITRA: Twelve hundred. Twelve hundred. Twelve hundred. Nineteen ninety-eight, ninety-nine, two thousand — Amma, this book starts before I was born.

SAVITHRI: Put it down.

CHAITRA: Every single line is signed.

SAVITHRI: Put it down.

CHAITRA: They are signed by Appa. Two hundred and eighty-eight times, in a hand I have never once seen him use.
