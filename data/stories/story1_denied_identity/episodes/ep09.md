# Episode 9 — "Medical Grounds"

SFX: A staff room. One table fan. A kettle on a spirit stove. Chalk being broken in half — that dry snap.

GIRIJA: (to the room, not to him) Some people write a leave letter for one day of fever and take three drafts. Some people write nothing for nineteen years and then write on a Saturday.

SFX: A fountain pen. Slow. The slowest writing sound there is.

GIRIJA: Master. Whatever you are writing, write it after the second bell. There is a substitute needed in fifth standard.

MALLESHA: Fifth standard has been given the twelve-times table to copy four times. It will hold until the second bell. That is all.

SFX: The pen. A page being blotted with the side of a hand.

NARRATOR: He wrote it in the way he had written six thousand pages: leaning too far forward, left hand flat on the desk, and slower than his hand wanted to go, because for twenty-eight years the hand had been going at a speed that was not its own.

SFX: Footsteps in the doorway. A girl's, fast, out of breath from the road.

CHAITRA: Appa.

GIRIJA: Chaitra! You cannot keep coming into this school like—

CHAITRA: Teacher, please, one minute.

GIRIJA: (a beat; then, unexpectedly, standing) One minute. (at the door, to the corridor) Yogesh! Come, we will check the drinking water tank. Now. Both of us. It is a long tank.

SFX: Two pairs of chappals leaving. A door pulled to, not shut.

CHAITRA: (low) What is that paper.

MALLESHA: A letter.

CHAITRA: Read it to me.

MALLESHA: (evenly) To the block education officer, through proper channel. Subject — request for voluntary retirement on medical grounds. Sir, the undersigned is suffering from continuous giddiness and reduced vision in the right eye since eight months and is unable to discharge duties to the satisfaction of the department. It is requested that the undersigned may kindly be relieved with effect from the third. Thanking you.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

CHAITRA: There's nothing wrong with your eye.

MALLESHA: The eye is not the subject of the letter.

CHAITRA: And the signature at the bottom?

MALLESHA: (nothing)

CHAITRA: Say it. You can write it but you can't say it, is that the arrangement? Twenty-four years and you can put it on paper two hundred and eighty-eight times but you cannot say it in a staff room to your own daughter.

MALLESHA: The letter is signed the way the letter has always been signed.

CHAITRA: (breaking) Lokanath. You are resigning as Lokanath.

MALLESHA: Yes.

CHAITRA: Do you know what that is? That is not a resignation, Appa, that is one more forgery, that is number two hundred and eighty-nine, and he told me so himself, he sat behind his table with the fan pointed at his own face and he told me exactly why he wants it in the old name — because a resignation in your own name is a confession and a confession is no use to him! You are giving him the four acres and you are giving him the post for his son-in-law and you are giving him twenty-four more years of quiet, and what do you get?

MALLESHA: You get a file with nothing on it.

SFX: Nothing. Long. The kettle beginning to sound, then taken off.

CHAITRA: I don't want it like that.

MALLESHA: (and this is as close as he comes, all season, to raising his voice, which is to say it goes lower) On the fourteenth of January, two thousand, a man went to the registrar with a newborn and he was asked for a name for column four, and there was one name that could be given and one name that could not, and he gave the one that could. Everything since then has followed from that morning. There is no version of this where the daughter is clean and the father is also standing. There has never been one. That is all.

SFX: An envelope. A flap being wetted. Pressed down with a thumb.

SFX: A corridor. A door with a nameplate that rattles when knocked.

SHANKARAPPA: Come. (a chair) Ah. Master. Sit. And — Chaitra amma. (a long sigh, audible, the sigh of a man with nineteen months to retirement) Sit, sit.

SFX: An envelope placed on a desk. Pushed across.

SHANKARAPPA: What is this.

MALLESHA: Voluntary retirement, sir. Medical grounds. Through proper channel. It requires the headmaster's forwarding endorsement on the reverse.

SFX: An envelope opened. Paper. A very long pause.

SHANKARAPPA: Giddiness and reduced vision in the right eye.

MALLESHA: Since eight months, sir.

SHANKARAPPA: In March you spotted a printing error in the fourth standard textbook, page sixty-one, the figure of a triangle. From the back of the room. I was standing next to you.

MALLESHA: (nothing)

SHANKARAPPA: (setting the paper down) No.

CHAITRA: Sir?

SHANKARAPPA: I will not forward it.

MALLESHA: Sir, procedure is that the headmaster forwards and the block office decides—

SHANKARAPPA: Do not tell me procedure. (and he is angry, which nobody in that school has heard) Twenty-four years you have taught in this department and eleven in this school and you have never once asked me for anything. Not a casual leave in advance, not a good posting, not the town school when your girl was in tenth. Other people bring me sweets and ask. You bring me nothing and ask nothing. Do you know what a man like me thinks about a man like that, for eleven years? He thinks: that one is frightened of something. And I never asked what, because as per procedure it is not my subject.

SFX: Nothing. Long. A ceiling fan.

SHANKARAPPA: And then on Thursday morning four hundred children heard a name read out twice.

CHAITRA: Sir, that was my doing—

SHANKARAPPA: It was not your doing, amma. It was my doing, in two thousand and eleven, when your father joined this school and I compared the joining photograph in the service register with the man standing in my office, and I saw what I saw, and I put the register back in the almirah and locked it, because I had a building sanction pending and a daughter's marriage and nineteen years to retirement. I have carried it since two thousand and eleven. Eleven years. So do not tell me whose doing it is.

SFX: A drawer. A key. A steel almirah opening — that boom.

MALLESHA: Sir. Whatever is in there — the school will need a substitute for fourth standard arithmetic at nine forty. They are weak in carrying over. That is all.

SHANKARAPPA: Master. Sit down. You have been saying that sentence to me for eleven years and I have finally understood that it is what you say when you are frightened.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

CHAITRA: Sir. The audit team sits on the fifth. If that register goes to them, they will find—

SHANKARAPPA: The register did not go to the audit team.

CHAITRA: Thammanna requisitioned the file at the district office on Monday. Nine twenty in the morning, purpose column pre-audit scrutiny, I saw the movement register with my own—

SHANKARAPPA: He requisitioned the district office copy. The school copy is in this almirah and has been in this almirah since two thousand and eleven and the key has been in my pocket every single day, including Sundays, including the day of my daughter's marriage. (paper, thick, lifted) And this morning at seven fifteen I went to the post office at Hosakere myself, before school, because the post office at Nerlige is Thammanna's wife's brother's.

CHAITRA: Sir. What did you post.

SHANKARAPPA: (and the anger is gone; what is left underneath it is very tired) Amma. I did not post a resignation. I have not read the letter your father wrote until three minutes ago, and by then it was already too late for it to matter.

CHAITRA: Sir, what did you post?

SFX: A postal receipt being unfolded. Thin paper, the kind that tears if you look at it.

SHANKARAPPA: Registered acknowledgement due, to the deputy director of public instruction, seven forty-one this morning. A statement in my own hand naming which man in this school has been signing the staff attendance register since nineteen ninety-eight, and stating that the headmaster of this school has known it since two thousand and eleven and did not report it.
