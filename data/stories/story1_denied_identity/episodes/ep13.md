# Episode 13 — "The Fourth"

SFX: A cycle thrown down on gravel, not parked. Running on a village road. A gate.

KEMPANNA: Amma, don't shout at me, I am seventy-one and I have a form to fill.

CHAITRA: You are the village accountant. You keep the mutation register for Nerlige. Look at me and tell me what you have just entered.

KEMPANNA: Survey number ending eleven. Four acres by the canal. Transfer by sale. Entry made this morning at ten.

CHAITRA: That land is in my grandmother's name.

KEMPANNA: It was in your grandmother's name until the fourth. On the fourth it was registered at the sub-registrar's office, Hosakere, sale deed number one one four seven, and a registered deed is a registered deed, amma, I only copy what comes. Paper comes to me, I write. That is the whole of my job and I have done it for forty-one years and I would like to finish it without a single entry of mine being reopened.

CHAITRA: The fourth was fifteen days ago. Nobody in my house signed anything fifteen days ago.

KEMPANNA: (mildly) Somebody in your house signed something in two thousand and eleven.

SFX: Nothing. Long. A crow.

CHAITRA: Say that again.

KEMPANNA: General power of attorney. Executed two thousand and eleven, registered, stamp paper of one hundred rupees, executant — Savithri, wife of the assistant teacher. Irrevocable, for all purposes relating to the said survey number. It has been sitting in a file for eleven years and on the fourth somebody took it out and used it.

SFX: A house. A kitchen. A vessel set down very carefully.

SAVITHRI: (before she is asked) Two thousand and eleven.

CHAITRA: Amma.

SAVITHRI: Your brother was six. He had the fever that year, the one that went to his chest, and the doctor in the town wanted eleven thousand rupees and we had four. Thammanna came on the seventh as usual and he sat in that chair and he said, Savithramma, there is a paper that costs nothing to sign and it does nothing unless it is needed. (a pause) I asked him what it does if it is needed. He said, amma, it means that one day, if some officer asks for the husband's signature and the husband cannot give it, the wife's signature will do instead. And I thought — I thought that was mercy. I thought he was giving us a way that did not need that name on it.

CHAITRA: You thought he was protecting Appa.

SAVITHRI: (flat) I was nineteen when they married me into this house and thirty-one when I signed that paper and I have never in my life read a document to the end. I signed it, and the boy got the eleven thousand rupees, and the boy is seventeen and standing in that doorway right now, and I would sign it again. That is the honest answer and I am tired of giving the other kind.

SFX: A boy in a doorway, not saying anything.

PRAKASH: (finally) You never told me it was for me.

SAVITHRI: Now you are told.

SFX: A block office corridor. Four fans, none in time. A photocopier. The hydraulic closer that does not close.

INCHARA: (fast, low) Don't go in there. Chaitra. Listen to me for once. The enquiry officer is in there taking Thammanna's statement and if you walk in—

CHAITRA: He sold four acres nine days ago.

INCHARA: I know. I have the deed number. I also have this, and you need to look at it before you open that door, because after you open that door you will not be thinking clearly for two days—

SFX: A door. A room of steel almirahs. A table fan pointed at one man.

NAGARAJ: (flat) This is a recorded statement. Who is at the door.

CHAITRA: Chaitra. Daughter of Mallesha.

THAMMANNA: (warm, immediate, delighted) Chaitramma! Sit, sit. Ramesha, one coffee. (to Nagaraj) Sir, this is the girl I was telling you about. Topper. She will make a very good teacher one day, I have said it from the beginning.

NAGARAJ: (to Chaitra) Stand at the wall. Do not speak. (to Thammanna) You were at paragraph nine.

THAMMANNA: (and his voice does not change at all; that is the entire horror of it) Paragraph nine. Sir, in September nineteen ninety-four, this humble servant of the department was deputed to Nerlige regarding a joining report that had not been received. At the house, this humble servant was approached by the younger brother of the deceased appointee, who was in a very bad condition, mortgage notice on the door, mother not eating. He requested that the joining report may be accepted. Sir, this humble servant refused.

CHAITRA: (from the wall) You—

NAGARAJ: Wall.

THAMMANNA: This humble servant refused, and this humble servant was thereafter offered money, and being a poor man with a small chair, this humble servant is ashamed to say that he did not refuse a second time. Twelve hundred rupees per month was paid to me by that family, in cash, at their gate, on the seventh, for twenty-four years, and I have brought the receipt book counterfoils, sir, they are here, two hundred and eighty-eight entries, every one bearing the signature of the person who paid.

SFX: A hardbound book set on a table. Pages, riffled with a thumb.

CHAITRA: (very quietly) That book was on our step.

THAMMANNA: It was on your step, amma, because a receipt book stays with the person who pays until the last page, and then it comes back to the office. (to Nagaraj) Sir, and in two thousand and nineteen, my conscience being what it is, I addressed a complaint to the deputy director of public instruction regarding this very matter. It was marked back to my own office for report, which is the tragedy of a small chair, and I was directed by my superior officer to prepare a nil report, which I prepared, and which he signed. Sixty-one files that day, he says. He signed all sixty-one.

NAGARAJ: (writing) You are stating that you were the informant.

THAMMANNA: I am the informant, sir. Three years ago. Before this family discovered its conscience.

SFX: Nothing. Long. A table fan turning away and coming back.

CHAITRA: Sir. Enquiry officer. May I say one line.

NAGARAJ: One.

CHAITRA: Ask him who paid for the funeral.

SFX: Nothing.

NAGARAJ: Whose funeral?

CHAITRA: The appointee's. Twenty-fifth of August, nineteen ninety-four. Eighty people fed, sixteen hundred and one rupees, paid in cash on the day. Ask him whose name is in the payer's column of the temple's own book, three weeks before he was ever deputed to that village.

SFX: A pen stopping.

THAMMANNA: (unhurried, pleasant, without one second of hesitation) A priest's book, sir. Not a record. Old man, eighty-four, writes with a nib, has been putting down what people tell him for forty-three years. I have given dakshine at forty funerals in this taluk, amma. It is called being from the place.

NAGARAJ: (after a moment; and it is the flatness that is terrible) Noted as denied. (writing) Spelling of your name, please.

THAMMANNA: T, H, A, double M, A, N, N, A. (a small laugh) Sir, one point only, for the record. Nobody has to spell my name in this district. That is why I have never been transferred.

SFX: A corridor. Two women walking fast. A photocopier. A door pushed open into hot air outside.

INCHARA: Chaitra. Stop. Look at the paper I was trying to give you.

CHAITRA: (breathing badly) The land is gone, the receipt book is his, he is the informant, he is the whistleblower, my father is the briber and my mother is the executant and I am the beneficiary. He built the whole thing in nineteen ninety-four and he has just been paid twenty-four years and he will walk out of that room and be given a certificate for alertness—

INCHARA: Chaitra. Look at the paper.

SFX: Nothing. A photocopy, warm, unfolded.

CHAITRA: What is this.

INCHARA: Recovery notice. It went to your house by post this morning; your brother gave it to me at the gate because your mother would not open it. Read the figure.

SFX: Paper.

CHAITRA: (and her voice goes flat) Recovery of pay and allowances drawn against post number two hundred and six from November nineteen ninety-four. One crore, eleven lakh, forty thousand rupees. Recoverable from the person and from his legal heirs.

INCHARA: Now the second sheet. That's the one I've had since Tuesday and couldn't get to you. It is the file copy of the two thousand and nineteen complaint — the anonymous one, the one everybody has been calling anonymous for three years. The office keeps the office copy of every anonymous complaint with the covering slip attached, because that is the beauty of that office, nothing disappears, everything is entered.

CHAITRA: The covering slip has a signature.

INCHARA: The covering slip has a signature.

SFX: Paper, held very still. A photocopier somewhere down the corridor, running and running.

CHAITRA: He wasn't lying. He did write it. In two thousand and nineteen he wrote the complaint that started all of this, and he signed it in his own hand, and then he wrote the report that buried it, and then he waited three years for the right month — and the right month was the month my verification came up.
