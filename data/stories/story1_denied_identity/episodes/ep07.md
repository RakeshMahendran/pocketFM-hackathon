# Episode 7 — "Inward Register"

SFX: A block office corridor. Ceiling fans, four of them, none in time with each other. A photocopier that has been repaired too often. Somebody's mobile playing a devotional ringtone twice before it is answered.

CHAITRA: Application for correction of entry in birth register, form fifteen, with affidavit. Where does it go.

CLERK VOICE: Establishment.

CHAITRA: It's not an establishment matter. It's a birth record.

CLERK VOICE: Correction of a name that touches a serving employee's file is establishment. Second door. After the tea trolley.

SFX: Chappals on a corridor floor that was mopped with too much water. A steel trolley, glasses.

INCHARA: (falling into step, fast, no preamble) You're Chaitra? Nerlige? Taluk topper, verification rejected Tuesday, counter four — was it Basavaraj at the counter or the other one, the fat one, and did he read the rejection out or did he write it, because those are two different things legally and I need to know which.

CHAITRA: Who are you.

INCHARA: Inchara. District stringer. Not staff, don't get excited, I get paid by the column centimetre and I have never once been paid on time. Was it read out?

CHAITRA: Move.

INCHARA: Ninety people in that corridor and eleven of them are from my taluk, so I already know it was read out, I'm only checking whether you'll tell me the truth about a thing I already know. That's how I decide about people. You passed. Second question—

CHAITRA: I said move.

INCHARA: — why does a village accountant's family tree register for Nerlige have a man who died in ninety-four and a man who is alive, and only one of them has a ration card?

SFX: Footsteps stopping.

CHAITRA: (very carefully) You've seen the family tree.

INCHARA: I've seen it since two thousand nineteen. I have a photocopy, it's three years old, and I have done exactly nothing with it, because I took it to my editor and he asked me one question. He asked, whose daughter is she. And when I said I don't know, he said find out, because a story about a man is one column and a story about a family is page one, and I have been waiting three years for somebody in that house to walk into a government building and be angry.

CHAITRA: I'm not your page one.

INCHARA: Everybody says that. (a card being pushed into a hand) The number works. I answer at night also.

SFX: A door with a hydraulic closer that does not close. A room of steel almirahs. Files stacked in three columns to shoulder height. A table fan pointed at one man and nobody else.

THAMMANNA: Ohh. Chaitramma. Sit, sit. Ramesha! One coffee.

CHAITRA: No coffee.

THAMMANNA: One coffee, Ramesha. (to her, warmly) In this office if a person sits without coffee people ask what is the matter, isn't it. Nobody asks anything about a person drinking coffee.

SFX: A plastic chair. A form being set on the table.

CHAITRA: Form fifteen. Correction of name of father in the birth register. Affidavit attached, notarised, two hundred rupees, this morning.

SFX: Paper being drawn across a table, unhurried. A page being turned.

THAMMANNA: Correcting from what to what, amma?

CHAITRA: You know what.

THAMMANNA: The form has columns. Existing entry. Corrected entry. If the columns are empty the file cannot move. I don't make the columns.

SFX: Nothing. Long. The table fan turning away, coming back.

CHAITRA: Existing entry — Lokanath. Corrected entry — Mallesha.

SFX: A pen. Then the pen stopping.

THAMMANNA: Say the second one again.

CHAITRA: Mallesha. My father's name is Mallesha. He has a seventh standard pass and a training certificate and a second prize from the district workshop in two thousand and nine, and his name is Mallesha, and I have said it out loud in a government office, which as far as I know has not happened since nineteen ninety-four.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

THAMMANNA: (and he is delighted; that is the horrible thing; he sounds genuinely pleased) Twenty-four years. Twenty-four years and nobody in that house said it in front of me. Not once, amma. Not at your grandmother's ceremony, not when the boy was born, not even when your mother was very angry with me in two thousand and eleven, and she had reason. And the girl says it in six seconds. (a chair creaking back) You are going to be a very good teacher.

CHAITRA: Put the file through.

THAMMANNA: I will put the file through.

CHAITRA: When?

THAMMANNA: (leaning forward; the warmth does not go anywhere, which is what makes it work) Amma. Do you know what happens to that form? It goes from this table to the tahsildar with a covering letter, and the covering letter has to say why the existing entry is wrong. Not that it is wrong. Why. So on the seventeenth of next month a letter will leave this office in my typing which says: the entry is wrong because the person shown as father in nineteen ninety-nine was deceased in nineteen ninety-four, and the person actually residing is one Mallesha, who is presently serving in the department under the deceased's name and drawing pay against post number two hundred and six.

SFX: A file being squared against the edge of the table. Twice.

THAMMANNA: That is one letter. It takes forty minutes to type. After it is typed, your father does not have twenty-four years of service. He has twenty-four years of evidence.

CHAITRA: (steady) Then type it.

THAMMANNA: (a small laugh) See, that is the part I like. Sit.

CHAITRA: I'm standing.

THAMMANNA: The audit circular I mentioned at your gate — it has come. Registers of all schools in this block, physical verification, nineteen ninety-five onwards. The team sits from the fifth of next month. Between now and the fifth, one register can be kept in this almirah for departmental scrutiny instead of going to the team, and nobody asks, because a register in the establishment section is not a missing register, it is a register under scrutiny. There is even a column for it.

CHAITRA: What do you want.

THAMMANNA: (mildly, as if she has asked him the time) Four acres. The land by the canal, survey number ending in eleven. Mutation direct to my daughter's husband's name, he is at Hosakere, sugar factory, decent boy. And one resignation letter, voluntary, medical grounds, dated the third — signed the way your father has signed for twenty-four years. Not the new name. The old one. A resignation in the new name is a confession, amma, and a confession is no use to anybody.

SFX: Nothing. Long. The photocopier in the corridor.

CHAITRA: And if I don't.

THAMMANNA: Then on the fifth the register goes to the team, and I am the man who handed it over, and I get an entry in my confidential report for alertness. (a shrug in the voice) I have been in this chair thirty-one years. I have never once been transferred. Do you know why? Because I have never been on the wrong side of a file. Not once.

SFX: A drawer opening. The heavy sound of a very large ledger being lifted onto a table and opened flat.

THAMMANNA: Now. Your form has to be entered in the inward register. Everything that comes into this office is entered, that is the beauty of this office, nothing disappears, everything is entered.

SFX: A pen uncapped. Writing — slow, deliberate, a clerk's hand.

THAMMANNA: Date. Serial. Subject — correction of birth entry. Received from —

SFX: The pen, writing.

THAMMANNA: — daughter of Lokanath, assistant teacher, Nerlige. The fifth, amma. Four acres and the resignation, by the fifth.
