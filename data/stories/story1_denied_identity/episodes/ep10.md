# Episode 10 — "Forty-One Sixty"

SFX: A courtyard before six. A steel vessel of water going over a threshold. A broom. Then a moped in the lane — four-stroke, slowing, and this time it does not stop.

PRAKASH: (at the gate) He went past.

SAVITHRI: Come in.

PRAKASH: Amma, he went past the gate. It's the seventh.

SAVITHRI: Come inside, Prakash.

PRAKASH: Twelve hundred is in the rice tin. I put it there myself, I've been putting it there since I was nine years old and I never once asked what it was for, and today he doesn't stop?

SAVITHRI: (flat) Then it is not the seventh any more. It is something else.

NARRATOR: For twenty-four years the arrangement had a shape, and the shape was twelve hundred rupees on the seventh. The morning the moped went past the gate, the mother of that house understood what the daughter had not yet understood: that they were no longer being milked. They were being closed.

SFX: A kitchen. A tin lid off, and on again.

CHAITRA: He didn't take it because he doesn't need it any more. The fifth has gone. The audit team sat on the fifth and he handed nothing over — Amma, he handed nothing over, I checked with Basavaraj, the district file is still in his almirah under pre-audit scrutiny. He didn't give it to the team.

SAVITHRI: Then why did he take it for twenty-four years?

CHAITRA: Because for twenty-four years he was selling us silence. He's not selling silence any more.

SAVITHRI: What is he selling?

CHAITRA: (slowly) Timing. He's the only one who decides when.

SFX: A school bell, hand-swung, three strikes, from two hundred metres away.

SFX: A school gate. Four hundred children in lines. A microphone popping on.

SHANKARAPPA: (amplified) Silence. Prayer. Then national anthem, then I have one announcement about the water tank—

SFX: A jeep. Not stopping outside — coming in through the gate, onto the yard, which no vehicle does at assembly time.

GIRIJA: (off) Whose jeep is that?

SFX: Doors. Three men's shoes on gravel. Leather, not chappals.

VOICE (process server): Government Higher Primary School, Nerlige. Service of notice. Is there one Savithri, wife of the assistant teacher of this school?

SFX: Four hundred children. And the specific silence of a school yard where the children have stopped moving and the teachers have not yet decided to make them.

SHANKARAPPA: (amplified, then off-mic) Sir, this is a school. There is an office. Come to the office—

PROCESS SERVER: Notice is to be served at a place where the respondent or her family can be located, sir. The lady is not at the house. The husband is here.

SHANKARAPPA: (off-mic, furious) Then serve it in the office!

PROCESS SERVER: (reading, and he reads at volume, because that is the job) In the court of the civil judge, junior division. Claim petition. Petitioner — Ratnamma, widow, aged forty-seven, resident of Hosakere. Respondents — one, the department, through the block education officer. Two, Savithri, wife of the person impersonating.

SFX: A murmur. Four hundred children do not understand the word and every adult in that yard does.

PROCESS SERVER: Claim — arrears of pay and allowances drawn against post number two hundred and six of Government Higher Primary School, Nerlige, from November nineteen ninety-four to date, together with family pension and terminal benefits due to the petitioner as the lawfully wedded widow of the appointee.

SFX: A microphone whining, unattended.

PROCESS SERVER: Value of the claim — forty-one lakh, sixty thousand rupees.

SFX: Nothing. Long. A crow on the tin roof, walking.

CHAITRA: (from the gate, arriving into the middle of it) Ratnamma.

SFX: A woman's slippers on gravel, unhurried.

RATNAMMA: I am here. I came in the jeep. I paid for the jeep.

CHAITRA: She slept in my room for nine nights. She ate from my mother's hand. Nine nights, Ratnamma!

RATNAMMA: Nine nights. Against twenty-eight years. Do that sum, you're the taluk topper.

CHAITRA: Why my mother? She was nineteen when they married her into that house, she didn't sign anything, she has never in her life signed anything except a school admission form—

RATNAMMA: Because the department is respondent one and it will take four years, and a lawyer told me that a claim against a department alone goes into a cupboard and a claim with a family in it goes into a courtroom. He told me that in nineteen ninety-eight and I did not believe him. I have now had twenty-four more years to believe him.

SFX: Chappals on gravel. Six steps. Stopping.

MALLESHA: Ratnamma.

RATNAMMA: Master.

MALLESHA: The children are standing in the sun. Whatever is to be said can be said at the gate.

RATNAMMA: (and she turns on him, and twenty-eight years come out at once) Don't you talk to me about children standing in the sun! I stood at your gate! I stood at your gate in Margashira and I stood at your gate in the rain and your wife gave me water four times in twenty-eight years — four times, I have counted, I have counted everything, that is all I have been doing, counting — and every first of the month a salary went into a bank account in my husband's name and every first of the month I went to the ration shop with a widow's card that they would not give me because on paper he never joined and on paper I was never married! Eleven days I had a husband. Eleven days! And then twenty-eight years of a man walking around this taluk with his name on him like a coat!

SFX: Nothing. Long. Four hundred children.

MALLESHA: (level, and it is worse for being level) Yes.

RATNAMMA: Yes? That is it? Yes?

MALLESHA: Yes. That is all.

RATNAMMA: (to the yard, to everyone, ruined) He answers to it! Twenty-eight years and he still answers to it! Say your own name once, in this yard, in front of these children, and I will tear the petition here on this ground!

SFX: Nothing. Long. A microphone whining, unattended.

SFX: Chappals. Turning. Walking away, towards the classrooms, at exactly the same unhurried pace as always.

MALLESHA: (from a distance, to no one, and it carries) Fourth standard. Arithmetic. Nine forty.

SFX: Four hundred children, released, moving, the roar of it.

CHAITRA: (quiet, wrecked) Ratnamma. Look at me. Look at me. You are right. Every single thing you said is right and I am not going to argue one line of it. But you have put my mother's name in a court paper and my mother has done nothing.

RATNAMMA: Then let her come and say so.

CHAITRA: She will not say anything, that is the whole tragedy of her, she has never said anything in her life. So I am saying it. Three questions. One — do you actually want forty-one lakh?

RATNAMMA: (a pause) No.

CHAITRA: Two — what do you want?

RATNAMMA: (and her voice goes somewhere very small) I want it written. Anywhere. One government paper in this state with my husband's name and my name on the same line. That's all. I am forty-seven. I don't want the money, I want a paper.

CHAITRA: Three — how long will you give me?

RATNAMMA: (long pause) Until the next hearing.

CHAITRA: When?

RATNAMMA: The twenty-second. Twelve days. (and the fight goes out of her all at once) Twelve days, child. I have waited twenty-eight years to give somebody twelve days.

SFX: A jeep door. An old woman getting into it slowly, the way a woman gets into a vehicle she has paid for herself.

SFX: Shoes on gravel. A file opened flat against a forearm. A docket.

PROCESS SERVER: Madam. Acknowledgement of service. Signature or thumb.

CHAITRA: My mother is at the house. There is nobody here to sign it.

PROCESS SERVER: The rules don't require her, madam. They require me to record where it was done.

SFX: A pen on paper, against the flat of a file. Unhurried. A ceiling of four hundred children being told, at last, to sit down.

PROCESS SERVER: (reading back as he writes) Claim value — forty-one lakh, sixty thousand rupees. Respondent number two — Savithri, wife of the person impersonating. Served at the gate of Government Higher Primary School, Nerlige, at assembly time, in the presence of the headmaster, the staff, and four hundred children.
