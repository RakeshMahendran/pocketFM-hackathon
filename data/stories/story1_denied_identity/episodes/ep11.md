# Episode 11 — "Seva Register, 1994"

SFX: A temple courtyard at five in the morning. A brass bell, once. Water on stone. An old man's slippers, flat, dragging slightly.

CHAITRA: Twelve days. I have used nine of them. Nine days I have gone to the sub-registrar, to the tahsildar, to two lawyers, and every single one has asked me for the marriage certificate.

SEETHARAMA: There is no marriage certificate.

CHAITRA: Because it was never registered.

SEETHARAMA: Nothing was registered in this taluk in nineteen ninety-four, child. Registration came into people's heads around two thousand and six, when the bank started asking. Before that there was the mantap and there were the people who came and there was my book.

CHAITRA: Then I want your book.

SEETHARAMA: (a laugh with no teeth in it) Everybody wants my book. Nobody wants it enough to carry it. It is four kilos.

SFX: A wooden cupboard. A hasp. Something heavy, cloth-wrapped, lifted down and set on stone with a thud.

INCHARA: (arriving, whispering, absolutely unable to whisper) Is that it? Is that the seva register? Since when? Since when does it start?

SEETHARAMA: Nineteen seventy-nine.

INCHARA: Forty-three years in one book—

SEETHARAMA: In eleven books. This is the fourth.

NARRATOR: A priest's seva register is not a government record. No department has ever asked for one, no rule mentions it, and no court has been shown one in this taluk in living memory. It is a book in which an old man wrote down, in the same hand, on the same day, what was done and who paid for it. Remember that.

SFX: Thick paper. Old paper. Turning.

SEETHARAMA: July, ninety-four. Sixteenth. (reading, in the sing-song of a man reading his own handwriting) Vivaha. Groom — Lokanath, son of the late— that column I have written badly. Bride — Ratnamma, daughter of the Hosakere family, the one with the borewell. Mantap decorated by the bride's uncle. Purnahuti at eleven fifteen. Dakshine, one hundred and one rupees, paid by the groom's younger brother because the groom's hands were full.

SFX: Nothing. Long. A bell somewhere.

CHAITRA: Say the last part again.

SEETHARAMA: It is what is written.

INCHARA: (breathing) Sir. Sir. Does anybody else's book in this district record a marriage that the government does not have?

SEETHARAMA: Every second marriage of that decade, child. That is not a story. That is a Tuesday.

SFX: A camera phone. Photographs, four of them, the fake shutter click.

SFX: A district office corridor. Four ceiling fans, none in time. A queue.

REVANNA: (from behind a desk) You again.

CHAITRA: Not a toilet block this time, sir.

REVANNA: (a sigh) What is it.

CHAITRA: Sir, there is a claim petition before the civil judge for forty-one lakh sixty thousand rupees against the department and against my mother. Next date the twenty-second. If it is heard on the twenty-second, the department will file a statement saying the petitioner's marriage is unproved, because there is no certificate, because it was never registered, and the case will run four years, and everything in it will be read out in open court including my mother's name.

REVANNA: That is a correct summary of what will happen. Why are you telling me?

CHAITRA: Because there is a second route and it does not go through that court. Rule sixty-one. Where a departmental claimant's marriage is not registered, the head of the department may accept proof of marriage by any two of — the priest's record, the photographic evidence, or the sworn statement of two persons who attended.

SFX: Paper set on a desk. Then more paper.

CHAITRA: The priest's register of Nerlige temple, entry of the sixteenth of July, nineteen ninety-four, photographed with the volume number and the pages either side. The studio photograph of the sixteenth of July, nineteen ninety-four, only print, taluk town studio. And the sworn statements of the bride's uncle, aged seventy-nine, and of Girija, assistant teacher, Nerlige, who attended that marriage as a girl of twenty-two.

REVANNA: (a long pause) Girija attended?

CHAITRA: She attended, sir. She recognised the mantap in the photograph before I told her what I was showing her. She wrote her statement in the staff room in eleven minutes and she cried for nine of them.

SFX: Nothing. Long. A ceiling fan.

REVANNA: And what does the department get out of accepting this?

CHAITRA: A widow's file instead of a court case, sir. And a deputy director who fixed it in nine days.

REVANNA: (dry) You have found the only sentence in the language that works on me.

SFX: A pen. Fast, angry, official.

REVANNA: (writing, and reading out as he writes, because that is now the custom in this district) Proof of marriage accepted under rule sixty-one on the strength of the temple record and two attesting statements. The petitioner is recorded as the lawfully wedded widow of the appointee to post number two hundred and six. Family pension is sanctioned from the date of this order, and the question of arrears is referred to the government, which will take four years, and I am telling you that so you don't wait by the door.

SFX: A stamp. Wood.

SFX: A veranda. A stool. A yard being swept. A jeep in the lane, stopping.

RATNAMMA: (from the gate, and her voice is not the voice from the school yard) Savithramma.

SAVITHRI: (a pause) Ratnakka.

RATNAMMA: Give me water.

SAVITHRI: At the gate or inside?

RATNAMMA: (and it takes her a moment) Inside.

SFX: A gate. Two women's feet on a veranda. A steel tumbler.

RATNAMMA: (formally, and loudly, because there are neighbours at the wall and she intends them to hear) I have this morning instructed my advocate to withdraw the claim petition as against respondent number two. Savithri, wife of the schoolmaster of Nerlige, has no part in this matter and never had. That is being said in front of the lane. (a pause) Say it back, so nobody has to ask you later.

SAVITHRI: (very quietly) She has withdrawn it against me.

RATNAMMA: Louder, amma. You have been quiet for twenty-three years, it is not doing you any good.

SAVITHRI: (and it comes out, and something in it cracks) She has withdrawn it against me!

SFX: A page of official paper, unfolded, held.

RATNAMMA: And this came by hand at four o'clock. Sanction of family pension. Order of the deputy director of public instruction. (reading, slowly, badly, the way a woman reads a thing she has read forty times in three hours) Ratnamma, wife of Lokanath. Same line. Same line, Savithramma. Twenty-eight years, and the two names are on the same line, and it is a government paper with a number on it, and the number is nine four seven stroke twenty-two.

SFX: Two women. Neither of them saying anything for a while.

RATNAMMA: (finally) Where is she?

SAVITHRI: At the temple. Since morning. She has not eaten.

SFX: A temple courtyard. Evening. Bells. Crows going home. Four kilos of book open on stone.

SEETHARAMA: Child, it is dark. The lamp is not for reading.

CHAITRA: One more month, sir. August.

SEETHARAMA: What is in August?

CHAITRA: The fourteenth of August, nineteen ninety-four, and then eleven days after it. There has to be an entry. There is always an entry, you wrote the marriage down for one hundred and one rupees, so somebody paid you for the rites.

SEETHARAMA: (paper turning, slowly) Somebody paid me for everything. That is the book.

SFX: Paper. Then paper stopping.

SEETHARAMA: Here. Vaikunta samaradhane. Twenty-fifth of August, nineteen ninety-four. Four lines below the marriage, because between the two there was only a naming ceremony and a satyanarayana pooja. Small month.

CHAITRA: Read the whole entry, sir.

SEETHARAMA: Rites performed for the late Lokanath of Nerlige. Feeding of eighty. Brahmin dakshine, five hundred and one. Mantap, tent and vessels, eleven hundred. Total paid on the day, sixteen hundred and one rupees. And then the payer's column, which I fill only when the payer is not the family, because when it is the family there is no need to write it.

SFX: Nothing. Long. A crow.

CHAITRA: The younger brother was in Bhiwandi. He got off the bus on the seventeenth. There was no money in that house, sir, they had mortgaged it for thirty-eight thousand rupees in June.

SEETHARAMA: (mildly) I did not say there was money in the house. I said the payer's column is filled.

CHAITRA: Read the payer's column.

SFX: An old man leaning close to a page in bad light. A finger on paper.

SEETHARAMA: Paid in full on the day, in cash, by Thammanna. First division assistant. Block education office.
