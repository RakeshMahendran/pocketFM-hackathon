# Episode 5 — "Eleven Days"

SFX: A school yard emptying. Four hundred pairs of feet going the other way. A cycle being wheeled, not ridden, over gravel — the slow tick of a free wheel.

GIRIJA: (hurrying, half out of breath) Chaitra. Chaitra, one minute. Listen to me. Whatever it is — and I am not asking, I am not that type — whatever it is, don't do it in the yard. Do it in the house. In the yard there are four hundred witnesses and every one of them has a mother.

CHAITRA: You've taught in the next room to him for nineteen years.

GIRIJA: Nineteen years, yes.

CHAITRA: What is his name?

GIRIJA: (a laugh that goes nowhere) What kind of question—

CHAITRA: What is his name, teacher?

GIRIJA: (and the gossip drains out of her voice, and what is left is careful) Master. We all call him Master.

SFX: The cycle chain. Gravel giving way to tar.

NARRATOR: Nineteen years in the next room. She had eaten from his lunch box on days she forgot hers, she had held his umbrella at two funerals, and when the question was put to her plainly she found she did not have an answer. Remember that.

SFX: A lane. Tamarind pods cracking underfoot. A radio somewhere playing film songs at the wrong speed.

PRAKASH: (from ahead, running back) Akka. Akka, don't go in.

CHAITRA: Move.

PRAKASH: There's a woman sitting on our step. She's been there since morning. Amma won't come out of the kitchen. She's just — she's standing at the stove with nothing on it.

SFX: The gate. Two footsteps. Stopping.

RATNAMMA: You are the girl.

CHAITRA: I'm Chaitra.

RATNAMMA: Twenty-two years and four months. Born in Margashira. Your mother did the naming ceremony on the eleventh day, in this yard, with sixty people, and nobody from your father's side stood on this side of the yard. I know. I counted from the wall.

SFX: Nothing. Long. A cow bell in the lane.

CHAITRA: Who are you?

RATNAMMA: Twenty-eight years I have been asking somebody in this district that question. You can wait five minutes.

SFX: A steel tumbler set down on the step, untouched, going cold.

CHAITRA: Amma! Come out here.

SAVITHRI: (from inside, and her voice is not steady, which is new) Give her water and send her.

RATNAMMA: She has given me water four times in twenty-eight years. Four times, always at the gate, never inside. She is a good woman. I am saying that seriously. A good woman who has been carrying somebody else's stone.

CHAITRA: You know my mother.

RATNAMMA: I know your mother's husband better.

SFX: A crow. The radio stopping mid-song, somewhere.

CHAITRA: Say what you came to say.

RATNAMMA: Eleven days.

CHAITRA: What?

RATNAMMA: I was married eleven days. Sixteenth of July to the twenty-seventh, nineteen ninety-four. On the twenty-seventh he went to the taluk office because the joining report had to be given within thirty days of the order. He took the bus at six. There was a lorry at the Hosakere junction carrying stone. He died on the fourteenth of August in the district hospital, in the third bed of the men's ward, and it took eighteen days because a young man's body does not agree quickly.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

CHAITRA: The fourteenth of August. Nineteen ninety-four.

RATNAMMA: You have heard the date.

CHAITRA: A clerk read it to ninety people.

RATNAMMA: (and there is something almost like relief in it) Good. Twenty-eight years, and finally somebody in this district has said that date out loud in a government building.

CHAITRA: Who was he?

RATNAMMA: (flat) A teacher. For eleven days he was a man with an appointment order and a wife and he walked around this taluk telling people he would get a tiled roof put on his mother's house before Deepavali. He did not get one day of the salary. Not one day. And then the salary started coming anyway, every month, on the first, for twenty-four years, and it is still coming, and it does not come to me.

SFX: A stool being dragged out onto the veranda. Sat on, heavily.

SAVITHRI: (from the doorway, quiet) Ratnakka. Come inside.

RATNAMMA: (not moving) Twenty-eight years.

SAVITHRI: Come inside. Please.

RATNAMMA: In front of the girl, or nowhere.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

SAVITHRI: In front of the girl, then.

CHAITRA: Amma. Who is she?

SAVITHRI: Eat first.

CHAITRA: Amma!

SAVITHRI: (and it comes out of her at last, worn to nothing) I have been saying eat first to you since you were four years old and it has never once worked. Sit down, Chaitra.

SFX: A gate. Chappals — a man's, the same six-step rhythm from the veranda that morning. Stopping dead in the lane.

MALLESHA: (from outside the gate. Not entering.) Ratnamma.

RATNAMMA: Master.

MALLESHA: The house has visitors. The buffalo is close. There is work in the shed. That is all.

RATNAMMA: Twenty-eight years and he still cannot walk through his own gate when I am sitting in it. (louder, to the lane, so the lane can hear) Master! You want me to go? Say my name and I will go. Not Ratnamma. Say who I am to you. Say it once, in the lane, and I will get up and I will never come to this gate again.

SFX: Nothing. Long. A moped passing, slowing to look, going on.

MALLESHA: (level) The fourth standard has arithmetic tomorrow at nine forty.

SFX: Footsteps. Going around the side of the house. A shed door.

RATNAMMA: (quietly, to Chaitra) He was in Bombay when it happened. Did you know that? Loading polyester at a godown in Bhiwandi, four hundred rupees a week. They sent a telegram and it reached him on the sixteenth. By the time he got off the bus the rites were done, the pot was broken, everything finished, all of it paid for. He never saw his brother's face.

SFX: Something falls inside the house. A vessel. Nobody picks it up.

CHAITRA: (very slowly) His brother.

RATNAMMA: (and now she is not fighting; she is old and she is tired and she has waited twenty-eight years to be asked) Yes, child. His brother.

CHAITRA: Whose brother.

RATNAMMA: My husband's brother. And before you ask me the next thing, and you are going to ask it, I have carried the answer in this bag since morning and I have carried it in every bag I have owned since nineteen ninety-four.

SFX: A cloth bag. A tin, opened — the tight double click of an old Nutrine tin. Newspaper unwrapped, layer after layer.

RATNAMMA: Studio at the taluk town. Two rolls. This is the only print. The sixteenth of July, nineteen ninety-four.

SFX: Nothing. Long.

RATNAMMA: There is the mantap. There is my husband. There is me, and I was nineteen and I am not looking at the camera because my mother-in-law had just told me not to. And standing behind the groom — the tall one, the one with his sleeves folded, the one holding the garland ready in both hands so that it does not touch the ground —

SFX: Paper, held very still.

RATNAMMA: — that is your father.
