François Picaud: The Shoemaker Who Came Back with a Fortune

REVENGE · 1807–1828 · Paris, southern France, and Napoleonic-era prisons
44
out of 50
~110 episodes

A bridegroom is secretly denounced by jealous friends, vanishes into prison for seven years, returns under another name with a hidden fortune, and begins dismantling the lives of everyone who betrayed him.

What was actually done

A false accusation destroys Picaud’s wedding and liberty; after imprisonment and a chance inheritance, he returns disguised as Joseph Lucher and investigates the conspiracy before targeting its participants.

Engine — why it keeps generating

Every revenge act reveals another conspirator, another disguise, and another moral cost as the avenger must choose between exposing the original lie and becoming a new criminal power.

The fear a listener recognises

The first emotional beat is universal: your friends destroy your life as a joke, then the man they buried returns rich enough to reach every one of them.

Why it was passed over

Its best-known version may be literary reconstruction rather than secure history, making clearance and marketing language unusually important.

Clearance reasoning

  • The surviving account is partly mediated through later police memoir material and has disputed historicity.
  • Change names, locations, and identifying details while presenting it as a dramatized historical case.
  • Do not market it as a fully authenticated biography.

Novelty

  • Alexandre Dumas used the account as one source for The Count of Monte Cristo; the novel and its many adaptations are not direct dramatizations of the specific Picaud case.
