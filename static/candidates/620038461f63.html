Broderick’s Forged Will

FAMILY BETRAYAL · 1863–1874 · San Francisco and the United States Supreme Court
41
out of 50
~75 episodes

After a powerful man dies, an executor manufactures a will, recruits witnesses, and persuades a probate court to bless a document that turns the dead man’s fortune into a private machine.

What was actually done

A forged will was supported with false witness testimony and handwriting claims, then admitted to probate while interested relatives received no meaningful notice.

Engine — why it keeps generating

Every procedural step makes the false will harder to challenge: witnesses must stay aligned, executors must spend the estate, and beneficiaries must prove fraud after the court has spoken.

The fear a listener recognises

The listener instantly understands the betrayal of opening a dead relative’s will and finding that someone else has rewritten your bloodline.

Why it was passed over

The surviving record gives a powerful legal skeleton but fewer intimate scenes than the strongest candidates.
